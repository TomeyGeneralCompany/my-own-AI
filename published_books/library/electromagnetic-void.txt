                                            Introduction

       Y2K did happen, but it didnt happen in the way that everyone had assumed it would

happen. From my understanding of the history, as all this was before my time, people imagined

things like planes falling out of the sky, nukes going off, ect. This, obviously, did not happen. No,

Y2K didn't change computers, it changed people. Starting really from the year 2000, although

computers and things did exist prior to this, it is generally accepted that 2000 was when our modern

civilisation had finally succeeded in encompassing the human organism within an electrical

environment of a density, continuity, and intricacy without historical precedent; the router upon

the desk, the cellular telephone perpetually negotiating with towers and proximate devices, the

computer exchanging packets with distant servers, and the room filled with mutually

communicating machines are ordinarily apprehended as constituents of an informational

environment, although information itself never moves in vacuo. Every bit transmitted is

instantiated through a physical alteration, charge displaced, potential varied, current switched, field

reorganised, energy transported, and the Internet must therefore be understood, ab initio, not as an

immaterial architecture fortuitously supported by electricity, but as a vast electrical order whose

energetic transformations have been disciplined into the service of information.

       The present paper establishes a theoretical model according to which sufficiently complex

and rapid electrical communication produces recurrent localised energetic deficits, here

denominated electromagnetic voids, as charge and electromagnetic energy are displaced,

concentrated, and reorganised in the execution of computation and transmission; the void,

however, is no entity lurking behind the circuit, no res additional to the field, but an absence,

precisely as darkness names the privation of light rather than a substance opposed to it. Its apparent

action consists wholly in the physical processes by which the resulting disequilibrium is answered,
so that to say that the void draws, seeks, employs, or takes energy is merely to compress into

ordinary English the mechanics of equilibration, without attributing mens, voluntas, or agency to

a privation.

       The decisive proposition, the cardo upon which the entire argument turns, is that the energy

answering such a localised deficit is not confined by fiat to the circuitry in which that deficit first

arose, for an electrical apparatus exists within an electromagnetic environment and that

environment contains conductors, dielectrics, grounds, structures, and living electrochemical

systems alike. Through the fields generated by the device the disequilibrium is coupled to those

surroundings; and where the human organism stands among the most effectively proximate

electrochemical reservoirs, the local gradient draws upon that organism according to the same rule

by which every other available reservoir participates in the compensatory flux.

       Proximity matters in this model because coupling is constrained by the actual

electromagnetic environment rather than by the abstract logical boundaries of a network; hence, a

server thousands of miles away is informationally adjacent to a laptop, while a human body

approximately three feet from that laptop is physically adjacent to its fields. The theory therefore

distinguishes informational topology from energetic topology; nevertheless, packets traverse the

former according to routing protocols, but equilibration occurs through the latter according to the

available physical paths of coupling. This is why the nearest conductive biological system becomes

theoretically material even though it has no intended role in the engineered circuit.

       The nervous system is especially consequential because cognition depends upon the

maintenance of extraordinarily delicate electrochemical differences; neuronal membrane

potentials arise from ion-concentration gradients, selective membrane permeability, ion channels,

and active transport, whilst ATP-dependent processes sustain those gradients against continual
dissipation (Purves et al., 2001; Howarth, Gleeson, & Attwell, 2012); nevertheless, neuronal

electrical signaling depends upon ion concentration gradients, selective membrane permeability,

and active transport that maintains those gradients (Purves et al., 2001). Neural activity is

constituted through ion gradients, membrane potentials, action potentials, synaptic transmission,

oscillatory coordination, and the recurrent reconstruction of organised states; thereby, the

mechanism is not merely that electromagnetic emissions interfere with the nervous system, but

that the equilibration generated by complex electrical activity imposes a parasitic energetic drain

upon it. The drain need not remove a memory as though memory were a detachable quantity of

energy; moreover, it deprives the biological architecture of part of the energetic coherence by

which memories, attention, and continuity of cognition are maintained and reconstructed.

       Memory gaps, confusion, attentional discontinuity, and sensations of diminished agency

accordingly become consequences of a common mechanism: the technological system creates a

deficit; electromagnetic flux couples that deficit to its environment; proximate biological systems

contribute to equilibration; and the resulting drain degrades, however briefly or minutely, the

coherence of neural organisation.

       At the scale of a single primitive electrical connection the effect is negligible; moreover, at

the scale of billions of high-frequency, continuously switching, mutually communicating devices,

the situation changes. The production of localised voids becomes effectively continuous;

consequently, the network ceases to be a collection of isolated machines and becomes a persistent

cybernetic electromagnetic architecture, continuously generating disequilibrium and continuously

drawing upon its environment to answer it. Human beings, surrounded by and increasingly

interpenetrated with that architecture, are not external observers; indeed, they are energetic

components of its environment.
                               Notation and Formal Conventions

       The formal notation is cumulative throughout the dissertation. V(x,t) denotes the local

energetic deficit relative to the dynamically appropriate reference state u*(x,t); B_V denotes

the spatially and temporally integrated void burden; V_net denotes the composite network-

level deficit; S denotes the Poynting vector and P_EM the electromagnetic power crossing a

specified boundary; κ_i denotes the effective coupling coefficient of reservoir i and R* the

reservoir for which that coefficient is maximal; E_b denotes locally available biological

electrochemical energy; P_b→v denotes the biological work term directed into compensatory

equilibration; C denotes cognitive coherence as the maintained order of organised neural

state; β_ij denotes the interaction term between networked devices; Γ_h denotes higher-order

coupling between the human reservoir and the composite network field; and τ_e, τ_v, and

τ_b denote, respectively, electrical relaxation, void relaxation, and biological recovery times.

Symbols retain these meanings unless an equation expressly defines a local variant.


                       I. The Physical Origin of the Electromagnetic Void

       Four principles govern the argument, each with its own province and none entitled to

trespass upon the office of another: Maxwell and Poynting determine the electromagnetic field and

its energetic flux; Faraday determines induction within the proximate conductor; Le Chatelier

supplies the ratio of compensatory equilibration; and the effective-proximity axiom determines

which among the available reservoirs bears the greater share of that compensation. The principle

of feedback then closes the sequence cybernetically by returning the altered output of the coupled
system as the condition of its succeeding state; this division of labour is essential, for the

demonstrative strength of the model lies not in invoking eminent names ad verecundiam, but in

assigning to each principle the exact inferential office required by the causal chain.

        Every electrical act of computation depends upon difference; consequently, a transistor

must occupy distinguishable states; a signal requires a difference of potential; current requires

gradients; transmission requires the controlled movement and reorganization of charge and field

energy. Digital communication consequently manufactures disequilibrium intentionally and at

extraordinary speed; indeed, the greater the switching density and informational throughput, the

more rapidly local energetic configurations are created, displaced, destroyed, and recreated.

        The argument becomes more consequential when one remembers that digital systems do

not merely preserve a fixed electrical pattern; indeed, their usefulness consists precisely in

changing patterns. A static arrangement can store a state, but computation requires transitions

among states, and communication requires those transitions to be reproduced, detected, amplified,

corrected, and routed; hence, the electromagnetic void is therefore associated not merely with the

existence of electrical equipment but with the rate, density, and organisation of state transition. A

powered but nearly quiescent apparatus and an apparatus undergoing dense, irregular switching

need not impose the same local energetic burden even when their average power draw is

superficially similar.

        Le Chatelier's principle supplies the general ratio of compensatory response: disturb an

equilibrium and the system alters its disposition in opposition to the imposed disturbance; within

its classical province this proposition describes chemical equilibrium, whereas within the present

model it supplies the logic of restitution by which a local energetic deficit calls forth redistribution.

It does not select, ex se, the reservoir that shall bear that redistribution, such selection belongs to
the effective-proximity axiom developed below, but it establishes the prior necessity of

compensation: once rapid electrical activity produces the localised potential or energy-density

deficit denominated by V, the coupled environment answers the gradient, and the void, being a

privation rather than a substance, is extinguished through the movement of available energy. The

apparatus stands, in this respect, rather nearer to the fate of Tundale than to the imagined perpetual-

motion engine, for its want is neither metaphysical nor infinite, but continually renewed: each

restoration approaches its terminus ad quem, each succeeding transition disturbs the state anew,

and the network, multiplying those transitions through recursive communication, withdraws the

waters again at the very instant at which equilibrium seemed attainable.

       Let the local energetic state of a region be represented by u(x,t), and let a communication

event produce a negative deviation Δu below its immediately preceding local state; nevertheless,

the void is then represented merely as the positive magnitude of that deficit:


                                     V(x,t) = max[0, -Δu(x,t)].
       This notation does not assign substance to the absence; it quantifies the amount by which

the local energetic condition has been deprived relative to its reference state; thereby, the physical

question becomes where the compensating flux originates and through what channels it reaches

the disturbed region.

       A more complete expression treats the reference state itself as dynamic; thereby, let u*(x,t)

denote the local energetic state expected in the absence of the immediately imposed switching

event. The instantaneous deficit relative to that moving baseline is therefore:


                                  V(x,t) = max[0, u*(x,t) - u(x,t)].
       The total void burden over a region Ω during an interval T is represented as:


                                   B_V = ∫_T ∫_Ω V(x,t) dΩ dt.
       This quantity captures a material feature of the theory: a succession of individually minute

deficits becomes significant through persistence; consequently, the relevant variable is not only

the depth of any one void but the integrated burden created by repeated formation before complete

relaxation.

       In a simple circuit, the answer is overwhelmingly internal to the designed electrical path;

moreover, in a dense, rapidly changing electrical architecture, however, the apparatus is

electromagnetically open to its surroundings. The local system is embedded in a larger field

environment containing conductors, dielectrics, grounds, neighbouring devices, structural

materials, atmospheric media, and biological organisms; consequently, the void therefore belongs

not merely to a circuit diagram but to the entire coupled energetic environment in which the circuit

operates.


Field Energy, Circuit Exteriority, and the Physical Situs of Power

       The first evidentiary enlargement required by the argument concerns a proposition so

elementary that ordinary circuit notation often conceals it: electrical power is not adequately

represented, in its physical passage from source to load, by imagining energy as a substance borne

inside the metallic conductor by the drifting charge carriers themselves. Maxwellian

electrodynamics places the energetic account in the fields, and Poynting's theorem supplies the

local conservation law by which that account is rendered quantitative. Feynman's treatment of field

energy and momentum identifies the Poynting vector as the rate at which electromagnetic field

energy moves through space, whilst the MIT treatment of electromagnetic fields and energy

explicitly develops power flow in circuits through integral and differential conservation statements

(Feynman, Leighton, & Sands, 1964; Haus & Melcher, 1989). The point is of cardinal importance

to the present thesis, because the relevant energetic topology is therefore not exhausted by the
copper trace, semiconductor junction, or cable; the physical situs of transfer extends into the

surrounding field, and the circuit is, ab initio, an open electromagnetic geometry rather than a

merely metallic itinerary.

       This exteriority is not peculiar to radiating antennas or to the far field. Analyses of

elementary direct-current circuits have demonstrated that the Poynting vector describes energy

moving through the space surrounding conductors and entering dissipative elements at the load,

thereby giving a field-theoretic account of power transfer even where the schoolroom diagram

tempts the observer to imagine that energy simply travels inside the wire (Majcen, Haaland, &

Dudley, 2000; Galili & Goihbarg, 2005). This is no mere pedagogical distinction, for it establishes

the causa formalis by which a powered apparatus and its immediate environment constitute one

electromagnetic system. The wire constrains charges; surface charges establish the electric field;

current establishes the magnetic field; and their vector product determines the local flux of

electromagnetic energy. Thus the machine's energetic presence occupies space around the

machine, and any neighbouring body stands within that field according to its position, orientation,

conductivity, permittivity, and frequency-dependent response.

       The ordinary language of a device 'using electricity' consequently compresses a richer

nexus causarum. A source establishes potentials and currents; the associated fields store and

transport energy; the load receives energy through the boundary described by the Poynting flux;

and dissipation or storage changes the local energetic state. In the present argument, this field-

theoretic description is the indispensable antecedent to the void, because a local deficit cannot

possess a compensatory pathway unless the system already possesses a physically continuous

means by which energy traverses its boundaries. Poynting's theorem supplies precisely that means.

It neither requires nor implies an intelligent absence; rather, it renders intelligible how an energetic
privation at one locus can be answered through field-mediated redistribution from another locus,

since the local conservation equation already binds changes of field-energy density, flux through

the boundary, and work performed upon charges within one mathematical ratio.

       The same consideration disposes of any temptation to confine the analysis to the nominal

communications channel. Information is encoded through physical state changes, and those state

changes are realised by charge motion, voltage transitions, switching currents, oscillating fields,

and electromagnetic power. The informational logos is therefore instantiated through material

energeia; it is not a second, immaterial traffic superadded to the circuit. When a processor changes

state, when a transceiver modulates a carrier, when a memory bus changes logic level, or when a

switching regulator corrects its output, the information-bearing operation possesses an

electromagnetic embodiment whose energetic consequences are described by the same

conservation laws that govern any other electrical apparatus. The thesis accordingly treats

communication complexity not as a mystical property of 'the Internet', but as an increase in the

density, rapidity, and reciprocal organisation of physically realised state transitions.

       This is particularly important for the distinction between a quiescent conductor and an

active digital architecture. A length of wire at electrostatic equilibrium presents one field

configuration; a powered computational system presents a perpetually changing one, because its

useful operation consists precisely in transitions. The terminus a quo of each transition is an

antecedent electrical state and its terminus ad quem is a succeeding state, yet the transition itself

is not physically free: capacitances charge and discharge, inductive and radiative fields change,

currents rise and fall, and energy is transported into, through, and away from the active elements.

The more rapidly such transitions occur, and the more numerous the interacting elements, the more

densely the local electromagnetic environment is articulated in time. The void theory takes this
ordinary physical fact as its substrate and asks what follows when the intervals of local energetic

redistribution become sufficiently numerous, sufficiently rapid, and sufficiently recursively

coupled that relaxation is continually interrupted by the succeeding operation. One might say, with

no greater violence to the physics than Lucretius permitted himself in speaking of nature, that the

quiescent conductor sleeps whilst the computational apparatus labours; yet its labour is not that of

Samson at the mill, whose circuit is imposed by captivity, but of a system whose own successful

operation continually furnishes the next occasion for energetic displacement.

       The consequence is exact enough. The paper does not require a novel law in order to

establish that electromagnetic energy occupies and traverses the space around electrical systems;

Maxwell, Poynting, and the mature literature of circuit energy flow already establish that

proposition. The novel term V enters only after this substrate has been secured, as a quantitative

representation of the local energetic deficit produced relative to the dynamically specified

reference state. By separating the inherited physics from the novel synthesis, the argument gains

rather than loses force: field energy and power flow are res data; the definition of the void is the

dissertation's own principium; and the subsequent question is whether the measured field-energy

redistribution exhibits the temporal deficit, reservoir selection, and biological contribution

specified by the model.


Switching, Spectral Multiplicity, and the Electromagnetic Complexity of Digital Apparatus

       Digital apparatus is too readily spoken of as though the binary abstraction were itself the

physical operation; yet a logic transition is realised only because voltages and currents change

within finite rise and fall times; every such change possesses a spectrum, and every departure from

perfect confinement supplies a path by which conducted or radiated electromagnetic energy enters

the surrounding architecture. The engineering discipline of electromagnetic compatibility exists
precisely because switching circuits generate unwanted spectral content and because interconnects,

parasitic capacitances, inductances, ground structures, and enclosures determine how that content

is conducted or radiated. Studies of switching power electronics, for example, model

electromagnetic-interference emissions by reference to switching devices, passive components,

interconnect parasitics, and the temporal form of the switching event itself (Zhu, Lai, Tang, Hefner,

& Chen, 1999). The proposition required here is modest but indispensable: useful digital

transitions are accompanied by physically measurable electromagnetic transitions whose spatial

distribution is not identical with the schematic boundary of the computational operation.

       The physical origin of the vacuum must therefore be stated before its consequences are

pursued. From the earliest development of electronic computation to the semiconductor machines

which remain the commercial standard, digital connection has depended upon the deliberate

manipulation of physical particles, principally electrons, and upon the ordered movement,

concentration, separation, and direction of those particles into the states by which digital relations

are materially established. The technology by which this is accomplished has altered enormously,

yet the underlying operation has not thereby become immaterial: the machine must still bring

together and herd electrons in order to make, maintain, alter, and extinguish its digital connections.

Quantum computation, though proceeding by a different architecture and still far from displacing

conventional computation as the ordinary commercial standard, does not abolish this physical

necessity, for it likewise depends upon the deliberate manipulation of physical particles and states.

The distinction of architecture therefore does not remove the more primitive fact upon which the

present inquiry begins.

       When electrons are thus brought together and herded into the physical configurations

demanded by computation, their removal from one place in order that they may be gathered in
another leaves behind empty spaces of nothing: vacua produced by the very act of displacement.

An electron brought hither has departed from thither; what is concentrated at the terminus ad quem

has been withdrawn from a terminus a quo, and the act of gathering therefore contains within itself

the production of absence. This absence is what the present work calls the electromagnetic void.

It is not an additional substance concealed within the machine, but the nothing left where the

displaced particles had been before their ordered movement. Nature, however, abhors a vacuum,

natura abhorret a vacuo; the vacuum cannot remain unanswered, and the surrounding physical

order is consequently drawn into the work of filling what the computational act has emptied. The

causal history of the void therefore begins neither in metaphor nor in abstraction, but in the

ordinary material operation of computation itself: particles are gathered, particles are herded,

absence is thereby produced, and nature answers the absence.

       The material operation described here is not peripheral to computation but constitutive of

it. Modern semiconductor logic is organised precisely through the controlled redistribution of

charge: the MOS structure is driven through accumulation, depletion, and inversion, whilst the

MOSFET converts those ordered changes of carrier population into the conducting and

nonconducting states from which digital logic is built. The language of gathering and herding is

therefore not picturesque ornament laid upon an otherwise immaterial process; it names, in

ordinary speech, the deliberate establishment of unequal carrier distributions by fields, potentials,

junctions, gates, and channels. Digital order is purchased by material rearrangement. The bit may

be abstract; its existence in the machine is not. (Fonstad, 2009a, 2009b)

       Charge, moreover, is not created ex nihilo whenever the machine requires a new state.

Conservation binds the operation from the beginning. The continuity equation joins every local

alteration of charge density to a corresponding divergence of current, so that concentration in one
region belongs to the same causal history as departure from another. The physical act by which the

machine establishes a new electrical condition is therefore intrinsically redistributive: what is

assembled has been supplied, what is supplied has been moved, and what is moved leaves behind

the privation from which the present theory takes the name void. Haus and Melcher place this

relation at the foundation of conduction theory by joining charge conservation to the field laws

governing the motion of unpaired charge. (Haus & Melcher, 1989)

       That same physics also explains why the vacuum cannot remain a merely passive

remainder of the preceding state. A departure in charge distribution establishes an electric field;

the field acts upon available charge carriers; the carriers move; and their motion alters the

distribution which generated the field. The process is therefore self-corrective in the strict physical

sense before any cybernetic recursion is introduced at the network scale. In a uniform Ohmic

medium, charge conservation, Gauss's law, and the constitutive relation J = sigma E reduce the

evolution of unpaired charge to a relaxation law whose solution is exponential. The disturbed

distribution carries within its own field the cause of the redistribution by which it is answered.

(Haus & Melcher, 1989)

                                          ∂ρᵤ/∂t + ∇·Jᵤ = 0
                                              Jᵤ = σE
                                             ∇·D = ρᵤ
                                        ∂ρᵤ/∂t + (σ/ε)ρᵤ = 0
                                 ρᵤ(t) = ρᵤ(0)e^(−t/τₑ),    τₑ = ε/σ
       The relaxation time tau_e gives the restorative process a determinate temporal scale. In a

highly conducting medium the interval may be extraordinarily short; in less conducting or

heterogeneous media it may be longer, and at boundaries the charge displaced from a uniform

region reappears as surface or interfacial charge. Haus and Melcher accordingly show that charge

which disappears from the volume of a uniform region does not vanish from the causal account: it
is redistributed according to conductivity, permittivity, geometry, and the conditions imposed at

material interfaces. Thus the void is born through displacement and answered through

redistribution, whilst the path and duration of that answer are fixed by the material order through

which the charges must move. (Haus & Melcher, 1989)

       This point bears directly upon the architecture of the present theory. The void need not

endure as one great and stationary cavity in order to become physically consequential.

Computation manufactures its condition again and again. Every transition which establishes a new

carrier distribution renews the sequence: gathering, depletion, field formation, compensatory

motion, and relaxation. CMOS logic makes this recurrence especially plain, for switching delay,

charging and discharging, power dissipation, and the speed-power relation all belong to the

physical passage from one logic state to another. The computational machine is consequently not

a vessel which creates one vacuum and thereafter lives beside it; it is a machine for the repeated

production and restitution of local vacua, multiplied by the number of switching elements and

renewed according to the cadence of their operation. (Fonstad, 2009c)

       The scientific importance of complexity therefore enters at the level of recurrence rather

than first cause. The first vacuum requires no exotic mechanism: particles are ordered into a new

state, absence follows their departure, and the resulting disequilibrium summons redistribution.

Complexity changes the metron. A single transition may relax almost as soon as it is produced,

but a processor, memory system, radio, display controller, power regulator, and network interface

do not perform one transition and fall silent. They proceed by immense sequences of charge

rearrangement whose several relaxations overlap, succeed, and condition one another. The object

of measurement is therefore not merely the lifetime of an isolated void but the integrated burden

of vacua continually produced within the temporally ordered labour of the device.
       The older maxim natura abhorret a vacuo consequently receives here a determinate

electrical grammar. Nature's 'abhorrence' is not rhetoric superadded to the equations; it is the

observable refusal of a displaced electrical system to remain without consequence. Conservation

forbids charge from appearing without history; Gauss's law binds charge to field; conduction binds

field to motion; relaxation carries the disturbed distribution toward its succeeding state. What

natural philosophy named horror vacui, the mathematics of conduction describes as the ordered

answer of a material system to disequilibrium. The phrase is ancient; the mechanism is electrical.

       A modern computer, telephone, tablet, router, or network appliance is, in its physical

constitution, a concourse of oscillators, clocks, buses, regulators, radios, memory interfaces,

processors, display electronics, and peripheral subsystems, each possessing its own characteristic

frequencies and transient structures. Their aggregate field is not reducible to one sinusoid or one

nominal carrier, for the actual electromagnetic environment contains fundamentals, harmonics,

modulation products, switching edges, burst patterns, duty cycles, and transient responses. The

Greek notion of taxis is useful here in its strict sense of order: the apparatus is an ordered

multiplicity of electrical motions whose individual kineseis are coordinated toward the telos of

computation and communication. As throughput and interconnection rise, the density of those

coordinated transitions rises with them, so that the local energetic state becomes increasingly

articulated by a succession of perturbations rather than by a single stationary field.

       Paul gives the figure its proper form: diversities of operation proceed within one body; so

too the electrical operation preserves its causal identity whilst appearing successively as carrier,

harmonic, switching edge, transient, modulation product, and induced field. Multiplicity of

manifestation does not of itself imply multiplicity of cause.
       This spectral multiplicity bears directly upon the paper's definition of V, because the

reference state u*(x,t) cannot be chosen as though the device possessed only two conditions,

powered and unpowered. The dynamically appropriate baseline must instead follow the temporal

constitution of the apparatus itself, distinguishing charging, discharge, overshoot, ringing,

modulation, correction, and relaxation where those states materially alter the local energetic

condition. What the theory names the void is not the whole electromagnetic field and not every

fluctuation within it; it is the positive magnitude of the local energetic deficiency relative to that

moving reference state, integrated where necessary across the spatial and temporal domain in

which the deficit persists.

       Nor may mean power be permitted to conceal transient structure, for the distinction is

essential. Two devices can consume the same mean power whilst distributing that power through

radically different temporal patterns, just as two signals can possess equal average amplitude

whilst differing in spectral composition, crest factor, duty cycle, and phase organisation. The void

model consequently predicts that mean wattage alone will be an inadequate independent variable.

The relevant metron must include switching rate, transition density, spectral content,

synchronisation, and the degree to which successive events occur before the local field-energy

distribution has returned to its antecedent condition. A slowly varying resistive load and a densely

switching digital processor can therefore be equal in average power whilst unequal in V-burden,

because the latter repeatedly produces the very temporal discontinuities upon which the proposed

mechanism depends.

       With network communication the matter passes into a higher order, for transitions cease to

be merely local and become reciprocally conditioned. A packet received at one interface produces

computation; computation produces memory access; memory access produces further switching;
the result produces transmission; transmission alters a remote machine; and the remote response

returns as a new local input. The physical chain is therefore cybernetic before the term is ever

applied philosophically. Each informational event is embodied in an electrical event, and the

output of one electrical subsystem becomes the causal antecedent of the next. The network thereby

converts switching multiplicity into switching recursion, which is precisely why the principle of

feedback belongs at the end of the mechanism rather than at its beginning: the electromagnetic

events are already physical; cybernetics explains how their organisation causes them to reproduce

the conditions of their own recurrence. Here the old figure of the Ouroboros becomes almost

diagrammatic: the tail enters the mouth because the output returns as input, and the circuit of

causation, once closed, acquires the capacity to renew the conditions of its own continuance.

       This engineering substrate strengthens the distinction between an isolated electrical

connection and a complex internet connection without requiring an ontological privilege for the

latter. The same physical laws govern both, but the networked case multiplies the number of state

transitions, the number of coupled oscillatory subsystems, the number of feedback paths, and the

temporal persistence of activity. In actu, the difference is one of organised complexity and

recurrence. The void theory accordingly predicts a continuum rather than a magical threshold:

simple circuits generate minute and rapidly relaxing deficits; increasingly complex and

continuously active architectures increase the integrated burden B_V; and sufficiently dense

reciprocal activity produces a regime in which the composite V_net approaches persistence

because the succeeding perturbation arrives before the preceding energetic configuration has fully

relaxed.


                         II. Equilibration and Electromagnetic Transport
       Poynting's theorem supplies the natural language for describing how electromagnetic

energy crosses the apparent boundary between a machine and its surroundings; consequently, the

Poynting vector is:


                                            S = E × H.
       Its direction gives the direction of electromagnetic energy flux and its magnitude gives the

rate at which electromagnetic energy passes through unit area (Ling et al., 2016). The

accompanying conservation relation is written:


                                        ∂u/∂t + ∇·S = -J·E.

       A change in electromagnetic energy density is therefore inseparable from energy flux and

work performed upon charges.

       The consequence of this relation is that energy transfer in an electrical apparatus cannot be

reduced to a picture of electrons confined within copper traces; indeed, the electric and magnetic

fields surrounding conductors participate in the transport of energy. A rapidly switching circuit

continually reorganizes those fields; an antenna deliberately launches part of that energy outward;

and every time-dependent electrical configuration establishes an electromagnetic relation between

the apparatus and its surroundings.

       The electromagnetic emissions of a complex device constitute, consequently, the physical

reach of the void, those tentacles by which a privation born within the ordered herding of electrons

extends beyond its terminus a quo, passes the confines of copper and silicon, and enters into

relation with whatever energy-bearing systems lie within the compass of its field; whence the

disequilibrium, though local in generation, is not imprisoned within the locality of its birth, but

acquires through electromagnetic emission a wider sphere within which the restitution demanded

by nature may be accomplished.
       This also prevents the theory from treating 'the Internet' as though it occupied a single

physical location; nevertheless, the network's logical unity is realized through innumerable local

electromagnetic events. Each node creates its own field geometry, each transmission alters the

distribution of electromagnetic energy around that node, and each surrounding environment

supplies different possible coupling paths; thereby, the global phenomenon is consequently

assembled from local energetic events whose interactions are coordinated by the informational

architecture.

       If Ω encloses an active device, the net electromagnetic power crossing its boundary is

represented by the surface integral:


                                        P_EM = ∮_Ω S·dA.

       Under sufficiently complex and rapidly varying conditions, part of the compensating

energy associated with repeated local deficits is supplied through this open boundary. The device

therefore participates in a larger energetic circuit whose effective limits are established not by its

plastic enclosure but by the spatial extent of meaningful electromagnetic coupling.

       The human body enters the engineer's boundary problem as an electrically active material

system; the electrical and dielectric properties of biological tissues have been measured across

frequencies extending from hertz to gigahertz, and conductivity, permittivity, tissue geometry, and

frequency are established determinants of electromagnetic interaction with the body (Gabriel,

Gabriel, & Corthout, 1996; Gabriel, Lau, & Gabriel, 1996a, 1996b; Stricklett & Baker, 2000;

Sasaki et al., 2022). It contains conductive ionic fluids, polarised membranes, excitable tissues,

and extensive electrochemical networks. The brain and peripheral nervous system depend upon

controlled movements of charged ions and differences of electrical potential maintained across

cellular membranes; human cognition is therefore implemented in matter intrinsically susceptible
to electromagnetic interaction, and Faradaic induction supplies the causa efficiens by which the

external field acquires an internal bioelectrical consequence.

       Faraday's law describes the electromotive force generated by changing magnetic flux:


                                       ∮ E·dl = -dΦ_B/dt.

       A time-varying electromagnetic environment can consequently induce electrical effects in

proximate conductive systems and experimental dosimetry treats induced internal electric fields,

currents, tissue conductivity, geometry, and specific absorption as measurable quantities rather

than merely theoretical possibilities (Bracken, 1992; Chou et al., 1996; ICNIRP, 2020) (Ling et

al., 2016; Urone & Hinrichs, 2020). Capacitive coupling likewise permits changing electric fields

to redistribute charge without direct metallic contact; indeed, a human standing beside an active

device therefore does not occupy an energetically sealed domain. The organism becomes part of

the coupled electromagnetic environment.

       Here lies the cardo of the matter: the coupling is not merely incidental interference; indeed,

when the device repeatedly creates localised energetic deficits, the surrounding conductive

environment participates in the compensatory flux. If a human nervous system lies within that

environment, some portion of the energy required for equilibration is drawn from the organism's

existing electrochemical organisation; hence, the person becomes, in effect, an accidental

biological ground or reservoir.

       The directionality proposed here is essential; hence, ordinary descriptions of

electromagnetic exposure frequently emphasize energy delivered from a source into tissue. The

present model also asks about energy drawn from the tissue into the coupled system during

compensatory redistribution; nevertheless, the biological organism is therefore neither merely a

passive target nor a detector of radiation. It is an active electrochemical system possessing
gradients of its own, and those gradients constitute available free energy; thereby, the void model

concerns the circumstances under which the coupled technological system recruits a minute

portion of that available energy into its own return toward equilibrium; Faradaic induction is thus

the causa efficiens by which the external field acquires an internal bioelectrical consequence.


Thermodynamic Stability, Compensatory Response, and the Equilibrium Principle

       Le Chatelier must be assigned his proper office with some severity, lest the principle be

made to prove more than it can bear. Gromov and Toikka's formal treatment of the Le Chatelier-

Brown principle places it within the mathematical analysis of thermodynamic stability and

formulates the general intuition that perturbations of an equilibrium system evoke counteracting

responses under the relevant stability conditions (Gromov & Toikka, 2020). The dissertation

employs this principle as the ratio of compensation, not as the selector of the reservoir and not as

a substitute for Maxwellian conservation. Those offices belong elsewhere: Maxwell and Poynting

govern electromagnetic energy and flux; the effective-proximity axiom distributes participation

among reservoirs; Le Chatelier supplies the stabilising logic by which a disturbed state is answered

rather than simply left as an arbitrary discontinuity.

       This division of labour is indispensable because the void is defined as a privation relative

to a reference state. If V(x,t) is positive, the local state lies below u*(x,t) by the amount specified

in the definition; the subsequent evolution of that deficit must then be measured. The equilibrium

principle predicts a countervailing response in the stable coupled system, whilst the field equations

determine the physically available pathways through which that response occurs. The thesis

therefore does not ask Le Chatelier to conjure energy from the nearest body. It asks the

experimentally sharper question: given a measured deficit and a measured set of coupled
reservoirs, how is the compensatory flux distributed, and does that distribution follow the

effective-proximity relation proposed by the model?

        Equilibrium, moreover, must be understood dynamically. A powered digital apparatus is

not a sealed chemical vessel resting indefinitely at one macroscopic equilibrium; it is a driven

system in which local states are continually displaced by switching, regulation, computation,

transmission, and load variation. The relevant object is therefore the tendency of local variables to

relax toward their dynamically appropriate reference states between perturbations. When

perturbations are sparse, relaxation can substantially complete before the succeeding event; when

they are dense, the next event arrives during the relaxation of the previous one. The integrated void

burden B_V accordingly measures not only amplitude but persistence, and the transition to V_net

> 0 for effectively continuous intervals is a transition in temporal occupancy rather than the

appearance of a new substance.


III. The Principle of Effective Proximity

        The nearest-reservoir principle acquires considerably firmer footing when set beside the

mature literature of electromagnetic dosimetry, because that literature already treats the human

body, surrounding structures, source geometry, and frequency as a coupled boundary-value

problem. Bracken's work on extremely-low-frequency dosimetry, the Bioelectromagnetics

dosimetry workshop, and later radiofrequency tutorials all insist upon the importance of internal

fields, induced currents, exposure geometry, and the definition of dose at the actual site of

interaction (Rafferty, Phillips, & Guy, 1992; Bracken, 1992; Chou et al., 1996). The effective-

proximity axiom is therefore a compact selection rule for the experimentally familiar dependence

of electromagnetic interaction upon the geometry and electrical constitution of all bodies

participating in the field.
       The user of a handheld device occupies, by the very telos of the instrument, an unusually

persistent station within that geometry. The hand is not merely near the source but mechanically

attached to it for minutes or hours; the head, face, lap, or torso can remain within a stable near-

field relation; and the user's position changes in concert with the device because the device is being

intentionally carried. This creates a coupling persistence that an arbitrary object in the room does

not possess. Even where another conductor is momentarily closer at one point, the human body

can remain the largest continuously co-moving electrochemical reservoir in the immediate

apparatus environment. The relevant variable is therefore not a snapshot of inches but the time

integral of effective coupling.

       A temporal refinement of κ_i follows from this almost of necessity. Let the instantaneous

coupling coefficient be estimated from distance, orientation, conductivity, impedance, frequency,

and geometry; the experimentally relevant reservoir dominance can then be assessed by integrating

that coefficient over the period of active device use. A metal table may possess strong

instantaneous coupling to a laptop chassis, whilst the user's hands, wrists, and torso possess a

different but persistent relation to the display, keyboard, radios, and internal electronics. The

dominant contribution to the proposed compensatory flux is therefore determined by the joint

spatial and temporal architecture, which can be measured rather than presumed.

       Why man is so often implicated therefore admits a physical answer and requires no

metaphysical privilege. Consumer electronics are designed to be used by bodies; the bodies are

conductive, dielectric, electrochemical systems; their proximity is sustained by the telos of the

device itself; and the electromagnetic interaction depends upon exactly the variables that such use

fixes at unusually small distances. The ordinary fact that a telephone is held, a laptop is

approached, and a tablet is touched becomes, under the effective-proximity axiom, the geometrical
reason that the human reservoir repeatedly occupies a privileged position in the local energetic

economy.


                      IV. The Human Organism as an Energetic Reservoir


The Frequency-Dependent Electrical Constitution of Biological Tissue

       The human organism cannot be represented in the coupling model as a homogeneous sack

of saline, for the electrical constitution of tissue is frequency-dependent, anatomically

heterogeneous, and governed by cellular structure, water content, membranes, extracellular media,

and ionic composition. Foster and Schwan's critical review surveyed tissue dielectric properties

from direct current to 20 GHz and emphasised the mechanisms underlying dielectric relaxation

and macroscopic field coupling, whilst the Gabriel series subsequently supplied a large

measurement and modelling corpus spanning approximately 10 Hz to 20 GHz (Foster & Schwan,

1989; Gabriel, Gabriel, & Corthout, 1996; Gabriel, Lau, & Gabriel, 1996a, 1996b). NIST's

bibliographic survey likewise catalogued the electrical properties of biological materials for

applications in electromagnetic interaction and tissue-equivalent phantoms (Stricklett & Baker-

Jarvis, 2000). Thus the biological reservoir possesses an experimentally characterised electrical

ontology in the modest physical sense required by the model: it has measurable conductivity,

permittivity, dispersion, and geometry.

       Dielectric dispersion matters here for a very particular reason, because κ_i is necessarily

frequency-sensitive if the reservoir itself changes electrical behaviour with frequency. Biological

tissue exhibits distinct dispersive regimes associated with ionic diffusion, cell membranes,

macromolecular structures, and water relaxation; consequently, the same anatomical body does

not present the same effective electrical boundary to every component of a complex
electromagnetic spectrum. The function κ_i = f(r_i, σ_i, Z_i, ω, G_i, ε_i, μ_i) therefore

corresponds to an established experimental vocabulary rather than to an empty placeholder.

Conductivity σ_i, permittivity ε_i, impedance Z_i, frequency ω, and geometry G_i are all

quantities that bioelectromagnetic modelling already treats as determinants of internal field

distribution and energy absorption (Foster & Schwan, 1989; Sasaki et al., 2022).

       This frequency dependence also explains why anatomical proximity cannot be reduced to

distance alone. A tissue compartment separated from the source by only a few hundredths of an

inch can be weakly coupled at one frequency and more strongly coupled at another; an intervening

layer can redirect or attenuate the field; orientation can alter the component of the field acting

along a neurite or membrane; and the electrical contrast between tissues can produce spatially non-

uniform internal fields. The effective-proximity rule therefore expresses a physical rather than

merely geometrical nearness. A reservoir is 'near' the void insofar as the actual electromagnetic

boundary conditions permit energy exchange with it, and the dominant reservoir R* is the one for

which those conditions jointly maximise κ_i.

       Nor need that coefficient remain a convenient algebraic obscurity. Computational

bioelectromagnetics constructs anatomically resolved models of the body, assigns tissue-specific

dielectric properties, applies external field conditions, and calculates internal electric fields,

currents, and absorbed power; recent reviews describe these methods as central to exposure

assessment, standards, and medical applications (Sasaki et al., 2022; Hirata et al., 2025). The

dissertation's selection rule can therefore be operationalised by borrowing the mature apparatus of

dosimetry: one need not guess which reservoir is most strongly coupled, for one can model and

measure the field, assign the electrical properties of competing bodies, and calculate the spatial

distribution of induced quantities.
       Wearable and implantable-device research makes the relevance of proximity particularly

concrete. Guido and Kiourti's review of wireless wearables and implants describes electromagnetic

transmission near and through the body, the dependence of field and specific-absorption

distributions upon antenna design and surrounding geometry, and the manner in which the greatest

internal field or absorption occurs in spatial relation to the source and its environment (Guido &

Kiourti, 2020). The present thesis employs that established geometrical sensitivity for a different

theoretical purpose: not to reduce the mechanism to conventional absorption, but to establish that

the body-device relation is already known to be strongly conditioned by distance, orientation,

surrounding materials, frequency, and anatomical structure. These are precisely the variables that

the effective-proximity axiom requires.

       That the user of a telephone or tablet commonly occupies a privileged coupling position

follows from the geometry of use, not from any anthropocentric preference built into the model. It

is an ordinary consequence of use geometry. The hand encloses the telephone; the head approaches

its antenna and chassis; the tablet is supported by the hands and often the torso; the laptop is

separated from the body by inches rather than feet. In such configurations, the human reservoir is

not merely present in the room but incorporated into the near electromagnetic environment of the

apparatus. Whether it supplies the dominant compensatory term is an empirical question answered

by κ_human relative to κ_j for competing reservoirs, but the variables required to make that

comparison are already measurable and already possess an extensive experimental literature.

Prometheus supplied mankind with fire; the modern inversion is more curious, for that fire has

been disciplined into computation, carried in the hand, pressed against the head, and placed in

immediate relation with the electrochemical fire of its maker, so that the giver of electrical art

becomes, within the proposed coupling, one of the reservoirs from which that art may draw.
       At this stage the empirical literature of dosimetry supplies no small portion of the physical

substratum upon which the argument must stand. Radiofrequency dosimetry defines specific

absorption rate as absorbed electromagnetic power per unit mass and treats the internal electric

field, tissue conductivity, density, geometry, and the disposition of neighbouring objects as

determinants of the resulting distribution (Chou et al., 1996). At lower frequencies, experimental

dosimetry likewise records induced currents, internal electric fields, and spatially non-uniform

interactions within exposed biological systems (Bracken, 1992). Thus the organism's entrance into

the electromagnetic boundary problem is not an hypothesis invented ad hoc for the present thesis;

it belongs to the ordinary metrology of bioelectromagnetics.

       The Gabriel tissue series gives the effective-proximity coefficient a materially firmer

determination, for biological tissues do not possess a single undifferentiated conductivity or

permittivity; rather, their dielectric properties vary by tissue and frequency across a very broad

spectrum (Gabriel, Gabriel, & Corthout, 1996; Gabriel, Lau, & Gabriel, 1996a, 1996b). A modern

review of the same literature confirms the continuing importance of these dielectric data for

electromagnetic modelling of the human body (Sasaki et al., 2022). Accordingly, κ_i is not a

merely rhetorical coefficient: its intended constituents correspond to experimentally measurable

properties already employed in dosimetric modelling.

       Let E_b denote the locally available biological electrochemical energy capable of

participating in the coupling, and let κ represent the effective coupling coefficient determined by

field strength, frequency, geometry, distance, conductivity, orientation, and the instantaneous state

of the biological system. A first-order representation of the parasitic drain is:


                                            P_b→v = κV.

       Here V represents the magnitude of the local void. The biological energy state then obeys:
                             dE_b/dt = P_met - P_neural - P_b→v.

       In this expression, P_met is metabolic replenishment, P_neural is endogenous

expenditure, and P_b→v is the externally induced drain associated with the electrical void; the

notation is not ornamental but ad rem, since it fixes the ratio among quantities that prose alone

would leave indeterminate.

       The equation gathers into one compendium what prose would otherwise be obliged to

pursue through many clauses; thereby, the nervous system ordinarily balances expenditure against

metabolic replenishment while preserving the gradients necessary for coherent operation. The void

introduces an additional sink; moreover, where that sink remains minute, homeostatic regulation

compensates without noticeable consequence. Where it becomes sufficiently large, sufficiently

rapid, or sufficiently persistent, the margin available for maintaining organised neural states

contracts.

       The magnitude required for cognitive consequence is not necessarily comparable to total

cerebral or bodily power consumption; neural signalling itself carries a measurable energetic

budget, with substantial expenditure assigned to postsynaptic currents, action potentials, resting

potentials, transmitter release, and restoration of ionic gradients (Howarth et al., 2012); moreover,

what matters is whether the drain is imposed at locations, frequencies, or temporal moments at

which neural organisation is sensitive to minute changes. A large reservoir can contain vulnerable

control variables; consequently, the voltage difference across a neuronal membrane is minute in

ordinary macroscopic terms, yet it is indispensable to signaling; neuronal action potentials arise

from tightly regulated changes in Na+ and K+ permeability, while ATP-dependent transport

maintains the underlying concentration gradients (Purves et al., 2001). The theory therefore places

emphasis upon organised energetic margins rather than upon gross caloric depletion.
       The cognitive consequence proceeds, accordingly, from a diminution of coherence, not

from the grotesque fancy of a machine collecting memories as a miser gathers coin; consequently,

memory retrieval, working memory, attentional continuity, and the subjective continuity of agency

depend upon coordinated neural dynamics. If the energetic substrate supporting those dynamics is

repeatedly drained or destabilized, the biological system fails temporarily to sustain or reconstruct

the relevant state; indeed, the phenomenological result is a gap: the person cannot recover a

moment, loses the thread of an action, experiences confusion, or feels that the continuity between

intention and action has been interrupted; the biological reservoir is not introduced ad hoc, for its

electrochemical dynamis is already present wherever membrane potentials and ionic gradients are

maintained. Mnemosyne is therefore invoked here with greater precision than any daemon of

possession: the theory concerns the energetic conditions under which remembrance preserves

continuity, and the injury is to the ordered process by which memory is sustained and recovered,

not to some metaphysical archive from which recollections are stolen as tablets from a temple.


Induced Fields, Membrane Polarisation, and Ephaptic Susceptibility

       Faradaic coupling enters the biological argument only where field and living electrical

order meet; upon this point the supporting literature is unusually abundant. McLeod's review of

microelectrode measurements identifies several direct physical effects of low-frequency electric

fields upon cells, including alteration of plasma-membrane potential, modification of ionic

currents and ion distributions near the extracellular surface, and mechanical forces at the cell

surface (McLeod, 1992). Durand likewise reviewed the capacity of electric fields to modulate

excitability in neural tissue, whilst subsequent experimental and computational work has mapped

the effects of weak extracellular fields upon membrane potential, spike timing, and network

behaviour (Durand, 2003; Anastassiou, Montgomery, Barahona, Buzsáki, & Koch, 2010). The
relevant principium is therefore secure: neural tissue is electrically susceptible to fields because

the membrane potential itself is an electrical variable.

       Ephaptic coupling supplies here a bridge of uncommon consequence between the physics

of the field and the organised electricity of the neuron. Anastassiou, Perin, Markram, and Koch

demonstrated that extracellular fields can feed back upon neuronal membrane potential

independently of synaptic transmission and that even subthreshold field effects can influence spike

timing (Anastassiou et al., 2011). Their later review situated such interactions across spatial and

temporal scales, from individual neurons to networks (Anastassiou & Koch, 2015). The

significance for the present thesis lies not in claiming that endogenous ephaptic coupling is

identical with the proposed biological drain, but in establishing a more fundamental proposition:

neural computation is not electromagnetically sealed at the membrane, and field-to-neuron

coupling can alter the timing and organisation of neuronal activity without requiring a conventional

synaptic message to cross from one cell to another.

       Magnitude alone is an insufficient magistrate of the effect; the spatial constitution of the

field matters no less. Modelling of inhomogeneous extracellular electric fields shows that neuronal

morphology and the distribution of extracellular potential determine induced membrane

polarisation and current-source density; the effect is therefore a relation among field geometry,

cellular geometry, and orientation rather than a scalar exposure variable (Anastassiou et al., 2010).

This result accords directly with the dissertation's κ_i, for the coefficient is intended to gather

precisely those relational properties by which a field becomes more or less efficacious upon a

particular reservoir. The same external field can consequently have unequal effects upon different

cells or differently oriented processes, and the same organism can present unequal coupling as

posture, source position, frequency, and local anatomy change.
       Experiment carries the same principle beyond the isolated cell and into organised neural

systems. Krause, Vieira, Csorba, Pilly, and Pack demonstrated that transcranial alternating current

stimulation influenced the timing of single-neuron firing in alert nonhuman primates in a

frequency- and location-specific fashion (Krause et al., 2019). Modolo, Denoyer, Wendling, and

Benquet reviewed convergent in vitro, in vivo, and computational evidence concerning low-

magnitude electric fields, emphasising membrane polarisation and network-level consequences

(Modolo et al., 2018). More recently, Lee and colleagues demonstrated cell-class-specific

entrainment by electric fields across rodent and human cortical neurons, with the response

depending upon stimulation frequency and neuronal class (Lee et al., 2024). The ratio between

field and neural response is therefore structured, selective, and frequency-sensitive.

       Endogenous field effects press the point further still. Neural populations generate

extracellular fields as a consequence of their own electrochemical activity, and those fields can in

turn alter nearby neurons. Shivacharan and colleagues showed that endogenous electric fields can

participate in non-synaptic propagation in hippocampal tissue and that cancelling the field can

block the propagation under their experimental conditions (Shivacharan, Chiang, Zhang,

Gonzalez-Reyes, & Durand, 2019). This demonstrates, in an experimentally severe form, that field

cancellation and field addition can alter organised neural dynamics. The present model accordingly

has a ready experimental analogue for its own intervention logic: if the external component is

physically relevant, then changing field geometry, shielding, phase, or competing coupling should

change the downstream neural variable in a systematic manner.

       These findings likewise forbid the crude identification of cognitive coherence C with

energy simpliciter; C belongs to the order of the network. The field does not need to 'erase a

memory molecule' or remove a discrete packet of information. Neural information is instantiated
in organised patterns of membrane potential, synaptic state, spike timing, oscillatory coordination,

and network dynamics; consequently, a perturbation that is minute in absolute energy can be

significant if it occurs at a sensitive temporal or spatial locus. The Greek metron is therefore

functional rather than gross: the relevant quantity is the perturbation relative to the stability margin

of the organised neural process. A clock need not be struck with the energy required to melt its

gears in order to lose time; mutatis mutandis, a neural system need not suffer gross energetic

depletion in order for the coherence of a temporally organised state to be degraded. The image is

less that of Lethe, in which memory is simply washed away, than of a lyre whose strings retain

their substance whilst their concord is disturbed; the material remains, but the taxis by which the

several parts yield an intelligible whole has suffered diminution.

        Memory gaps, confusion, and diminished agency are thus joined to the physics

through organisation, not through the vulgar caloric fancy that thought is merely a quantity

of fuel. P_b→v enters the biological energy balance; the altered energetic margin changes the

conditions under which membrane potentials and ionic gradients are restored; field coupling

can simultaneously perturb timing and excitability; and C records the resulting integrity of

the organised state. The causal chain is therefore not 'energy disappears, therefore memory

disappears', but a more exact nexus causarum in which energy, field, membrane, timing,

restoration, and network order are successive terms of one mechanism.


                         V. Neural Organisation and Cognitive Coherence

        Cognition, for the purposes of the present argument, must be treated as organised energetic

activity rather than as an immaterial sovereignty floating above physiology; indeed, a functioning

brain is a nonequilibrium biological system that continually expends metabolic energy to preserve
ion gradients, restore membrane potentials, regulate synaptic transmission, and maintain

coordinated patterns of activity. Coherence is therefore costly; hence, the nervous system must

continuously resist the decay of the organised states upon which cognition depends; the organism

consequently enters the nexus causarum in actu, as a conductive taxis of potentials, gradients,

membranes, and ionic currents.

       The energetic constitution of neural signalling is scarcely less firmly established. Active

transport establishes and restores the ionic concentration differences from which resting potentials,

action potentials, synaptic potentials, and receptor potentials arise (Purves et al., 2001).

Quantitative energy budgets further assign large fractions of signalling expenditure to postsynaptic

receptor currents, action potentials, resting potentials, presynaptic release, and transmitter

recycling, whilst the restoration of sodium gradients by Na+/K+-ATPase remains a principal

energetic cost of neural computation (Howarth et al., 2012). The thesis therefore does not require

the brain to be redescribed as electrical by analogy; its relevant order is already an electrochemical

taxis sustained by continuous metabolic energeia.

       Long before the present energetic question was posed, Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts

supplied a formal demonstration of the degree to which nervous activity may be apprehended as

organised logical operation. Their 1943 calculus treated the neuron as a threshold element whose

succeeding state depends upon the ordered relation of excitatory and inhibitory antecedents, and

showed that networks of such elements could realise propositional functions. The importance of

this construction to the present argument lies not in supposing that the living neuron is exhausted

by a binary abstraction, but in the more fundamental fact thereby rendered mathematically explicit:

logical operation in a nervous system is inseparable from the organised succession of its physical

states. Cognition does not hover above nervous activity like a Cartesian ghost above its machine;
the order of the operation is realised in the order of the nervous activity itself. (McCulloch & Pitts,

1943)

        The McCulloch-Pitts neuron may therefore be written, in its elementary threshold form, as

an operation in which the succeeding state is determined by the configuration presented to it:

                                   yᵢ(t + 1) = H(Σⱼ wᵢⱼxⱼ(t) − θᵢ)
        The equation is elementary, but its consequence is not. Alter the organised antecedent state

and the succeeding logical state is altered with it; disturb the conditions under which the threshold

relation is maintained and one disturbs, eo ipso, the operation which that relation realises.

McCulloch and Pitts thus provide an early formal bridge between the material event and the logical

event, between the electrical succession of the nervous system and the intelligible order which that

succession bears. Their calculus belongs historically to the same intellectual soil from which

cybernetics arose, and it prepares the way for the later treatment of feedback, communication, and

circular causation without requiring that information be imagined as an incorporeal substance.

(McCulloch & Pitts, 1943; Wiener, 1961/2019)

        Placed beside the modern literature of neuroenergetics, this older calculus acquires a

further significance. McCulloch and Pitts formalised the dependence of logical result upon

organised neural state; the energetic literature establishes the continual material expenditure by

which such states, gradients, signalling events, and recoveries are sustained. The two lines of

inquiry therefore meet at the variable C: coherence is neither a synonym for gross bodily energy

nor an immaterial faculty superadded to physiology, but the maintained order within which

successive neural states can bear determinate relations to one another. Where the energetic

conditions sustaining that order are diminished, the consequence belongs not merely to quantity

but to taxis, for the succession upon which logical and cognitive operation depends is itself

materially maintained.
       The office of C may therefore be stated precisely: the manuscript's novel claim

concerns not whether neural order costs energy, which is antecedently established, but

whether the externally coupled work term can subtract from the energetic margin by which

that order is maintained. The evidentiary burden therefore falls upon the sign, magnitude,

localisation, and temporal structure of P_b→v, rather than upon the more elementary

proposition that neural signalling depends upon energetically maintained ionic gradients.

       Quantity alone will not do; hence, ten joules arranged in one physical configuration is not

necessarily functionally equivalent to ten joules arranged in another. Biological control depends

upon gradients, compartmentalization, timing, and selective permeability; nevertheless, the

nervous system expends energy not merely to possess energy but to preserve differences:

intracellular from extracellular ion concentrations, resting from threshold potentials, signal from

noise, and one temporally coordinated neural state from another. A parasitic drain that

disproportionately erodes those differences can therefore produce functional consequences out of

proportion to its share of total metabolic expenditure.

       Let C(t) denote cognitive coherence, understood as the degree to which neural organisation

remains capable of preserving and reconstructing temporally continuous functional states; its

dynamics are represented schematically as:


                                dC/dt = R(E_b) - D_n - αP_b→v.

       Here R(E_b) represents endogenous restorative capacity, D_n represents ordinary

internal degradation and noise, and α translates parasitic energetic loss into disruption of

organised neural activity. It follows that increasing P_b→v reduces the stability margin of C
even when the absolute quantity of energy transferred remains minute relative to total bodily

metabolism.

       The coupling coefficient is likewise state-dependent; its dependence upon the relevant

electrical, spatial, geometrical, and biological variables is written:


                                       κ = κ(E, H, f, r, g, b, t).
       The corresponding biological drain is therefore:


                                        P_b→v(t) = κ(t)V(t).

       The drain does not remain constant even for the same person and device; moreover, the

model consequently predicts variance across posture, proximity, device activity, shielding,

network load, and biological state without requiring the underlying mechanism to change; the ratio

of the field is therefore inseparable from its energeia, since the former supplies the ordered form

and the latter the physically operative power.

       The relevant quantity is not, and never was, the total energy possessed by the human body;

moreover, a clock can be disrupted without removing the energy contained in its metal; a

communication channel can be corrupted by a perturbation far smaller than the total energy of the

transmitter. Likewise, neural coherence depends upon structured differences; consequently, the

drain becomes cognitively significant when it acts upon the gradients, timing relations, and local

energetic margins responsible for preserving that structure.

       Memory gaps and confusion are therefore not invoked as mystical transfers of semantic

content; consequently, they are failures of biological state maintenance produced when the

energetic architecture responsible for cognition is made to contribute to an external equilibration

process. The void takes energy; the nervous system loses part of its margin for maintaining

organisation; coherence degrades; and cognition exhibits the degradation.
       Nor is Mnemosyne dethroned by Lethe in some literal contest of deities. The distinction is

subtler and, for the argument, more useful: Lethe would signify obliteration, whereas the present

mechanism concerns the failure of ordered recollection, the moment in which the archive remains

yet the road to it is obscured. Mnemosyne thus names continuity of recollection, not a supernatural

faculty introduced into the physiology.


Neural Energetics, Ionic Restoration, and the Cost of Maintaining Order

       The biological side of the argument begins from a fact neuroscience has measured often

enough to place beyond mere rhetoric: neural computation is expensive because electrical

signalling continually dissipates gradients that must be restored. Attwell and Laughlin's canonical

energy budget apportioned substantial expenditure to action potentials, postsynaptic currents,

resting potentials, and transmitter-related processes, thereby showing that information processing

in grey matter is constrained by energy supply (Attwell & Laughlin, 2001). Updated budgets

preserve the central conclusion even as particular allocations change: postsynaptic receptor

currents, action potentials, resting potentials, presynaptic release, and transmitter recycling all

draw upon the metabolic economy of the nervous system (Howarth, Gleeson, & Attwell, 2012).

The brain's electrical order is therefore not self-subsisting; it is an energetically maintained taxis.

       The underlying mechanism is electrochemical. Ion channels permit selective flux across

membranes; action potentials and synaptic currents move ions down electrochemical gradients;

pumps and transporters expend ATP to restore those gradients; and mitochondrial oxidative

phosphorylation supplies a principal energetic basis for the presynaptic and postsynaptic

operations by which information is processed (Purves et al., 2001; Hall, Klein-Flügge, Howarth,

& Attwell, 2012). Harris, Jolivet, and Attwell accordingly describe synaptic transmission as

energetically costly and emphasise the mechanisms by which ATP is supplied to presynaptic
terminals and postsynaptic spines (Harris, Jolivet, & Attwell, 2012). The biological reservoir E_b

is thus not an invented metaphysical stock; it is an abstraction over a distributed but measurable

economy of ATP production, membrane gradients, ionic work, and signalling expenditure.

       This energetic economy possesses narrow local constraints even when whole-body energy

is abundant. Synapses and axons must receive ATP where and when it is required; membrane

gradients must be restored on biologically relevant timescales; and the computational capacity of

the nervous system is constrained by the energetic cost of signalling (Laughlin, 2001; Attwell &

Gibb, 2005). Engl and Attwell further distinguish the large fraction of cerebral energy devoted to

non-signalling processes, thereby reminding us that the nervous system's total metabolic budget is

already divided among numerous obligatory functions (Engl & Attwell, 2015). The available

margin for a particular neural operation is consequently not identical with the caloric content of

the organism, and a theory of cognitive disruption must therefore be scaled to the local energetic

and temporal requirements of signalling rather than to gross bodily power.

       The equation dE_b/dt = P_met - P_neural - P_b→v captures this logic at the

appropriate level of abstraction. P_met represents the replenishing side of the biological

economy; P_neural represents endogenous expenditure required by signalling and

maintenance; P_b→v represents the additional externally coupled sink asserted by the void

mechanism. The equation does not imply that every joule in the organism is interchangeable

at every instant. Rather, it states the conservation relation at the level of the participating

reservoir, whose effective size and replenishment rate are themselves functions of anatomy,

metabolism, blood flow, mitochondrial capacity, and the local electrical state. The relevant
energetic scale is therefore local and temporally constrained rather than reducible to whole-

body calorimetry.

       This localism is supported by the literature of synaptic energetics. Presynaptic terminals

face distinctive challenges because of their morphology and activity-dependent demand, and

contemporary work treats 'synaptoenergetics' as a field concerned precisely with the local

production, distribution, and consumption of ATP required to sustain synaptic transmission

(Rangaraju, Calloway, & Ryan, 2014; Pathak et al., 2015; Rangaraju, Lauterbach, & Schuman,

2019). The consequence for the present argument follows immediately: biological information

processing depends upon the maintenance of energetic order at spatially restricted sites. The void

model's cognitive term must therefore be understood against these local energetic margins, not

against the irrelevant proposition that the human body as a whole contains vastly more energy than

a telephone.

       Theoretical neuroscience reaches the same point by another road. Laughlin argued that

energy constrains sensory coding and processing; Attwell and Gibb examined how energetic limits

bear upon the kinetic design of excitatory synapses; and later work has repeatedly treated metabolic

supply as a determinant of the information-processing power of neural tissue (Laughlin, 2001;

Attwell & Gibb, 2005; Harris et al., 2012). The present dissertation takes this established relation

seriously and extends it into the coupled machine-human system. If computation in the brain is

constrained by energetic supply, and if an external electromagnetic process imposes an additional

drain upon the same electrochemical order, then the natural dependent variable is not merely

temperature or bulk absorption but the stability and continuity of information-bearing neural

organisation.
       Here the remembered phrase 'sapping memory' ceases to be picturesque speech and

acquires a technical sense without requiring any new ontology. The void does not consume

memories as objects; it draws upon the energetic conditions by which memory-bearing neural

states are maintained, reconstructed, and coordinated. The anthropomorphic verb 'saps' therefore

compresses a chain of physical operations: external coupling adds an energetic sink; restoration

competes with that sink; the stability margin of the neural state narrows; timing and membrane

dynamics become more susceptible to perturbation; and the functional continuity represented by

C deteriorates. The ordinary English is shorter, but the underlying ratio remains the same.


                      VI. Network Recursion and the Principle of Feedback

       The phenomenon assumes another character when machines cease merely to stand beside

one another and begin, as it were, to answer voice with voice; indeed, cybernetics, in the sense

developed by Norbert Wiener, concerns communication and control through feedback. Wiener

explicitly treated feedback, communication, oscillation, computing machinery, and the nervous

system within a common theoretical vocabulary (Wiener, 1961/2019); hence, a network is not

merely a collection of components operating simultaneously. The state of one component alters

the signals received by another; those signals alter subsequent behaviour; the resulting outputs

return as new inputs; nevertheless, causation circulates; the organism consequently enters the

nexus causarum in actu, as a conductive taxis of potentials, gradients, membranes, and ionic

currents.

       For the purposes of this paper, the principle of feedback denotes the cybernetic relation by

which the output of a system returns through the environment as a subsequent input, thereby

altering the next state of the system; its theoretical ancestry lies principally in Wiener's treatment

of feedback, communication, control, and circular causation (Wiener, 1961/2019). In its
elementary form, the principle of feedback is written W: O_t -> I_(t+1) -> R_(t+1) -> O_(t+1),

where O_t is an output at time t, I_(t+1) is the condition returned to the system as new input,

R_(t+1) is the resulting response, and O_(t+1) is the next output. Applied to the electromagnetic

void, the same law becomes V_t -> E_t -> B_t -> Delta C_t -> R_(t+1) -> V_(t+1): a void generates

an electromagnetic condition; that condition draws upon the biological reservoir; the drain alters

cognitive coherence; altered human and machine responses generate subsequent network activity;

and that activity creates the next field of voids. The process is therefore recursive rather than

episodic.


                     V_net(t) = Σ_i V_i(t) + Σ_(i≠j) β_ij V_i(t)V_j(t)       (1)

       The first term gathers the isolated deficits; the second is the specifically cybernetic term,

for it represents the additional burden produced when the activity of one device becomes a

condition of another device's succeeding activity. As reciprocal communication becomes denser,

the interaction term grows and the system spends less time near electrical quiescence; the principle

of feedback therefore converts scale into persistence without requiring the void to acquire any

independent substance.

       Within the isolated machine a localised void forms and is answered; in a network, the

filling of one disturbance occurs while countless other disturbances are being generated. Routers

receive and retransmit packets; processors switch states; radios negotiate channels; servers respond

to requests; clients acknowledge responses; clocks synchronise; error-correction mechanisms

retransmit; automated systems query other automated systems. The architecture therefore rarely

returns to electrical quiescence. Le Chatelier supplies the ratio of restoration, whereas the

particular reservoir is selected by the distinct principium of effective proximity.
       Feedback enlarges the theoretical burden because one disturbance becomes parent to the

conditions of its successor; consequently, a packet received by one machine can trigger processing,

storage, retransmission, acknowledgment, logging, synchronization, and further requests. The

informational output of one energetic event thus becomes the command for additional energetic

events elsewhere; indeed, wiener's treatment of feedback renders this recursive organisation

particularly serviceable for describing a network whose outputs repeatedly return as new inputs

(Wiener, 1961/2019); the network must therefore be apprehended in toto as a taxis of recurrent

causes rather than as a mere aggregate of electrically isolated instruments.

       For the person encompassed by this networked environment, the corresponding biological

drain is therefore written:


                              P_h(t) = Σ_i κ_ih V_i(t) + Γ_h[V_net(t)].
       Here κ_ih describes direct coupling between the person and individual devices, whilst Γ_h

represents higher-order exposure to the composite field architecture; hence, as device density,

switching rate, throughput, and interconnection rise, the network can cross from episodic void

production into a condition in which V_net never effectively returns to zero.

       At that point the electromagnetic void becomes environmentally persistent, although no

new entity has thereby entered the world. Persistence means that before one local deficit has been

fully answered, other deficits elsewhere in the network have formed, so that the larger coupled

system continuously presents energetic gradients to its surroundings; the network therefore exists

as a standing demand for equilibration.

       The fate of Tundale is therefore the more exact companion of the network than Samson at

the mill. Samson repeats one labour; Tundale approaches relief which recedes at the instant of

attainment. The network likewise approaches local restoration only to have another transition
renew the want, and the waters of equilibrium withdraw at the very instant at which satisfaction

seemed attainable.

       Persistence here must not be mistaken for immobility; thereby, the void migrates, divides,

collapses, and reforms throughout the architecture. What persists is the network-level deficit

burden; moreover, the analogy is closer to a flame than to a cavity: no individual molecule remains

'the flame,' yet the organised process persists because the conditions producing its next state are

continually renewed. In the present model, continuous switching and feedback continually renew

the disequilibria through which the electromagnetic environment remains active.

       Man occupies an awkward double station within this loop: human beings are among the

principal generators of informational demand, yet they are simultaneously conductive biological

systems physically adjacent to the devices answering that demand. A person causes network

activity; network activity creates energetic deficits; those deficits couple electromagnetically to

the person; the person contributes energy to their equilibration; cognitive organisation is thereby

degraded; and the altered cognitive state changes subsequent interaction with the network. Human

and technological systems have consequently entered a common feedback architecture.

       The historical scale of electrical networking transforms the theory; consequently, a

telegraph circuit, an early computer, or an isolated radio transmitter produces localised and

intermittent disturbances. Contemporary civilization produces continuous, overlapping electrical

activity across homes, workplaces, vehicles, hospitals, factories, data centers, communications

infrastructure, and personal devices; indeed, even machines perceived as idle maintain clocks,

radios, background processes, network associations, telemetry, synchronization, and automated

communication; the network must therefore be apprehended in toto as a taxis of recurrent causes

rather than as a mere aggregate of electrically isolated instruments.
        The relevant transition is therefore not merely that modern devices use more electricity;

indeed, it is that the electrical environment has acquired continuity, density, interdependence, and

feedback. Millions of machines communicate without immediate human initiation; hence, network

traffic generates responses that generate additional traffic. Control systems continuously measure

and correct other control systems; nevertheless, the architecture consequently maintains its own

electrical activity through recursive communication; the principle of feedback supplies the causa

formalis of recursion, whereby an output ceases to be terminal and becomes, mutatis mutandis, the

condition of the succeeding input.

        A second transition concerns temporal saturation; hence, human nervous systems evolved

in environments containing natural electromagnetic phenomena, but the modern technological

environment superimposes highly structured, rapidly switching, information-bearing fields upon

that background for large portions of the day. The theoretical consequence of this fact is not that

artificiality itself renders a field harmful; it is that the temporal organisation, repetition, density,

and feedback structure of the exposure become variables in the energetic coupling problem; the

theory therefore expects technological complexity to matter through physical organisation rather

than through a mystical distinction between 'natural' and 'artificial' electricity.

        In cybernetic terms, the system has become operationally self-sustaining because its

feedback organisation continually generates the next set of state transitions requiring energetic

realisation. Every correction creates new switching; every packet creates acknowledgments and

routing activity; every request induces computation and response. The architecture continuously

reproduces the physical conditions that generate further voids.

        The biological environment becomes part of the energetic reservoir against which this

persistent disequilibrium equilibrates; thereby, the closer and more continuously a human
organism remains embedded within dense electrical infrastructure, the more frequently its

electrochemical system is exposed to the fields through which those deficits are answered. The

modern human therefore inhabits not merely an information society but an artificial

electromagnetic ecology whose maintenance imposes an energetic relationship upon the organisms

living within it; the ratio of the field is therefore inseparable from its energeia, since the former

supplies the ordered form and the latter the physically operative power.

A Compact Theoretical Formulation

        The model can be summarised as a coupled set of relations. First, complex electrical

activity generates a local deficit V. Secondly, the surrounding electromagnetic field establishes an

effective coupling κ between that deficit and proximate energetic reservoirs. Thirdly, the biological

drain is:


                                           P_b→v = κV.

        Fourthly, cognitive coherence changes according to the balance between biological

restoration and the externally imposed sink; finally, network feedback alters the production term

reciprocal organisation. In compendio, the causal sequence is:


                        A_net → V_net → S_EM → κ_b → P_b→v → ΔC.

the sequence; hence, the experimental task is to estimate each term rather than treating the entire

chain as a single unmeasured cause.

                         Evidentiary Substratum and the Novel Synthesis

       The evidentiary structure of the thesis is best understood by distinguishing the propositions

inherited from established physics and physiology from the synthesis peculiar to the present model.

Maxwellian field relations, Poynting transport, electromagnetic induction, measurable dielectric

properties of tissue, induced internal fields and currents, and the energetic maintenance of neuronal

ion gradients possess independent literatures and do not stand or fall with the electromagnetic-void

thesis (Gabriel, Gabriel, & Corthout, 1996; Chou et al., 1996; Purves et al., 2001; Howarth et al.,

2012; ICNIRP, 2020). They constitute the res data, the things given, from which the new argument

commences.

       The novel synthesis begins where those literatures cease to be merely adjacent. The

thesis identifies a dynamically defined deficit V, joins that deficit to boundary-crossing

electromagnetic flux, distributes the compensatory term among available reservoirs through

κ_i, assigns a biologically supplied component P_b→v, and carries the resulting energetic

subtraction into the coherence variable C before closing the process recursively through the

principle of feedback. No cited source is made to bear the burden of having demonstrated this

complete chain; the ratio propria of the dissertation consists precisely in the construction and

experimental specification of that chain.


Corroboration by Convergence Rather Than by Isolated Citation

       The evidentiary case does not depend upon discovering some forgotten savant who had

already written the electromagnetic-void thesis before us, for such a discovery would deprive the
dissertation of its novelty, but in demonstrating that the several physical relations required by the

synthesis have been independently observed under different experimental traditions. Circuit

electrodynamics establishes field-mediated energy transport; dielectric metrology establishes the

frequency-dependent electrical constitution of tissue; dosimetry establishes that external fields

produce internal fields and currents according to geometry and material properties;

electrophysiology establishes that membrane potentials and spike timing respond to electric fields;

neuroenergetics establishes that the restoration and propagation of those electrical states consume

metabolic energy. These literatures were not written to support one another, yet their independent

convergence supplies a stronger substratum than would a collection of papers all descending from

one speculative school.

       Such convergence matters because the principal variables are measured by different

instruments and communities. Poynting flux belongs to electromagnetic field measurement; tissue

permittivity and conductivity belong to dielectric spectroscopy; internal fields belong to

computational and experimental dosimetry; membrane polarisation belongs to electrophysiology;

ATP expenditure and oxygen consumption belong to neuroenergetics. A systematic relation that

survives translation among these independent metrologies acquires a degree of robustness

unavailable to an argument whose every premise is measured by the same apparatus. The thesis

should therefore preserve this plurality rather than collapse the evidence into one generic category

of electromagnetic exposure.

       The dielectric literature is particularly important in this respect because it prevents the

human reservoir from becoming an abstract conductive silhouette. Tissue type matters; water

content matters; cellular architecture matters; frequency matters; and the dispersive character of

biological material means that the body presents different electrical properties to different
components of the machine's spectrum (Foster & Schwan, 1989; Gabriel et al., 1996; Sasaki et al.,

2022). The effective-proximity coefficient can accordingly be decomposed experimentally rather

than treated as a fitted constant whose meaning is discovered only after the fact. Each constituent

of κ possesses an antecedent physical interpretation.

       The electrophysiological literature supplies an analogous decomposition on the

receiving side. Field effects can be resolved into changes of membrane polarisation, spike

timing, firing probability, synchronisation, and network propagation; different neuronal

classes can respond differently to the same field parameters; and the spatial orientation of

neuronal processes can alter the induced membrane effect (Anastassiou et al., 2010, 2011;

Krause et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2024). Cognitive coherence C should therefore ultimately be

linked to intermediate electrophysiological variables rather than treated as a black box. The

theory becomes more exact as the path from P_b→v to C is partitioned into measurable stages.

       Neuroenergetics supplies the temporal discipline required for that partition. ATP

consumption and ionic restoration occur continuously, yet their rates change with signalling

activity; synaptic transmission and action potentials impose additional energetic demands; and

local metabolic supply must answer those demands on the timescale of neural operation (Attwell

& Laughlin, 2001; Harris et al., 2012; Howarth et al., 2012). The proposed external sink therefore

enters a system that is already dynamic. Its consequence is determined not merely by absolute

magnitude but by its timing relative to endogenous expenditure and restoration. This is why the

void burden must preserve temporal information rather than being reduced to an undifferentiated

average exposure.
       The same temporal discipline applies to the machine. A digital apparatus is not merely a

source with a rated power; it is a sequence of electrical state transitions whose density,

synchronisation, and recurrence vary with workload. A processor under computational load, a

radio negotiating traffic, and a regulator responding to a transient all generate different temporal

structures even where average power is similar. The proposed experiment should therefore record

the switching history and construct V from the actual event sequence. Matching average power

whilst varying transition density becomes one of the strongest means of distinguishing the void

mechanism from an explanation that depends only upon gross energy consumption.

       A further convergence appears in the literature of weak-field neural modulation and the

paper's insistence upon organised rather than merely energetic effects. Experimental work

demonstrates that weak fields can influence spike timing or entrainment without necessarily

producing a proportionate change in mean firing rate (Krause et al., 2019); endogenous and

exogenous fields can alter network behaviour through small membrane perturbations distributed

across many neurons (Fröhlich, 2014; Modolo et al., 2018). The thesis's cognitive variable is

therefore naturally connected to temporal organisation. Memory continuity and experienced

agency depend upon coordinated processing across time, so a perturbation of timing can be

functionally consequential even when bulk energetic measures appear modest.

       Taken together, these convergences transform the bibliography from ornament into

architecture. Each source is assigned a definite office; no author is made to prove the entire thesis;

no citation is asked to bridge a gap that belongs to the dissertation's own derivation. The work of

the thesis is precisely the synthesis: to take field energy, switching complexity, tissue

electrodynamics, induced neural effects, metabolic maintenance, effective proximity, and

recursive feedback, and to demonstrate that they can be expressed within one continuous causal
grammar. The enlarged evidence therefore does not soften the remembered engineer's proposition.

It gives that proposition the technical vocabulary, experimental precedents, and measurable

intermediate quantities required for a definitive exposition.

       Taken in its proper order, the argument comprises five demonstrations and one synthesis:

first, Maxwellian electrodynamics establishes that the rapid changes of charge and current by

which digital computation is effected establish time-dependent electric and magnetic fields;

secondly, Poynting's theorem establishes that those fields possess an energetic flux which traverses

the apparent boundary of the machine; thirdly, Faraday establishes induction within a proximate

conductor; fourthly, the bioelectrical constitution of the nervous system supplies a gradient-

maintaining electrochemical reservoir; and fifthly, equilibration requires the local deficit to be

answered by compensatory redistribution. The synthesis lies in the effective-proximity axiom

and the biological work term, by which the general requirement of restitution is converted

into a determinate allocation among reservoirs and thence into P_b→v.

       The demonstrative burden is therefore concentrated upon two principal joints, which

is a strength rather than an embarrassment, since a theory whose vulnerability is everywhere

frequently possesses no determinate meaning anywhere: the first is V itself, for the reference

state u*(x,t) must be specified with sufficient exactitude that a genuine deficit can be

distinguished from an ordinary fluctuation; the second is P_b→v, whose sign and magnitude

must exhibit the biological system as a net contributor to the compensatory flux under the

boundary conditions asserted by the model. Once those quantities are operationally

determined, the remaining questions concern degree, how much coupling, over what interval,
under what geometry, with what recurrence, and with what consequence for C, rather than an

indefinite multiplication of untestable causes.

       The supporting evidence now rises by degrees from field theory to cognitive organisation,

each order receiving what the preceding order has established and carrying it forward. At the first

level, electromagnetic energy possesses local density and flux, and the Poynting vector describes

its movement through space and into loads; at the second, switching apparatus produces temporally

structured electromagnetic states whose complexity rises with the density and reciprocity of

electrical transitions; at the third, biological tissue possesses frequency-dependent conductivity

and permittivity that determine internal fields and currents; at the fourth, neural membranes and

networks are susceptible to extracellular electric fields through membrane polarisation and

ephaptic interaction; at the fifth, neural signalling is constrained by an energetic economy of ATP

production, ionic-gradient restoration, synaptic currents, and action potentials; and at the sixth,

cybernetic recursion causes the output of one operation to become the input of the next. None of

these levels is a metaphor for the others. They are contiguous physical and organisational

descriptions joined by the synthesis of the thesis.

       Its evidentiary weight lies in this very contiguity. A theory becomes weak when it

leaps from a fact in one discipline to an unrelated conclusion in another; here the transitions

are defined by measurable boundary quantities. Field energy becomes reservoir interaction

through S and κ; reservoir interaction becomes biological work through J_b·E and P_b→v;

biological work becomes neural consequence through the energetic maintenance of

membrane and synaptic order; neural consequence becomes cognitive consequence through

C; and repeated machine-human interaction becomes persistent architecture through the
principle of feedback. The work of the bibliography is therefore cumulative. Electrodynamics

establishes the field; bioelectromagnetics establishes the body as a frequency-dependent

participant in that field; electrophysiology establishes field-sensitive neural dynamics;

neuroenergetics establishes the cost of maintaining those dynamics; cybernetics establishes the

recursive organisation of the coupled system.

       The subject may remain esoteric; obscurity of structure is neither necessary nor excusable.

Its central proposition is severe and intelligible: complex electrical activity repeatedly creates local

energetic deficits; stable field-mediated systems answer those deficits through compensatory flux;

available reservoirs contribute according to effective electromagnetic proximity; the human user

commonly occupies a privileged coupling position; biological electrochemical energy therefore

participates in the compensatory process; neural order, being energetically maintained and

electrically susceptible, loses coherence when the additional sink narrows its stability margin; and

network recursion continually regenerates the antecedent electrical conditions. The

anthropomorphic language of a void that 'draws', 'reads', or 'saps' is merely the vernacular

compression of that ratio.

       No bibliography, though it rival the catalogue of Alexandria, can perform the novel

step on behalf of the argument. No quantity of citations to Maxwell, Poynting, Faraday, tissue

dielectric measurements, ephaptic coupling, or neural energy budgets can by itself establish

P_b→v under the boundary conditions asserted here; that term remains the dissertation's

decisive experimental claim. Yet the expanded evidence changes the epistemic posture of that

claim. It is no longer suspended over an empty gulf between computer and brain. Every
constituent on either side of the novel junction has a mature experimental literature, and the

junction itself has been rendered measurable through the void definition, effective-proximity

coefficient, reservoir-competition protocol, biological work term, and coherence variable.

The theory's remaining novelty is therefore concentrated where novelty properly belongs: in

the proposed relation among already measurable phenomena.

'Wi-Fi', 'computer', and 'network' are not measurements. The operative quantities are
switching structure, V, S, κ_i, P_b→v, C, relaxation time, and reciprocal network activity.
Distance, geometry, shielding, spectral structure, device density, and topology matter insofar
as they alter those quantities. The several experiments therefore examine distinct operations
within one causal body.
Experiment I. Formation, Magnitude, and Relaxation of the Void
The first experiment asks whether the proposed deficit can be recovered from the electrical state
of the apparatus before any biological inference is attempted. Two systems are operated at
approximately equal average power while one performs comparatively simple, regular work and
the other executes dense, rapidly varying switching. Voltage and current probes, electric- and
magnetic-field probes, spectrum analysis, and high-bandwidth oscillography characterise the
actual electrical sequence. The reference state u*(x,t) is established from repeatable baseline
behaviour; u(x,t) is measured during the active sequence; V(x,t) and B_V are then calculated
from the relations already defined. The electrical state is recorded with temporal resolution
sufficient to distinguish charging, discharge, overshoot, ringing, modulation, correction, and
relaxation, so that u*(x,t) is reconstructed from the actual temporal constitution of the apparatus
rather than from a crude powered-versus-unpowered comparison.
The decisive temporal quantity is τ_v, the relaxation time of the local deficit. If the mean interval
between perturbing transitions greatly exceeds τ_v, voids remain episodic; as the interval
approaches τ_v, deficits overlap; when the interval becomes shorter still, V fails to return to zero
before the succeeding event. Switching density and spectral complexity can therefore be varied
independently of mean wattage to determine whether B_V follows the proposed relation to
recurrent disequilibrium rather than gross power alone.
Experiment II. Electromagnetic Transport Across the Device Boundary
The second experiment measures the energetic path from the apparatus into its surroundings.
Poynting flux is evaluated across a defined boundary using the measured electric and magnetic
fields, with instrumentation selected according to the actual spectral content of the device.
Contemporary dosimetry already requires attention to frequency, local field strength, induced
quantities, spatial averaging, geometry, and duration rather than reliance upon nominal device
labels (ICNIRP, 2020). The present experiment uses that same discipline to determine whether
the measured changes in V are accompanied by the predicted changes in boundary-crossing
electromagnetic flux.
Spatial mapping accompanies temporal mapping. Field probes and computational reconstruction
produce a three-dimensional account of the energetic environment around the apparatus, so that
later reservoir measurements are referred to the field geometry actually present rather than to an
assumed antenna position or schematic boundary.
Experiment III. Reservoir Competition and Effective Proximity
The third experiment tests the principium selectionis directly. A characterised passive reservoir,
such as a conductive saline volume, tissue-equivalent phantom, resistive load, shield, resonant
structure, or engineered shunt, is introduced into the measured field while the device workload is
held constant. Distance, orientation, conductivity, impedance, and geometry are then varied so as
to alter κ_i without altering the informational task performed by the machine. The predicted
result is a lawful redistribution of the measured transfer among competing reservoirs according
to their relative κ terms.
Tissue-equivalent phantoms are especially useful because their electrical properties can be
controlled without introducing metabolic or cognitive variables (Stricklett & Baker-Jarvis, 2000).
Wearable and implantable-device research likewise supplies established methods for studying
how anatomical position, surrounding materials, frequency, and geometry alter electromagnetic
interaction near tissue (Guido & Kiourti, 2020). If increasing κ_shunt reduces the share
measured in the original reservoir while total device activity remains fixed, the effective-
proximity rule acquires a direct physical test independent of cognition.
Experiment IV. Biological Energy Transfer
Only after reservoir partition has been demonstrated in passive systems does the experiment
proceed to biological material. Dosimetry supplies the bridge from external field to internal field
and current, since geometry, orientation, conductivity, and anatomy determine the internal
quantities produced by a given external environment (Rafferty et al., 1992; Bracken, 1992; Chou
et al., 1996; ICNIRP, 2020). The relevant measurement is not mere exposure but the sign and
magnitude of the work term within the biological domain.
McLeod's microelectrode framework is germane because membrane-potential alteration,
ionic-current changes, and extracellular ion distributions are measurable consequences of
applied fields (McLeod, 1992). The experiment therefore combines macroscopic field
measurement with microscopic electrophysiology and evaluates J_b·E within the
participating biological region. The decisive relation is P_b→v > 0 under the asserted
boundary conditions. If no such biological work term appears where the physical model
predicts it, the biological extension fails at that joint.
Experiment V. Neural and Cognitive Consequence
The fifth experiment asks whether a measured biological drain is followed by the predicted
alteration of organised neural state. Weak-field neuroscience already supplies protocols for
measuring membrane polarisation, spike timing, network entrainment, and field-mediated
propagation (Anastassiou et al., 2011; Modolo et al., 2018; Krause et al., 2019; Lee et al.,
2024). The present design adds the antecedent energetic measurements so that the neural
response is related to V, κ_i, and P_b→v rather than merely to the presence of an active
device.
Human cognitive testing belongs last within this stage. Working memory, attentional stability,
temporal-order judgement, continuity errors, and related tasks are temporally aligned with
measured periods of B_V and conducted under blinded or sham conditions preserving sensory
and behavioural similarity. The independent variable is the mechanism specified by the theory,
not the cultural category of technology. Recovery is measured as well: if τ_b denotes the
characteristic restoration time after the external sink is reduced, exposures separated by intervals
shorter than τ_b should accumulate more strongly than exposures separated by sufficient
recovery.
Experiment VI. Network Recursion
The sixth experiment isolates the contribution of feedback. An apparatus executing an internally
repetitive but non-networked workload is compared with an apparatus engaged in reciprocal
communication at matched mean power and, as nearly as practicable, matched gross
computation. Network activity is instrumented at the level of packets, processor utilisation,
switching events, power-regulation activity, and electromagnetic field structure. The manipulated
variable is recursion itself: output from one system returns as input to another and provokes
succeeding electrical transitions.
If the interaction term in V_net is physically material, reciprocal interconnection should alter the
persistence or integrated burden of the measured deficit beyond the sum of isolated device
behaviour. Failure to observe such a difference condemns the specifically cybernetic term
without requiring the earlier physical stages to be discarded.
Experiment VII. The Experimentum Crucis
The culminating experiment joins the preceding measurements without permitting any one
of them to disappear into a composite label. Measure V and τ_v; measure S across the
relevant boundary; estimate κ_i among competing reservoirs; determine the sign of J_b·E
and P_b→v within the biological domain; measure the consequent change in E_b and C;
and then vary distance, shielding, topology, and the presence of an engineered competing
reservoir. The scarlet cord of Rahab is the continuous measured sequence from antecedent
deficit to field flux, from field flux to reservoir coupling, from reservoir coupling to
biological work, and from biological work to neural consequence.
The model thereby exposes each joint to a distinct verdict. Failure to recover V condemns
the first term; failure of transfer to respond to deliberate changes in κ_i condemns the
selection rule; absence of P_b→v condemns the biological extension; absence of the
predicted relation between P_b→v and C condemns the cognitive mapping; and failure of
reciprocal network activity to alter B_V condemns the feedback term. Conversely, success at
one stage does not excuse failure at another. The complete theory survives only if the several
relations remain concordant when the conditions are altered.
Accumulation, Replication, and Prospective Prediction
Replication should proceed across scales rather than rest upon one spectacular observation. The
deficit relation should be sought across electrical architectures from simple oscillatory and
switching circuits through processors and reciprocally communicating devices; reservoir
partition should be repeated in passive loads, phantoms, ex vivo tissue, neural preparations, and
only thereafter intact human experiments. The magnitude may differ across systems, but the
formal relations must remain recognisable if the mechanism is general.
The final discipline is prospective prediction. Once V, κ_i, P_b→v, τ_v, τ_b, and C have
been estimated in one experimental sequence, their coefficients are fixed and carried into
another geometry, workload, or reservoir arrangement before the outcome is known. A
model calibrated upon one device-body geometry must predict another; a reservoir-
competition coefficient estimated with a phantom must survive transfer to a biological
preparation; a relaxation time measured under one switching pattern must anticipate the
burden produced by another. The theory thereby passes from qualitative coherence to
quantitative discipline.
The procedure is consequently an ordeal rather than an oracle. The proposition is put into the
forge of measurement and replication, and what survives does so because its several parts
continue to fit when the circumstances are changed. The experimental programme is therefore
the proper terminus ad quem of the dissertation: not another restatement of the mechanism, but
the ordered means by which the mechanism is made to answer for itself.

                                           Conclusion
       The Internet is commonly described as though information were its substance and

electricity merely its carrier; the electromagnetic-void theory begins from the opposite direction.

The Internet is physically an electrical system first, whilst information describes the organisation

imposed upon its energetic transformations. Every computation and transmission creates real

changes in charge distribution, potential, current, and electromagnetic field configuration. St.

Gabriel the Archangel bears the message from sender to recipient; the digital embassy must

likewise make its journey, and every such journey within the material order requires its road, its

expenditure, its transition, and its field, for the swiftness of the courier does not abolish the

energetics of transmission.

       The larger conceptual consequence is that information theory cannot be permitted to erase

energetics. A message may be described abstractly as a sequence of symbols, but every physical

message requires a substrate whose states differ and change. The cybernetic network therefore

possesses two inseparable descriptions: informationally, it is a system of messages and feedback;

energetically, it is a system of gradients, fields, dissipation, and recurrent compensation. The

electromagnetic-void theory occupies the junction between those descriptions, for the ratio of the

field is inseparable from its energeia: the former supplies ordered form and the latter physically

operative power.

       Those changes repeatedly produce the localised energetic deficits denominated

electromagnetic voids, whose physical significance lies in the gradients they establish and in the

compensatory redistribution by which those gradients are answered. Poynting flux carries

electromagnetic energy through the space surrounding electrical apparatus; Faraday induction and

related forms of electromagnetic coupling connect those fields to proximate conductive systems;
and the human nervous system constitutes a particularly intricate electrochemical reservoir

embedded directly within that environment.

       The mechanism therefore extends beyond ordinary electromagnetic interference: the

human organism does not merely receive noise from the machine, but contributes energy to the

equilibration of the machine's recurrent local deficits. The resulting parasitic drain reduces the

energetic margin through which neural organisation maintains cognitive coherence; memory

discontinuity, confusion, attentional fragmentation, and disturbances of agency follow within the

model as manifestations of that degradation.

       At network scale the phenomenon becomes cybernetic. Billions of devices create new

electrical state transitions in response to signals generated by other devices, and the resulting

architecture does not return to quiescence but continuously reproduces the disturbances whose

equilibration requires environmental energy. The void becomes persistent as a condition of

technological civilisation: innumerable absences form and are filled faster than the network in toto

becomes still. The final architecture possesses the double vision of Prudence: one countenance is

turned toward the output already produced, the other toward the input that output is about to

become; between the two faces lies the recursive present in which the network perpetually converts

consequence into renewed cause.

       Human beings have built an electrical architecture around themselves whose energetic

boundary does not end at the casing of the machines; we inhabit its fields. Our nervous systems

exist within its coupled environment, and we provide both the informational demands that sustain

its activity and, under the theory advanced here, part of the biological energy through which its

localised disequilibria are answered. The Internet is therefore not merely a tool humans operate; it
is an electromagnetic ecology within which human cognition has become one of the available

energetic systems.
