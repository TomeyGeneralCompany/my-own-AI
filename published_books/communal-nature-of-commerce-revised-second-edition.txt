minds, both those who, like myself, were born under the wings of that great Eagle of Washington,

and those who were born in the old world, oft compose a prologue, in great and exalted language,

       [1] Being is communion. Upon that proposition the present work stands, and from it the

whole subsequent analysis of capitalism proceeds. Created identity is neither bare nor self-

originating, neither complete within the enclosure of self-reference nor subsequently decorated by

relations which it might equally have lacked; every creature exists as a distinct participant within

an active order of reception, communication, dependence, and return whose source is the Triune

God. Communal ontology names this constitution of reality. Hooker’s doctrine of Eternal Law

will disclose the manner of its creaturely participation, while the Spanish Scholastics and the

Austrian tradition will disclose the economic operations of finite persons within property,

exchange, price, capital, and social cooperation; yet none of these economists supplies the first

principle. The ontology precedes the economics, furnishes the measure by which its mechanisms

are interpreted, and governs the conclusions to which the argument descends.

       [2] The essence of communal logic is stated through two terms, A and B. The relation of

A to B is immanent to the identity of A, while the relation of B to A is immanent to the identity of

B. Neither term is manufactured by the other, neither is reducible to the other, and neither possesses

an actuality completed first in isolation and only afterward brought into contact. A is itself in active

relation to B; B is itself in active relation to A. The relation does not arrive after the identities as a

bridge erected between finished shores. It belongs internally to the actuality by which each identity

is singularly itself.

        [3] No identity, whether in heaven or upon earth, is an inert and self-enclosed A equals A,

untouched by the other and complete before activity. Identity is always already actual, and actuality

is directed: it proceeds from, answers, communicates with, receives from, or moves toward

another. The other is therefore neither an accidental addition to identity nor a negation which must

be conquered before the self can become absolute. As Saint Sophrony's language permits us to

say, the person is irreducibly singular and, in that qualified sense, absolute; yet this singularity

exists as a centre of relation, open toward God and the world, and becomes more perfectly itself

through communion rather than through enclosure. Distinction and mutual interiority increase

together. (Lunot, ‘The Trinitarian Nature of Being’; Sophrony, We Shall See Him as He Is)

        [4] Communion is consequently the constitutive relation which establishes self and other

in their singularity. It is not a secondary arrangement negotiated by identities whose essential work

of self-constitution has already been completed. The self does not first become a person and then

choose whether other persons shall matter to what it is. It is conceived, begotten, named, nourished,

addressed, instructed, corrected, loved, and judged within relations that precede reflective consent

and make reflective consent possible. Even the inward word by which the self says ‘I’ has been

received from another. The identity which imagines itself prior to relation is not primordial man

but an abstraction produced by forgetting the gifts which made abstraction possible. (Genesis 2:18-

23; Deuteronomy 6:6-9; 1 Corinthians 4:7)

from the Divine gift which calls, sustains, orders, and perfects him. The truth of man is not a secret

divinity buried beneath social appearances, nor a self-sufficient core waiting to be recovered by

deeper introspection. It is first given from without: in the Divine intention which creates him, in

the image according to which he is formed, in the command and promise which address him, and

        [20] The attempt to find an ultimate ground inside subjectivity accordingly terminates in

the discovery of an absence which no further act of self-reference fills. One notorious terminus of

the German Idealist inheritance is Slavoj Žižek's presentation of the subject through constitutive

negativity, a void whose positive determinations never amount to a self-subsisting core. This is

not an accidental eccentricity at the edge of modern philosophy. It exposes the destination toward

which self-relational logic moves: when the subject strips away every relation, gift, determination,

and received content in search of what belongs to itself alone, it finds nothing, because nothing

alone is what the creature contributes to the fact of its own existence. (Lunot, ‘The Abyss of

Nothingness’; Žižek, Less Than Nothing)

        [21] The Christian confession does not flee this nothingness by inventing a created ground.

It names it truthfully through creation ex nihilo. Considered as its own sufficient cause, creation

has no ground; positively stated, that ‘ground’ is precisely nothing. The creature does not have to

exist. No reason intrinsic to creation enables it to deduce, demand, or explain itself. Yet the nothing

from which it is made is not its destiny, because its actuality proceeds from the will of God. The

creature therefore stands in openness before glory: radically contingent in itself, positively willed

by God, and called to discover in living communion the meaning which it already possesses in

essence as Divine intention and gift.

       [22] Because the reason of the creature lies beyond the creature, the creature possesses a

real purpose and a real end. Telos is possible precisely because identity is ecstatic, directed beyond

its present enclosure toward a perfection it does not manufacture from its own resources.

Humanity's ekstasis toward Divinity is therefore the fulfillment of human nature, not its abolition.

Man becomes most properly human as he participates in the life of God according to a creaturely

mode, for grace does not replace nature with another substance but brings the received capacities

of nature into their appointed actuality.

       [23] Without this movement toward the truly Other, human existence would remain

confined within the monotony of natural recurrence, repeating its finite contents without arrival.

With it, creaturely life becomes pilgrimage, transformation, thanksgiving, and glory. (2 Peter 1:4;

2 Corinthians 3:18; 1 John 3:2)

       [24] This movement is active because gift calls forth reciprocation. The creature's

dependence does not reduce it to an inert effect or a theatrical appearance of Divine causation. God

gives a determinate nature, definite powers, a situated field of action, and an end capable of being

freely pursued or resisted; the creature receives these as its own and answers through worship,

judgment, labour, invention, promise, cultivation, and love. Agency is the historical enactment of

received being. Its freedom consists neither in self-creation nor in indifference to the good, but in

the power to appropriate the gift faithfully and render it fruitful according to the creature's singular

condition. Procession and return form one directed sequence without converting the creature into

a passive conduit.

         [27] Second Axiom: Communion is hierarchical. It is not mere ‘relation’ because relation

is readily conceived as solely horizontal. For the communal ontology, the ultimate ground of all

relations must be the infinite God, who stands above (while being present in) the creation.

Communion involves the condescension of the higher and the reciprocation of the lower, which is

both an eternal divine reality (the Father gives Himself wholly to the Son, who then reciprocates

this total giving) and a created one (God gives Himself to man, and man gives thanksgiving to

God).

         [28] The second axiom gives constitutive relation its source, order, and direction.

Communion cannot be reduced to a horizontal network of terms which mutually presuppose one

another but collectively possess no reason for being, because the whole created network is itself

contingent and incapable of giving what none of its members possesses intrinsically. God is the

first giver who does not receive being from the network He establishes. His transcendence

preserves the gratuity of the gift; His immanence makes created actuality possible at every

moment. Vertical communion is therefore not an external hierarchy imposed upon an otherwise

self-sufficient world. It is the immediate relation by which there is a world, by which each creature

is itself, and by which horizontal communion receives intelligibility. (Romans 11:35-36; 1

Corinthians 8:6; James 1:17)

       [29] The eternal archetype of this hierarchy is the taxis of the Holy Trinity. Calvin attributes

principium of the Godhead, not as a prior being or superior essence, but as the eternal fountainhead

within the one undivided Divine life.

the Son and manifests the perfection of their undivided communion. Order is real, communion is

total, and equality of essence does not flatten the irreducible modes of the persons. (John 5:26;

        [40] The two axioms are one logic. Identity is a unity of self and other because the other is

internally present to the actuality by which the self becomes singularly itself. Communion is

hierarchical because this mutual interiority neither originates nor sustains itself, but is given from

above by the God who creates distinct beings together with the relations through which their lives

become actual. The first axiom defeats the fiction of the isolated monad; the second defeats the

fiction of the self-grounded collective. Society does not manufacture the person, and the person

does not manufacture himself. God gives self, other, and their field of communion in one creative

act, and each becomes actual through a differentiated answer to that gift.

       [41] Communal ontology thus supplies the positive account of agency required for

economic analysis. A person acts as a singular centre whose powers, language, knowledge,

preferences, obligations, and opportunities have been received through Divine and created

relations. Action is truly his because the gift becomes actual according to his particular nature,

judgment, office, and history; action is never his alone because its materials, meanings,

consequences, and ends extend through others. Commerce will intensify this structure until

strangers who never meet become mutually interior to one another's provision, labour, price,

expectation, and risk. Before that economic descent occurs, however, the metaphysical form of

participation must be made explicit.

       [42] Communal ontology has therefore supplied the governing truth: identity is constituted

through communion, communion descends as gift, and creaturely actuality consists in the active

reciprocation of being received from God. What remains is not a second ontology but the

metaphysical articulation of the first. How does the creature possess a determinate, God-given

nature while attaining actuality through participation in Divine being; how is God truly present in

created operation without becoming one being among others; how, finally, do secondary causes

exercise powers which are genuinely their own without acquiring an autonomous fountain of

actuality, order, or end? Richard Hooker’s doctrine of the First and Second Eternal Law supplies

the participatory metaphysic by which these questions are answered. Hooker neither originates

communal ontology nor displaces it; he furnishes the grammar by which its procession into

differentiated creaturely operation can be stated without confusion of Creator and creature. He

renders intelligible the manner in which its constitutive relation to God becomes actual throughout

creation.

       [43] The exposition which follows is governed by W. J. Torrance Kirby’s reconstruction

of Hooker. This is more than the invocation of a respected interpreter after an independent account

has already been supplied, for Kirby identifies the architecture by which Hooker’s metaphysics is

to be read: the hidden first principle, the identity of God and Law, the two aspects of the one

Eternal Law, the procession of manifold creaturely forms within the Second, and their return

toward the Divine source. Hooker’s own words remain the object of interpretation; Kirby supplies

the sequence through which those words disclose a coherent legal ontology. Only after that

sequence has been established will the present work extend its consequences into economic life.

(Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 251-260; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 3-11)

       [44] Kirby begins where Hooker begins, with the movement from the highest and most

concealed cause to the most concrete institution. Hooker compares first principles to the buried

foundation of a house and the concealed root of a tree: most persons enjoy the shelter and fruit of

good laws without seeing the source from which those laws receive life. Yet Hooker descends

from that hidden source into the ecclesiastical and constitutional controversies of his own place

and time. Kirby therefore calls Hooker’s extended application of the highest metaphysical

principle to concrete institutional questions exceptional.

       [45] The method is decisive because the present inquiry undertakes the same descent into

commerce. Commerce does not prove communal ontology, for an effect cannot be required to

furnish the first principle by which the order of its own being is rendered intelligible; rather, created

institutions become intelligible only when situated within the order of being, power, causation,

and end which makes their operation possible. The descent from first principle to economic

institution is therefore not an excursion away from metaphysics but its proper application to one

determinate field of creaturely action. (Hooker, Laws I.1.2-I.1.3; FLE 1:57.6-58.19; Kirby,

‘Reason and Law,’ 251-252; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 4-5.)

        [46] The order is vertical before it is horizontal. Every creature receives being, power,

measure, and end from God; because many distinct creatures participate in the one Divine source,

they enter real communion with one another without ceasing to be distinct. Horizontal communion

is neither an agreement among self-created monads nor a collective substance swallowing its

members. It is the creaturely relation made possible by common vertical dependence. God gives

the person and the order of relation together. The community does not manufacture the person,

and the isolated person does not manufacture himself.

        [47] Hooker’s definition of law supplies the first step in Kirby’s reconstruction: ‘That

which doth assigne unto each thing the kinde, that which doth moderate the force and power, that

which doth appoint the forme and measure of working, the same we tearme a Lawe.’ Law is

anterior to legislation and more comprehensive than command. It names the rule by which an

action is made suitable to its end, the form by which a power becomes a determinate operation

rather than an indefinite capacity. The definition is teleological because kind, power, form,

measure, action, and end are held together. A law does not hover above an already completed

being. It belongs to the actuality by which a being works as the kind of thing it is. (Hooker, Laws

I.2.1; FLE 1:58.26-29; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 252-253; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 5-6)

       [48] Hooker immediately radicalizes the definition: ‘The being of God is a kinde of lawe

to his working,’ and therefore ‘God is a law both to himselfe, and to all other things besides.’ God

receives no rule from above, for then He would not be first; neither is Divine freedom an absence

of reason, constancy, or end. In God, the worker and the law of working are one. Kirby

consequently treats the proposition ‘God is Law’ as Hooker’s foundational claim, the ontological

source from which the generic division of law and the later institutional argument proceed. The

identity is not a metaphor for Divine reliability. It names the unity of Divine being and Divine

activity. (Hooker, Laws I.2.2-I.2.3; FLE 1:59.12-15, 1:60.16-18; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 251-

255; Kirby, ‘Creation and Government,’ 411-415)

       [49] This identity must be read under Hooker’s emphatic confession of Divine simplicity:

‘our God is one, or rather verie Onenesse, and meere unitie, having nothing but it selfe in it selfe.’

The manifold laws that order creation cannot therefore be pieces into which Eternal Law has been

divided, additions made to God, or intermediate substances inserted between God and the world.

Kirby reads Hooker through a Neoplatonic causal logic in which the origin remains simple and

self-identical while producing real and differentiated effects. Every derivative form of law

participates in one undivided source; the abundance of its effects manifests rather than partitions

the perfection of that source. (Hooker, Laws I.2.2; FLE 1:59.14-22; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’

253-256; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 6-7)

       [50] Kirby names the resulting motion procession and return. The many proceed from the

One without exhausting, enlarging, or altering the One; the many are directed in their several

operations toward the good which has its source in the One. Procession is not a temporal event

added after creation, nor an emanation by which creatures become fragments of Deity. It is a causal

grammar: the finite effect receives its being and power from an unparticipated source, remains

distinct as a genuine effect, and tends toward the perfection proper to its received nature. Return

is the corresponding fulfillment of that nature in its source and end. Hooker’s order of exposition,

from general meditation to particular decision, imitates this movement from the One into the many.

(Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 253-256; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 3-9; Kirby, ‘Neoplatonic Logic,’

49-57)

         [51] Within this architecture Hooker distinguishes the First Eternal Law: ‘that order which

God before all ages hath set down with himselfe, for himselfe to do all things by.’ The First names

Eternal Law in the inaccessible self-identity of the Divine lawgiver, God as He is to Himself, the

uncreated reason of Divine working in which author, law, and observer are one. It is not a blueprint

existing beside God, nor a necessity imposed upon His will, nor an object available to neutral

creaturely inspection. It is God’s own self-ordering plenitude, wholly prior to and independent of

creaturely reception. (Hooker, Laws I.2.5-I.2.6; FLE 1:61.28-63.5; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 256-

258; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 9-10)

         [52] The First Eternal Law therefore imposes an apophatic discipline. Hooker declares that

our soundest knowledge is to know that we do not know God as He is in Himself, and that our

safest eloquence concerning Him is silence. Kirby identifies here the boundary between discourse

concerning Eternal Law in its original simplicity and discourse concerning that Law as outwardly

manifested and received. The boundary does not render God unknowable in every respect. It denies

creaturely comprehension of Divine self-identity and refuses to contain the Eternal within a finite

         [53] Providence belongs first to this concealed plenitude as the eternal reason, knowledge,

and counsel according to which creation and every created operation stand before God.

Government is the outward enactment of that order through creation and the direction of creatures

toward their ends. Kirby draws the distinction with particular clarity from Hooker’s notes:

‘Operatio Dei ad extra est duplex: Creatio. Gubernatio. Gubernatio praesupponit creationem.’

Government presupposes a creation to be governed, while both creation and government remain

works of the one God according to the one Eternal Law. Providence is therefore not the aggregate

intelligence of creaturely causes. It is complete in God; created causes participate in its government

without becoming coauthors of the Divine counsel. (Hooker, Laws I.2.6-I.3.1; Kirby, ‘Creation

and Government,’ 411-419)

       [54] Hooker then introduces the Second Eternal Law as ‘that which with himselfe he hath

set downe as expedient to be kept by all his creatures, according to the severall conditions

wherewith he hath indued them.’ Following Kirby, this sentence must control the interpretation.

The Second is not a second substance, a lesser divinity, a created instrument, or a layer of

administration placed between God and creatures. It is the one uncreated Eternal Law intrinsically

organised toward Divine external working and creaturely reception. If the First names God as He

is to Himself, the Second names God as He is to creation: source, lawgiver, sustaining influence,

order, and end. (Hooker, Laws I.3.1; FLE 1:63.6-13; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 257-260; Kirby,

self-identity and Divine externality toward creatures. The distinction is relational

multiplies uncreated laws, and it is not generated by the creaturely standpoint

through which the Second is received. It distinguishes the hidden source as God is

to Himself from the outward organisation of that same source as God is toward

creation. Kirby’s terms are God as law in se and God as law ad extra. The present

work renders the relation as God ad se and God ad creaturam. (Kirby, ‘Reason and

       [61] When Hooker writes that ‘the being of God is a kinde of lawe to his working,’ the

sentence does not compare God to a magistrate subject to a statute external to Himself, nor does it

employ law as a convenient metaphor for regularity. The Law is God’s own self-ordering actuality,

the identity of Wisdom, Will, Goodness, and operation within the Divine simplicity. This is why

the First Eternal Law cannot be a blueprint situated beside God, why it cannot be an abstract

necessity above Him, and why it cannot be an object of creaturely possession or mastery. The

source is personal, living, and inexhaustible. It is not law without lawgiver, order without

goodness, or reason without being. (Hooker, Laws I.2.2; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 253-258) From

that confession the First Eternal Law names the Divine order in its ineffable unity, God as He is to

Himself and for Himself, prior to every creaturely reception and independent of it. The Second

Eternal Law names no subsequent substance, created instrument, or lesser divinity. It is the same

Eternal Law as God is toward the creaturely order which He gives being, nature, power, end, and

intelligibility. The term Second therefore expresses organisation, not temporal succession;

relation, not composition; outward procession, not separation from the source.

       [62] If the First is hidden plenitude, the Second is that plenitude communicatively ordered

toward the many, while remaining wholly the one Divine Wisdom from which the many proceed.

(Hooker, Laws I.2.2-I.3.1; Kirby, ‘Creation and Government,’ 405-423) This is why Hooker’s

distinction cannot be reduced to two observer standpoints. Mortal knowledge certainly encounters

the one Law under conditions of finitude, and our concepts never circumscribe God as He is;

nevertheless, creaturely observation does not generate the Divine externality which it observes.

God’s relation toward creation is not manufactured by the intellect which receives it. The creature

discovers itself already addressed, ordered, sustained, and summoned toward an end.

       [63] The Second Eternal Law is therefore objective and uncreated, even though every

created apprehension of it is partial, mediated, and determined according to the recipient’s several

condition. The ontological distinction makes epistemological reception possible, rather than

arising as an artifact of reception. Kirby’s language of procession and return renders the structure

of Book I coherent. From the ineffable unity of the First proceeds the manifold ordering of the

Second; within the Second, natural law gives rise to celestial and rational operations, revealed law

corrects the disorder introduced by the Fall and directs the creature toward restoration, and human

positive law determines common principles under contingent civil conditions.

       [64] These are not portions cut from a Divine whole. They are dependent forms of reception

and operation whose actuality proceeds from the undivided source. The motion of return is equally

real: rational creatures do not merely undergo order as stones undergo gravity, but knowingly

answer it, conforming or failing to conform their actions to the good, receiving correction, forming

institutions, and rendering created goods toward the ends for which their powers were given.

(Hooker, Laws I.3-I.10; Kirby, ‘The Neoplatonic Logic,’ 54-64) The result is not a hierarchy of

degrees in which one creature possesses more of the Eternal Law as though participation were a

quantity poured into vessels of unequal size.

       [65] Hooker speaks instead of several conditions and several operations. A stone, an angel,

a merchant, a household, and a commonwealth do not stand upon one scalar continuum of

participation. They receive and enact the one Second Eternal Law through different contextures,

because their natures, powers, forms of knowledge, and appointed ends differ. The law remains

one at its source; its operations become many in the recipients. This is precisely why economic

acts belong to the same Law as natural necessity, rational judgment, revealed command, and civil

determination without becoming identical to any of them. The same account establishes secondary

causation with greater force than either occasionalism or autonomy permits.

       [66] Occasionalism makes the creature a mask worn by the only actor; autonomy makes

the creature an independent source which is related to providence only after the fact. Hooker

permits neither fiction. Divine government gives each thing the kind, force, measure, and form

proper to it, and thereby makes its operation genuinely its own. The merchant judges, the worker

labors, the lender estimates, the magistrate commands, and the household disposes because God


has given rational and social creatures powers capable of those acts. Their acts are derivative

without being counterfeit, dependent without being unreal, and providentially ordered without

being mechanically dictated. (Hooker, Laws I.2.1-I.3.4; Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 7-15) Kirby’s

appeal to the lex divinitatis clarifies the causal descent. The first principle communicates goodness

through ordered intermediaries, and each recipient becomes capable of communicating a received

good according to its own office. Hooker appropriates this grammar without turning creation into

a scale of semi-Divine substances. The uncreated source remains simple; derivative laws and

causes remain dependent; their dependence is precisely what allows them to convey powers which

are not self-generated.

       [67] A magistrate truly judges because judgment has been given a human and civil form; a

parent truly nourishes because life and provision have been made creaturely offices; a producer

truly makes because matter, skill, judgment, and cooperation have been received as operative

powers. (Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 5-13; Kirby, ‘Richard Hooker’s Metaphysics of Divine

Participation,’ 292-302) The full motion of participation is procession and return. The creature

proceeds from its cause and bears a finite resemblance to the good received; it returns toward that

cause as its powers attain their appointed act. Return is not spatial travel into God, the cancellation

of created nature, or repayment of an ontological debt. It is fulfillment.

       [68] A rational creature fulfills this return knowingly when judgment and will are

conformed to the good; an institution returns derivatively when its offices permit persons and

goods to reach fitting ends. Kirby’s account of processio and redditus therefore supplies the motion

which communal ontology had stated as condescension and reciprocation. The terminology differs

because the first names the metaphysical order and the second names its communal form, but the

directed structure is one. (Hooker, Laws I.5.1-I.5.3; Kirby, ‘The Neoplatonic Logic,’ 56-62) The

relation of nature and grace prevents this participatory order from becoming an optimistic

cosmology in which every natural development completes itself.

       [69] Hooker’s generic division is inseparable from a Protestant account of the Fall. Natural

and rational law express the created order proceeding from God, yet fallen reason neither perceives

every saving truth nor restores its own disordered will. Revealed Divine Law answers that

incapacity and directs the creature toward return. Human positive law also answers disorder within

civil life by determining general principles under contingent conditions. Kirby therefore describes

Hooker’s legal architecture as an articulation of Neoplatonic procession with Protestant

conceptions of nature and grace, not a departure from the Reformation into an autonomous natural

theology. (Hooker, Laws I.11-I.14; Kirby, ‘The Neoplatonic Logic,’ 49-67) This Reformed

structure matters economically. A market coordinates natural needs, rational calculations, and

lawful claims while remaining incapable of revealing the final good of man or healing avarice.

Commercial feedback corrects a mistaken estimate without correcting the appetite which made

profit ultimate. Civil law restrains fraud without generating charity. Religious instruction judges a

practice that its participants have made economically viable. These limits are not failures of the

Second Eternal Law, for the distinction of contextures belongs to its order.

       [70] They are failures produced when one derivative operation is asked to perform the work

of another or when the creature refuses the corrective law addressed to its condition. Human law

gives the participatory descent an institutional form. Hooker does not imagine that universal reason

mechanically yields one positive rule for every society. General principles require determination

through counsel concerning persons, customs, dangers, offices, and ends. The positive law is

therefore derivative without being deduced in every detail. It receives authority from the rational

and Divine order, while its particular form belongs to historical judgment.

       [71] Property registries, rules of incorporation, bankruptcy priorities, standards of measure,

labour protections, and remedies for breach are intelligible within this grammar. They are

contingent determinations of a field which they did not create and which remains open to

correction. (Hooker, Laws I.10.8-I.10.13) The polity itself provides Hooker’s decisive proof that

metaphysics descends into institutions without embarrassment. The Lawes does not leave Divine

simplicity in an isolated first chapter and then change subjects. Hooker carries the hidden source

of law into arguments about jurisdiction, consent, office, custom, worship, and constitutional

settlement.

Offspring,’ 3-7) A participatory account must also preserve evil as privation and disorder rather

directing them against their proper ends.

within the Second Eternal Law because no created act originates an independent field of being.

Sin does not create an outside. It introduces disharmony into the creaturely reception and

enactment of the Divine ordering, while the privation itself is neither a positive being nor an

ontologically sustained and causally effective even as their exercise becomes morally

nonconformable. The one Law therefore admits differences of truth without admitting different

ultimate sources.

       [74] A natural statement concerning scarcity, an economic judgment concerning

object under a fitting distinction. Yet none occupies a universe closed against the others, because

the objects and agents judged remain creatures within the one government of the Second Eternal

Law. Polycontexturality receives its metaphysical warrant here: distinctions of operation are real,

but the distinction of context does not fragment reality. Thus the Second Eternal Law supplies

communal ontology with the participatory metaphysic it requires.

       [75] Identity is constituted through relation because every creature’s actuality is received

Communion is hierarchical because that field does not generate its own being, measure, or end,

but receives them through Divine condescension and answers through creaturely reciprocation.

Horizontal communion among creatures is possible because their differences are jointly grounded

in one vertical order; their relations become mutually interior without either abolishing singularity

or elevating the social whole into a rival creator. The metaphysical descent into commerce is now

licensed by Hooker’s own architecture, as explained by Kirby, rather than appended to it by

resemblance.

       [104] The thesis is therefore stronger than compatibility and more exacting than

interpretation. It must show that capitalist coordination presupposes differentiated yet relational

agents; that atomistic self-sufficiency is false of the very persons, offices, titles, contracts, and

in which knowledge is finite, jurisdiction bounded, specialization productive, and dependence

upon the distinct powers of others unavoidable. The subsequent economic argument is the

demonstration of those propositions, not a search for analogies by which a prior preference for

capitalism might be retrospectively sanctified.

nevertheless possesses a real jurisdiction in their exercise. Labour, vocation, possessions,

presumptively and within their proper bounds, to the judgment of the person whose office they

       [107] Property is one especially visible boundary of that office. Private title establishes a

judgment of strangers, not because ownership creates an autonomous moral universe, but

because a received office is still a real office. The owner may employ, save, exchange, improve,

received command over goods which themselves remain within the created order. Received

authority is therefore not revocable permission from other men, while private authority is not

self-originating dominion before the Divine source.

       [108] Capitalism, as the term is used here, is not a synonym for avarice, commerce, money,

private possession, or the simple existence of markets, each of which occurs outside a fully

capitalist order. It names a more determinate civilizational arrangement in which private control

of productive property, the accumulation and investment of capital, extensive commodity

exchange, monetary calculation, credit, wage labour, enterprise, profit and loss, and juridically

enforceable contract are joined through prices and institutions across a wide field of strangers.

       [109] Its forms vary by law, custom, technology, scale, and political constitution, but its

characteristic operation converts dispersed knowledge, differentiated labour, material resources,

and expectations about the future into coordinated production and exchange without requiring a

single participant to comprehend the whole.

       [110] Seen through communal ontology, capitalism is communal before it is

individualistic, not because individual action is unreal, but because no economically intelligible

action is self-sufficient. Property depends upon recognition, adjudication, restraint, and inherited

law; contract depends upon language, promise, remedy, and trust; price depends upon the

intersecting judgments of persons who do not control it alone; production depends upon tools,

knowledge, capital, labour, transport, and demand distributed among many offices; profit and loss

communicate consequences generated by relations extending beyond the firm; and consumption

depends upon a division of labour whose participants are unknown to the consumer. The individual

acts, but the action becomes economically effective only within a communal field of reciprocally

conditioning operations.

       [111] Capital discloses the same constitution across time. Accumulated stock becomes

capital when present command is committed to future production under uncertainty, thereby

joining abstention, judgment, labour, credit, heterogeneous goods, and expectation among persons

whose contributions differ in kind and whose returns are neither simultaneous nor guaranteed.

Investment is consequently never exhausted by the solitary relation of an owner to an object. It is

a present disposition ordered toward a future field of cooperation, demand, legal continuity,

technological complementarity, and fidelity to promises, all of which exceed the investor’s private

possession even while the decision to commit capital remains properly his. (Böhm-Bawerk, The

capable of exact expression. The equations which follow continue rather than inaugurate that

formal development, carrying the same grammar from communal ontology and participation into

capital, exchange, information, credit, and institutional order.

efficacy of that decision arises only through a field of complementary acts which cannot be

commanded into existence by ownership alone. Investment consequently extends redditus

are failures of relation rather than mysterious collective punishments. Prices no longer coordinate

because balance sheets force sales; credit contracts because each institution’s prudence becomes

another’s loss of liquidity; unemployment reduces demand and validates further retrenchment;

political guarantees alter incentives while preventing immediate collapse. No participant need

intend the spiral. Responsibility remains because offices entered, designed, concealed, amplified,

or failed to restrain the feedback.

       [208] Communal ontology therefore acknowledges emergent causation without inventing

an emergent subject who alone is guilty. Recovery also depends upon differentiated offices.

Households adjust consumption, firms reorganize production, creditors extend or withdraw terms,

courts allocate failure, governments preserve payment and public capacity, churches and

associations meet needs which neither contract nor administration can immediately answer. No

office possesses the whole remedy. When one contexture claims sovereignty, it arrests one

disturbance while producing another. The order recovers when sufficient variety remains for losses

to be acknowledged, claims to be ranked, viable capacities to be preserved, and persons to form

new combinations.

       [209] Effectual demand must be distinguished from need. Need exceeds effectual demand

whenever a person urgently requires food, shelter, medicine, or transport but possesses too little

purchasing power to call forth the available supply through price. The market then reports no

effective bid, not the absence of a human good. This limit does not make prices false within their

office. It makes their office narrower than providence or moral judgment. Households, charity,

insurance, mutual aid, public provision, and changes in income distribution answer needs which

commercial demand has left economically mute. Unemployment discloses a related failure of

conjunction.

       [210] The worker retains powers and desire to reciprocate; firms retain unmet possibilities;

yet wage, location, skill, timing, expectation, and credit fail to bring them together. The person is

not economically valueless because no current bid has appeared. Unemployment communicates

the need for movement, retraining, wage revision, or capital reallocation, while long duration

consumes skill, confidence, household stability, and the local networks through which new

employment arises. Correction must therefore preserve the agent while the conjunction is being

remade. Worker combination introduces horizontal communion within the hierarchical firm.

       [211] Individual workers who possess limited power of waiting associate to bargain over

wages, safety, scheduling, and discipline. The association corrects a hierarchy which would

otherwise receive each worker’s dependence separately. The association remains subject to the

same demands of justice and internal answerability as every other economic office. Its moral

character is determined by the goods it enables workers to defend and by the ordering of its own

authority. Interest states the price of temporal command, risk, and liquidity. The lender forgoes

present use and accepts uncertainty; the borrower advances a project or consumption before his

own resources permit it.

       [212] A positive rate therefore expresses a real difference between present and future

control without making money naturally fruitful in isolation. Usury arises where superior

command over necessity extracts a return disproportionate to risk, time, and service, or where the

terms preserve a claim which the lender knows the borrower cannot fulfill without destructive

dependence. Liquidity is itself a relational capacity. An asset is liquid when a field of buyers,

institutions, information, settlement, and confidence allows it to be converted without intolerable

loss. The property does not carry liquidity as an intrinsic physical quality. Market makers, banks,

exchanges, legal finality, and expectations produce it together.

       [213] In crisis, the rational flight of each participant toward liquidity destroys liquidity in

common. A higher office then supplies temporary liquidity without pretending that an insolvent

claim has become sound. Pension funds, insurers, and mutual funds make ownership increasingly

mediated. Through these institutions a worker becomes a residual claimant upon corporations he

never chose individually through an institution whose managers vote, monitor, and allocate on

behalf of thousands. This dispersal extends capital ownership beyond the wealthy household, while

it also places several layers between beneficiary and enterprise.

       [214] Fiduciary office becomes the form of communion: the manager receives discretion

and must return it as prudent loyalty to persons whose future subsistence depends upon a portfolio

they cannot personally govern. Securities markets permit an existing claim to move without new

capital entering the firm. The seller gains liquidity, the buyer acquires the future return, and the

price communicates revised expectations to managers, lenders, and other investors. Secondary

exchange supports primary investment because an investor is more willing to commit when exit

remains possible. Yet rapid exit weakens the patient knowledge required for governance.

       [215] Liquidity and stewardship are therefore complementary and conflicting goods whose

institutional balance cannot be reduced to trading volume. Speculation occupies the boundary

between discovery and amplification. A speculator who risks capital against his judgment reveals

information, bears inventory others refuse, and moves prices toward conditions not yet recognized

throughout the market. A speculative order becomes disordered when leverage permits one

conjecture to amplify itself, when profit depends upon inducing the movement predicted, or when

losses are transferred to persons excluded from the gain. The moral distinction does not lie between

acting on the present and acting on the future. All investment anticipates.

       [216] It lies in the relation between knowledge, exposure, consequence, and productive

service. Intellectual property makes ownership govern a reproducible form rather than a scarce

physical object. Patent and copyright reward disclosure, finance invention, and preserve a

temporary field in which costly creation receives return. The same exclusion obstructs cumulative

knowledge, essential medicine, interoperability, or independent creation. Duration and scope

therefore belong to the substance of justice. The office is granted for an end and must expire or

narrow where the condescensive good of disclosure has been replaced by extraction from a field

others are capable of developing.

       [217] International trade extends the communal order across different jurisdictions,

currencies, labour conditions, and political histories. Comparative advantage makes unlike

capacities mutually serviceable and enlarges the goods available to each side, but the movement

of capital and goods also outruns the law and association by which injured persons answer. A lead

firm selects the jurisdiction whose weakness lowers its cost while selling into a jurisdiction whose

security sustains its revenue. The relevant community is then wider than either domestic contract,

and the allocation of responsibility must follow the transnational pattern of control and return.

Trade adjustment again proves that aggregate gain does not distribute itself.

       [218] Consumers receive lower prices and exporting sectors expand while a concentrated

town loses an industry, tax base, skill ecology, and inherited form of life. The national account

remains positive while the local viability vector crosses several minimum thresholds. A defense of

trade worthy of communal ontology must explain mobility, investment, compensation, and the

time required for new conjunctions, not merely repeat that gain exceeds loss after unlike persons

and places have been added together. Regulation is itself exposed to capture, opacity, and limited

variety.

       [219] Dependence upon regulated firms for technical knowledge, future employment, data,

or implementation compromises the regulator; detailed rules freeze incumbent methods and make

entry costly; political officers seek visible protection while hiding dispersed cost. None of this

proves that regulation is futile. It proves that the regulatory office requires its own feedback,

jurisdictional limits, disclosure, appeal, competition, and periodic revision. The judge remains a

creature inside the order judged. Beneficence within the firm must finally be separated from

paternalism. An employer provides education, health, housing, relief, or exceptional security

beyond the wage and thereby exercises genuine condescensive care.

       [220] The gift becomes corrupt where dependence upon it purchases conscience, political

obedience, gratitude on command, or intrusion into household government. Beneficence leaves

the recipient more capable of acting according to his office; paternalism uses provision to enlarge

the provider’s office beyond its proper end. Freedom is preserved not by refusing every gift, but

by refusing to make gift a concealed instrument of ownership over the recipient. Capital is power

because it places present resources under a judgment concerning future action. The owner opens

or closes a line of production, selects a technology, bears a period without return, establishes

conditions of employment, or moves resources between places.

       [221] This power is neither identical with political sovereignty nor reducible to the

consumption which wealth permits. It is directive capacity within a productive hierarchy.

Communal ontology therefore binds the freedom of capital to the condescensive duties of

foresight, truthful accounting, provision, and exposure to consequence. The greater the range of

persons whose action one decision reorganizes, the greater the office exercised through ownership.

Inequality follows from differences in gift, inheritance, judgment, risk, luck, office, demand, and

the cumulative recursion of earlier returns. Difference of result is not itself proof of extraction,

because communal hierarchy does not require an equal distribution of every good.

       [222] The moral question concerns origin, operation, and end. Was the gain acquired

through service or privilege; does the difference enlarge productive and personal capacities or

convert necessity into submission; does the order remain corrigible; and does the holder recognize

that superior command binds him to obligations which no smaller office could bear? Equality

before justice and inequality of office coexist. Immunity from judgment cannot. Mobility answers

local domination by permitting capital, labour, and persons to leave an unfruitful relation. Exit

disciplines the town, employer, supplier, or jurisdiction which had treated dependence as

permanent.

       [223] Yet mobility also becomes the superior party’s power to depart after using capacities

whose formation was local and slow. A plant closes more quickly than a worker acquires a new

trade or a city reconstructs its tax base. The justice of exit therefore depends upon notice, contract,

remediation, and the distribution of transition costs, not upon a demand that every inherited

relation remain forever unchanged. Place is economically constitutive because knowledge, trust,

infrastructure, skill, memory, and natural capacity accumulate locally. A town is not an

interchangeable coordinate upon which capital temporarily lands. It is a history of relations which

makes an enterprise possible before those relations appear in the firm’s accounts.

         [224] Local loyalty becomes protectionism or nostalgia when it imprisons resources in

failed forms; placeless calculation becomes extraction when it consumes a community’s inherited

capital and treats departure as proof that no obligation survived. Prudence must judge both the

good of movement and the good embodied in situated continuity. Philanthropy enters only after

these claims have been distinguished. Philanthropy converts capitalist surplus into hospitals,

schools, churches, fountains, relief, art, research, or direct gifts whose beneficiaries could not have

commanded the expenditure through prices. Such action is a genuine good when freely ordered

toward recipients and fittingly administered.

         [225] It does not alter an unpaid wage into a paid one, cure a fraudulent title, or extinguish

a creditor’s claim. Justice governs the acquisition and operation of wealth; beneficence governs a

higher disposition which presupposes rather than substitutes for justice. The benefactor’s office

also remains finite. Wealth identifies an available capacity to give, but never confers

comprehensive wisdom concerning what another person or community should become. Effective

beneficence therefore receives local knowledge, permits refusal, measures consequence, and

avoids making continued provision depend upon praise of the giver. Anonymous giving is not

always superior, for public example summons imitation and institutional gifts require accountable

names.

         [226] The governing principle is that the recipient’s good, rather than the donor’s

enlargement, determines the form of descent. Intergenerational judgment extends beyond

inheritance. Capital projects preserve forests, infrastructure, research, institutions, and productive

knowledge for persons not yet capable of reciprocating; debt, depletion, contamination, and

unfunded promises also place present consumption under claims upon their future labour. The

unborn do not bid in today’s market, vote in today’s corporation, or inspect today’s covenant.

Their absence from present communication does not make them absent from the act’s actuality.

       [227] Stewardship is the office by which present persons represent that silent relation

without pretending to know every future preference. The common good cannot be reduced to an

aggregate of satisfactions because aggregation conceals the destruction of the persons and

institutions from which the satisfactions arise. A gain large enough for one group numerically

outweighs severe loss imposed upon another, while no actual act of compensation or communion

joins them. Aggregate analysis remains useful for observing scale, tradeoff, and trend. It becomes

morally deceptive when a sum is treated as a subject receiving injury, consent, or benefit.

       [228] The common good is the ordered field in which distinct persons and offices attain

fitting goods together, not a giant preference hovering above them. Gross output, income,

productivity, employment, wealth, and profit therefore disclose real aspects of capitalist

performance while remaining selective observations. Output rises through the repair of preventable

harm; productivity rises while skill and agency decline; wealth rises through an asset valuation

which makes entry impossible for outsiders; employment rises through work incapable of

sustaining the household. Each increase is real, and none establishes that the underlying order is

sound. The answer is not to despise measurement.

       [229] It is to maintain a plurality of measures answerable to the natures and ends of the

capacities observed, and to place every measure under judgments it cannot generate from its own

numerical success. Intermediate associations preserve this plurality between person, firm, and

state. Households, churches, guilds, unions, charities, professions, mutual societies,

neighborhoods, and local governments receive problems under distinctions which neither price

nor centralised administration can completely preserve. They form agents, transmit knowledge,

pool risk, discipline office, and render need visible before it becomes catastrophe. Their own

hierarchies fail, and their localism excludes, whenever correction and reciprocation are suppressed.

       [230] Their necessity lies not in innocence, but in the variety of observation and

reciprocation they make possible within a complex commercial order. The social question posed

by capitalism is therefore not whether self or community shall prevail. No self exists without

constitutive relations, and no created community gives being to the persons from whom it is

composed. The question is which forms of communion will govern property, labour, capital, and

exchange: communion ordered by gift, fitting office, truthful difference, and reciprocation, or

communion deformed into extraction while retaining the outward operations of cooperation.

Capitalism cannot cease to be communal.

       [231] It manifests either ordered communion or deformed communion within the

communal ontology under which every created economy already acts. Capitalism therefore names

neither the market alone nor a single moral character. It is a historically variable order in which

alienable property, wage labour, monetary calculation, firms, accumulated capital, credit, profit,

loss, investment, corporate continuity, and entrepreneurial entry become reciprocally determining

institutions. Its communal nature is not a compliment added after evaluation. It is the ontological

and operational fact which explains how the order exists.

       [232] The evaluative question concerns the form of that communion:

whether its hierarchies communicate goods and receive fitting reciprocation,

whether its signals preserve truthful difference, and whether its accumulation

serves the persons and created capacities from which it proceeds. The expanded

institutional account now closes into one viability system. Economic action alters

several stocks at once, and continuation is conformable only where it does not

purchase one form of capital by destroying the minimum conditions of the others.

       [245] Capitalism must consequently be approached as a nested rather than autonomous

order. Material scarcity, embodied need, rational deliberation, moral formation, law, custom,

family, property, money, credit, firm, and market do not belong to a single undifferentiated

mechanism. They are distinct contextures whose operations intersect, constrain, and interpret one

another. A price communicates relative scarcity without pronouncing a final judgment upon

justice; a contract specifies an enforceable obligation without exhausting fidelity; a firm

coordinates labour without becoming the source of the laborer's personhood; and a state secures

the juridical field without becoming the intelligence from which economic order proceeds. The

first economic task is therefore to distinguish these forms while explaining their communion.

       [246] Human commercial life stands at the intersection of several such contextures.

Material scarcity and biological need belong to the natural conditions of embodied life.

Deliberation, valuation, promise, calculation, and the recognition of another's claim belong to the

Law of Reason. Property, contract, currency, corporate form, weights and measures, and the

adjudication of breach belong to Human Law, though they remain answerable to the rational goods

from which their authority is derived. Revelation judges the ends toward which wealth is ordered

and exposes those corruptions of desire which escape the judgment of natural calculation alone. A

market is therefore not an autonomous order with a metaphysic of its own.

       [247] Its concrete juridical and historical arrangements are contingent, but the need for

relational coordination among finite, distinct, mutually dependent actors is not; market institutions

are nested within several derivative forms of the Second Eternal Law. (Leviticus 19:35-36;

Proverbs 11:1; Romans 13:1-4)

       [248] This nesting prevents a grave confusion. The market is not itself the Second Eternal

Law, and no institution of buying and selling is identified without remainder with God as He is

toward creation. Markets arise historically, vary juridically, decay morally, and suffer deformation

through fraud, violence, or avarice. The uncreated law does not become a commercial code or a

price mechanism. This book extends Hooker's account by arguing that every economic

operation, as the act of a creature sustained and governed by God, already participates in the

Second Eternal Law. Justice, truthful communication, mutual provision, and the proper ends of

created agency determine the operation's conformity to that Law, not whether it participates at all.

The economic application is mine; its participatory grammar is Hooker's as reconstructed by Kirby.

(Hooker, Laws I.3.1; I.8.7; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 258-270; Kirby, ‘Creation and Government,’

418-423)

       [249] Hooker's own distinction between conformity and Divine ordering makes the point

exact: ‘All things therefore, which are as they ought to be, are conformed unto this second law

eternall,’ while even things not conformable to it are ‘in some sort ordered by the first eternall

law.’ Fraud therefore does not become holy because providence contains it, nor does market failure

disprove Divine government. The dishonest merchant fails to participate faithfully in the rational

and human forms of the Second Law, even while his act and its judgment remain encompassed by

the First. This preserves moral responsibility without carving an autonomous province outside

Divine sovereignty. (Hooker, Laws I.3.1; FLE 1:63.23-29; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 257-258;

Kirby, ‘Divine Offspring,’ 10-12)

       [250] Participation, under this account, is not the erasure of the Creator and creature

boundary. It is the real reception of Divine being and order by a creature whose nature remains

distinct, finite, and active. The cause is present in its effects without becoming identical to them;

the participant acts truly because the Second Eternal Law does not compete with secondary

causation but gives secondary causes their powers, relations, and ends. This secures communal

ontology against both atomism and collectivism. The atomist forgets that being and agency are

received; the collectivist assigns to the social whole the constitutive power that belongs to God

alone. (Acts 17:28; 1 Corinthians 3:6-7; Philippians 2:12-13)

       [251] The creature is therefore neither puppet nor demiurge. If secondary causes were

unreal, every market transaction would be only the visible costume of immediate divine action,

and responsibility, learning, contract, and consequence would become theatrical fictions. If

secondary causes were autonomous, the market would possess an independent source of order and

would no longer require providence except as a ceremonial blessing placed above the machinery.

Hooker's architecture rejects both errors. Created agents really perceive, choose, promise, produce,

exchange, and fail; yet the intelligibility, continuity, and causal efficacy of these acts remain

derivative from the uncreated government in which they participate. (Proverbs 16:9; Acts 14:17;

James 4:13-15)

       [252] Providence belongs to the complete and immutable Divine reason; government is

that reason's ordered enactment through the several created causes that operate in time. The

distinction is analytic, not a sundering of the Eternal Law. Kirby connects the two aspects to

creation and government while insisting that derivative forms remain subordinate to one Divine

source. Temporal execution adds no information to God. It makes the providential order

historically effective according to the conditions of finite recipients. Knowledge remains perfect

in the source; operation becomes manifold in creation. (Hooker, Laws I.2.6-I.3.1; Kirby, ‘Creation

and Government,’ 405-412, 416-423; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 256-260)

       [253] Economic participation belongs within that manifold execution. A farmer's judgment

of soil, a carrier's choice of route, a lender's assessment of risk, a customer's expression of

preference, and a merchant's adjustment of inventory are not fragments of a hidden central

calculation. They are local operations performed through distinct powers and limited horizons.

Their coordination serves ends no participant explicitly intended, because the order of secondary

causation is more extensive than the consciousness of any single cause. Providence does not

require each participant to possess the providential view. Indeed, a creature who possessed it would

no longer act under the proper conditions of creaturely finitude. (Ecclesiastes 8:17; 11:5; 1

Corinthians 13:9)

       [254] This point supplies the metaphysical ground for decentralization before any argument

from efficiency is introduced. The several conditions of creatures are not defects awaiting

administrative abolition; they are the differentiated sites through which created order becomes

operative. Knowledge is local because persons are situated. Capacities differ because creation

displays variety. Judgment is corrigible because rational creatures act historically rather than from

eternity. A centralised power which attempts to replace this plurality with a single operative

intelligence does not merely accumulate administrative burdens. It suppresses the very

differentiated field through which the Second Eternal Law is received in rational and human forms.

(Romans 12:3-8; 1 Corinthians 12:17-21)

       [255] Hooker's closing praise of law gathers these claims into one image: ‘her seate is the

bosome of God, her voyce the harmonie of the world.’ Harmony is not sameness. It is ordered

difference whose several operations become intelligible through one source and end. The sentence

names a world in which relation is real because distinct creatures receive and answer one ordering

voice without ceasing to act according to their proper forms. (Hooker, Laws I.16.8; Kirby, ‘Reason

and Law,’ 251-260; Kirby, ‘Creation and Government,’ 405-410)

       [256] Liberty, consequently, is not metaphysical independence. It is the power of a rational

creature to operate according to its nature within the limits imposed by justice, office, and the

equal claims of other rational creatures. Freedom becomes intelligible because it is ordered; an

unbounded will would possess no determinate good by which one choice could be judged better

than another. In commercial society, natural liberty provides the juridical space within which

situated knowledge, differentiated capacity, and responsible experiment become effective. Human

law properly guards that field, corrects violation, and secures the conditions of exchange without

pretending to originate the rational order from which its authority is drawn. (Hooker, Laws I.10.1;

Galatians 5:13; 1 Peter 2:16)

       [257] Voluntary exchange now receives its precise definition from Hooker's participatory

grammar. It is a particular economic operation within the Second Eternal Law, enacted by rational

creatures through real secondary causes. Its moral conformity consists in the coordination of

provision through truthful promise, lawful possession, just measure, and reciprocal consent. This

definition judges capitalism according to the conformity of commercial action with Divine

government. Consent preserves the singularity of the parties, exchange establishes a limited

communion of ends, and delivery and payment enact reciprocation. Where consent is coerced,

measure falsified, property stolen, or need exploited through deception, the exchange remains an

act within Divine government while its rational and moral form is corrupted. (Hooker, Laws I.3.1;

I.8.7; Kirby, ‘Reason and Law,’ 265-270)

       [258] The market's moral preconditions follow from this structure. Trust is necessary

because agents cannot inspect one another's interiors. Justice is necessary because liberty without

a boundary against injury becomes predation. Prudence is necessary because action must be fitted

to uncertain circumstances. Self-command is necessary because appetite, if treated as its own law,

consumes the institutions upon which satisfaction depends. Sympathy is necessary because the

claims of another must become imaginatively present before they can moderate self-preference.

None of these virtues is an ornamental addition to exchange. They are creaturely dispositions by

which rational participation in the Second Eternal Law becomes stable across repeated action.

(Psalm 15:1-5; Proverbs 16:32; Matthew 7:12)

       [259] The relevant movement is therefore not simply preference, choice, and market.

Divine order gives creaturely nature and relation; formation shapes moral judgment; judgment

becomes situated action; communication and feedback join many actions in an emergent

coordination. Formation is resisted, judgment errs, and action violates its proper end; these are


deformations within creaturely participation, never exits from it. Yet market analysis cannot begin

with preference as an unexplained primitive, because preference is already the activity of a formed

participant. (Deuteronomy 6:6-9; Romans 12:2)

       [260] The economic descent now passes into the historical development of market analysis

itself. Long before the nineteenth-century Austrians, the Spanish Scholastics had carried natural-

law reasoning into property, contract, money, scarcity, exchange, valuation, price, banking,

taxation, and the limits of political power over economic life. Rothbard accordingly rejected the

conventional elevation of Adam Smith as the originating father of modern economics and

recovered the late Scholastics as major progenitors of the subjective-value and free-market

tradition later systematized by the Austrian School. Grice-Hutchinson had already demonstrated

the sophistication of the Salamanca monetary and price tradition; Rothbard made the

historiographical consequence explicit. The Austrian genealogy employed here therefore runs

through Salamanca before it reaches Vienna. (Grice-Hutchinson, The School of Salamanca;

Rothbard, Economic Thought Before Adam Smith, chs. 4-5.)

       [261] This genealogy matters because the Spanish Scholastics were not merely moralists

pronouncing upon commerce from outside it. Their casuistry forced them to analyze actual

economic operations: what makes a price just, what ownership permits, what fraud destroys, what

scarcity does to estimation, what an influx of money does to purchasing power, what exchange

across places does to monetary ratios, what competition does to prices, and what a prince may

lawfully take or alter. Questions of justice therefore generated increasingly exact economic

analysis. The market entered scholastic reasoning as an order whose concrete judgments were

made by buyers, sellers, lenders, borrowers, merchants, workers, rulers, and households acting

under different circumstances.

       [262] Vitoria supplies an early juridical ground for this analysis through dominium.

Property is not mere physical possession but an enforceable power of disposition: a person who

possesses title can retain, use, transfer, donate, exchange, or otherwise order the good within the

bounds of law. Economic action consequently begins from differentiated jurisdictions. Exchange

is possible because A and B do not already possess the same thing in common; each controls

something the other lacks, and each possesses sufficient authority over his own good to make

alienation a real act. The legal distinction of persons and possessions therefore precedes the market

relation which subsequently joins them.

       [263] The same structure gives contract its force. Consent is economically consequential

because the parties enter the bargain from distinct jurisdictions and distinct estimations. A transfer

made without authority is theft; a transfer induced by falsifying the object of judgment is fraud; a

transfer compelled by unlawful force is not the same economic act as voluntary exchange. The

Scholastic concern with commutative justice thus establishes the institutional grammar within

which market estimation can operate: title determines what may be offered, truth determines what

is actually presented to judgment, consent determines whether the transfer belongs to the parties,

and performance determines whether the promised relation has been fulfilled.

       [264] Diego de Covarrubias supplies the decisive subjectivist proposition. The physical

constitution of wheat does not change merely because it crosses an ocean, yet its economic

estimation can change radically with place, scarcity, wants, and the judgments of those who seek

it. Economic value is therefore not a quantity hidden inside the commodity and waiting to be

measured. The thing is objectively what it is; its economic significance arises in the relation

between the thing and the wants of persons situated under particular conditions. The same object

can accordingly receive different estimations without contradiction, because the economic relation

itself has changed. (Grice-Hutchinson, The School of Salamanca; Rothbard, Economic Thought

Before Adam Smith, ch. 5.)

        [265] Covarrubias's proposition also separates economic value from physical excellence.

A technically superior object may command little because no urgent want depends upon it, while

an ordinary object may command much where scarcity makes its possession decisive. The market

does not deny the objective properties of either object; it reveals that economic value concerns the

importance attached to command of those properties under concrete circumstances. Subjective

estimation is therefore not arbitrariness. It is situated judgment concerning objective goods by

persons whose wants, alternatives, stocks, expectations, and places differ.

        [266] Luis Saravia de la Calle carries this insight into price. The seller's labour and

expenditure cannot by themselves determine the just market price, because the buyer is not bound

to reimburse a productive history merely because the seller incurred it. Costs can be real,

burdensome, and commercially decisive for the seller while the market price nevertheless falls

below them. Price arises from common estimation amid abundance and scarcity, numbers of

buyers and sellers, usefulness, urgency, monetary conditions, and the concrete circumstances of

trade. The direction of explanation therefore runs from market estimation toward the viability of

costs, not from cost toward a price which buyers must accept. (Grice-Hutchinson, The School of

Salamanca.)

       [267] The consequence is severe for any theory which treats production cost as an

independent source of economic value. A merchant may expend ten units upon a thing for which

buyers will surrender only six. His expenditure explains his loss; it does not create four additional

units of market value. Conversely, a good acquired cheaply may command a high price where

circumstances make it scarce and urgently desired. The market thereby judges productive plans

retrospectively. Costs constrain the entrepreneur, but consumer estimation determines whether

those costs can be recovered through exchange.

       [268] Luis de Molina deepens common estimation by treating price as the outcome of

concrete market circumstances rather than an abstract number legislated in advance. Numbers of

buyers and sellers, scarcity, abundance, usefulness, place, time, and the availability of money enter

the formation of the price because they alter the judgments and bargaining positions of actual

participants. Competition is therefore not a decorative condition surrounding an independently

knowable price. Rival offers and refusals are among the acts through which the market's estimation

becomes manifest.

       [269] Common estimation must not be mistaken for a collective consciousness. No market

mind stands above the traders and first determines what they ought to think. The estimation is

common because many singular judgments become mutually consequential through bids, asks,

purchases, refusals, substitutions, inventories, and competing uses. A socially real price can

therefore arise without a social substance doing the valuing. The market relation is generated by

distinct actors whose judgments remain their own while becoming causally joined through

exchange.

       [270] The Scholastic discussion of the mathematically exact just price exposes the

expectation, local scarcity, bargaining condition, future contingency, and change in monetary

circumstances would have to be simultaneously known and correctly weighted. The exact price in

that exhaustive sense belongs to Divine knowledge; human economic judgment proceeds without

it. The market does not solve finitude by making men omniscient. It coordinates their several

judgments while they remain finite.

knowledge relevant to price is generated and altered by the very actions through which participants

respond to one another. A buyer's willingness changes when another buyer bids; a merchant

redirects stock when a price rises; a producer expands output when expected receipts justify

additional cost; a substitute becomes attractive when the original good grows dear. The economic

field is therefore not a completed table of facts waiting for a sufficiently energetic magistrate to

copy it. Human action continually changes the facts which subsequent human action must

confront.

influx of American precious metals into Spain made the point historically visible: a greater

abundance of money could diminish what each unit commanded in exchange. Monetary value

different purchasing power in different markets, and exchange between places therefore reflects

more than the physical identity of coin. Distance, scarcity, risk, demand for remittance, and local

coin remains materially the same coin while the command over goods associated with it changes

across places and times.

to remain unchanged. The economy answers the intervention through the distributed actions of

       [277] Salamanca consequently supplies more than isolated anticipations of later doctrines.

Its economic analysis already contains a connected field: dominium establishes private

jurisdiction; contract joins distinct jurisdictions through consent; subjective estimation explains

why goods matter differently to different persons; scarcity and common estimation enter price;

monetary abundance alters purchasing power; competition makes rival judgments consequential;

and the finitude of human knowledge limits the possibility of an exact price imposed from above.

Rothbard's recovery of these writers as ancestors of Austrian economics rests upon this cumulative

structure, not upon a resemblance of vocabulary alone.

goods. A thing possesses goods-character when a human need exists, the thing has properties

capable of standing in a causal connection with satisfaction of that need, the person knows that

causal connection, and the person can command the thing sufficiently for it to enter action. The

economic good is therefore defined through a real relation between person, want, causal capacity,

knowledge, and command. (Menger, Principles of Economics, ch. 1.)

       [282] Scarcity transforms goods into economic goods because available quantities become

insufficient for all known uses. Economizing then requires choice among satisfactions. The actor

available means, knowledge of causal relations is partial, and command over goods is limited.

Choice is the ordered response of a finite actor to that condition.

for the satisfaction of a ranked want. (Menger, Principles of Economics, ch. 3.)

significance of the unit. The paradoxes which confound usefulness with market value therefore

disappear: water can be indispensable to life and yet command little where abundant, while a less

necessary good can command more where the available quantity is scarce relative to ranked wants.

        [312] Mises generalizes this architecture into the science of human action. Action is

purposive conduct: a person experiences a condition as less satisfactory than an attainable

alternative, imagines means capable of altering it, and acts in order to substitute the preferred

condition for the less preferred one. The proposition is formal rather than psychological. It does

not require a complete inventory of motives or a transparent interior; it identifies the structure

present whenever conduct is genuinely purposive. (Mises, Human Action, I.)

        [313] Every action therefore contains preference, means, cost, and time. To choose A is to

leave B unrealized; the value of the forgone alternative makes choice costly even where no money

changes hands. Means are scarce because the same unit cannot simultaneously serve incompatible

ends. Action occurs through time because the actor intervenes now for a state expected later.

Uncertainty enters because the future conditions under which the action reaches its result are not

already possessed as knowledge.

        [314] Methodological individualism follows because only individuals act. A nation, class,

corporation, church, or market acts only through persons occupying offices and performing acts

attributable to those organisations under rules. This analytical principle does not create an

ontologically self-sufficient individual. Mises's own system immediately moves from acting

persons into exchange, association, division of labour, money, calculation, entrepreneurship,

capital, and market society. The locus of choice is singular; the field in which choice becomes

effective is relational.

        [315] Mises distinguishes autistic exchange, in which an actor substitutes one condition of

his own affairs for another without interpersonal transfer, from interpersonal exchange, in which

persons reciprocally surrender and receive. The latter is possible because the parties value the

prospective states differently. Each acts for his own preferred end; the acts nevertheless become

complementary because each person's chosen means includes the voluntary action of another.

Cooperation therefore does not require identical purposes. It requires an institutional form in which

unlike purposes can become mutually serviceable. (Mises, Human Action, X.)

       [316] The division of labour is the decisive expansion of this cooperation. Mises treats it

not as a secondary technique attached to an already constituted society but as the fundamental

social phenomenon through which isolated production gives way to concerted action. Differences

in natural conditions, individual capacities, acquired skills, available resources, and the

productivity of combined effort make specialization advantageous. Society in the economic sense

emerges as persons repeatedly discover that differentiated cooperation yields more desirable

results than autarkic action. (Mises, Human Action, VIII.3.)

       [317] The law of association extends the argument beyond the easy case in which each

participant possesses an absolute advantage in a different task. Even where one actor is more

productive in every line, cooperation can remain advantageous because the sacrifice involved in

diverting his superior capacity differs among employments. Comparative superiority therefore

matters more than absolute superiority. The more capable actor concentrates where his advantage

is greatest; the other performs work which releases that capacity for its more productive use.

Unequal powers become grounds of reciprocal gain rather than reasons cooperation must cease.

(Mises, Human Action, VIII.4.)

       [318] The theorem can now be stated more strongly. Let S_i denote the degree of

productive specialization of actor i, P_i the productive capacity made effective through that

specialization, and D_i the actor's dependence upon complementary outputs supplied by

others. Within a developed division of labour, S_i↑ ⇒ P_i↑ and S_i↑ ⇒ D_i↑. Increased

specialization makes the actor more economically distinctive precisely by making the

successful exercise of his office more dependent upon tools, foods, transport, finance,

information, energy, maintenance, and products supplied by persons occupying other offices.

Capitalism increases individual productive distinctiveness by increasing interdependence.

       [319] Social cooperation is therefore not a sentiment superadded to production. It is the

durable substitution of coordinated action for isolated action because each participant can attain

ends through the division of labour which would otherwise remain unattainable or require greater

sacrifice. The farmer depends upon machinery he did not fabricate; the machinist upon food he

did not grow; both upon transport, credit, energy, law, communication, and consumers whose

wants they cannot command. Their dependence does not erase their purposes. It makes the

differentiated pursuit of those purposes productive at a scale autarky cannot reproduce.

       [320] The market society generated by this process has no single productive plan. Each

household, firm, lender, worker, and consumer orders only a portion of the field, yet their actions

become mutually consequential through property, contract, money, and price. The social order


therefore does not precede individual action as a collective actor issuing commands, nor does

individual action precede society as the conduct of a completed economic atom. Acting persons

reproduce the network of cooperation through the very exchanges by which they pursue their

several ends.

       [321] Money enlarges the possible extent of this cooperation by separating sale from

purchase and replacing the necessity of a double coincidence of wants with indirect exchange. A

person may sell to one party, hold the generally accepted medium, and later purchase from another.

The network of possible cooperation expands because each transaction no longer requires the

immediate counterparty to possess the particular good the seller ultimately desires. Money

therefore becomes a generalized medium through which differentiated offices can coordinate

across persons, places, and times.

         [322] Monetary calculation performs a different office from subjective valuation. Value

judgments rank alternatives; money prices record exchange ratios. Because values are ordinal and

personal, they cannot be added into a social sum. Prices, however, can be arithmetically compared.

The entrepreneur can therefore calculate expected receipts against money costs, compare

alternative productive plans, maintain accounts, and discover ex post whether the plan yielded

profit or loss. Economic calculation deals in prices precisely because subjective values themselves

are not cardinal quantities. (Mises, Human Action, XIII; XVI.3.)

         [323] This calculation requires an institutional order. Private ownership of productive

means permits factors to be offered and withheld by distinct owners; exchange establishes money

prices for those factors; a generally used medium makes heterogeneous prices comparable; and

entrepreneurship uses those prices to appraise alternative plans. Remove exchange in productive

factors and the calculator loses the market prices through which heterogeneous machines, land,

labour, materials, and intermediate goods become economically comparable. Calculation is

therefore not a technique which a planner can preserve after abolishing the market relations which

generate its data. (Mises, Economic Calculation in the Socialist Commonwealth; Human Action,

XIII.)

         [324] Mises consequently distinguishes technological knowledge from economic

calculation. Engineering can identify what combinations are physically possible and what

quantities of inputs a technique requires. It cannot by itself determine whether copper should

become wire, roofing, machinery, or some future product, because that judgment requires

comparison among competing uses under scarcity. The technically superior method can be

economically wasteful if it consumes factors whose market prices reveal more urgently valued

alternative employments. Economic rationality therefore requires information generated beyond

the workshop itself.

       [325] The entrepreneur occupies the office which acts upon this uncertainty. He purchases

or commits factors at present prices because he anticipates a future configuration of consumer

demand, factor availability, and selling prices which does not yet exist. His plan is a conjecture

embodied in contracts and productive commitments. Entrepreneurship is therefore inseparable

from error: if the future were already known in the relevant detail, entrepreneurial judgment would

collapse into mechanical execution.

       [326] Profit and loss return the judgment of the market upon these conjectures. Profit

appears when the entrepreneur has acquired factors at a total money cost below the receipts

generated by the product; loss appears when the resulting receipts fail to justify the factors

consumed. These monetary results do not reveal every moral property of the enterprise. They

reveal whether, under the prevailing structure of property and prices, the entrepreneur combined

scarce means in a manner for which consumers were willing to surrender more than the alternative

uses represented by factor costs. (Mises, Human Action, XV.)

       [327] Profit therefore performs an epistemic as well as distributive function. It attracts

attention and capital toward discrepancies between present factor appraisements and anticipated

consumer demand; loss withdraws command over resources from plans which repeatedly fail that

test. The process redistributes the capacity to direct productive means according to demonstrated

success in anticipating market conditions. Entrepreneurial authority is thus recursively disciplined

by consequences which no entrepreneur controls merely by possessing title.

       [328] The consumer's role enters through this same process. Owners and entrepreneurs

possess legal command over productive goods, but the profitability of their plans depends upon

voluntary purchases by consumers. Consumer valuation therefore reaches backward through

money prices into the appraisal of higher-order goods. Menger's causal order of value and Mises's

market process meet here: expected demand for first-order goods governs entrepreneurial bidding

for complementary productive factors, and realised demand later confirms or defeats the conjecture

embodied in those bids.

        [329] Capital consequently cannot be understood as a homogeneous quantity detached

from plans. Machines, buildings, inventories, patents, transport links, tools, and intermediate

goods occupy different places within temporally ordered structures of production. Their economic

significance depends upon complementarity, expected future outputs, available substitutes, and

the entrepreneur's plan. Böhm-Bawerk's temporal production, Hayek's intertemporal coordination,

and Lachmann's heterogeneous capital all develop consequences already latent in Menger's higher-

order goods. Capital is differentiated productive capacity ordered toward anticipated future

satisfactions.

        [330] Credit extends this temporal structure between persons. The lender relinquishes

present command over purchasing power in return for a claim upon future repayment; the borrower

receives present capacity because the lender judges the future claim worth more than the present

alternative use forgone. Interest belongs to this temporal valuation and to the risks and

opportunities surrounding the loan. Credit therefore places one person's present property inside

another person's plan while preserving the juridical distinction between lender, borrower,

principal, and promised return.

        [331] The firm arises within this market society as an organised conjunction of

entrepreneurial judgment, heterogeneous capital, labour, contracts, and internal direction. Its

hierarchy coordinates a bounded productive plan, but the firm remains surrounded by markets for

factors, credit, labour, and output which it does not command. Profit and loss return those external

relations upon internal direction. The firm can order its offices, but it cannot decree the valuation

of its product or abolish the opportunity costs represented by the prices of the factors it employs.

       [332] Hayek adds the epistemic consequence of this division of labour and entrepreneurial

calculation. Economically relevant knowledge exists as knowledge of particular circumstances of

time and place, dispersed among persons whose positions disclose different facts, opportunities,

scarcities, techniques, and changes. No central mind possesses this field in its concrete totality.

The problem of economic order is therefore how knowledge which is never given to anyone in its

entirety can nevertheless become coordinated in action. (Hayek, ‘The Use of Knowledge in

Society,’ 519-530.)

       [333] The price system economizes the communication of that dispersed knowledge. A

change in price can alter the plans of persons who never learn the event which originally produced

it, because the signal makes the changed relative condition consequential for their own

calculations. Price does not transmit the whole causal history of the market. It compresses enough

of the change to induce economizing responses by actors who continue to possess different local

knowledge.

       [334] Kirzner completes the sequence by treating competition as entrepreneurial discovery.

Opportunities for gain exist because plans are not already mutually adjusted and relevant facts

have not already been noticed by every participant. Alertness discovers discrepancies, unmet

wants, mispriced resources, and possible combinations; action tests the conjecture; profit and loss

return information; and later plans are revised. Salamanca supplies the early subjectivist and

juridical terrain; Menger supplies the causal theory of goods, value, production, and exchange;

Mises supplies action, association, social cooperation, calculation, and entrepreneurship; Hayek

supplies dispersed knowledge and price communication; Kirzner supplies discovery. The resulting

economics is Austrian throughout, while the ontology established before it remains untouched.

       [335] The Second Eternal Law is received according to several creaturely conditions, and

Austrian coordination joins persons whose knowledge is radically partial. Neither moral judgment

nor economic action reduces to the transparent execution of explicit preference. The agent acts

from formation, memory, habit, inherited distinctions, local knowledge, and expectations which

exceed conscious inspection. At the scale of an individual, those differences remain analytically

important; at the scale of a large economy, however, most idiosyncratic ethical choices wash out

unless they become sufficiently repeated to form a population-level regularity.

       [336] Culture is therefore treated here as an invariant habit parameter rather than as a

collection of individual moral opinions. Formation, memory, sentiment, habit, and inherited

distinctions generate population-level regularities without making individual judgment

transparent. The market carries histories of communication, expectation, rules, debts, reputations,

and recursive adjustment which no participant surveys. Finite agents coordinate through this

opacity: they communicate, learn, regulate, and correct without becoming transparent either to

themselves or to one another. The parameter does not mean that every person possesses identical

tastes. It records the regularities which persist across enough persons and transactions to alter the

conditions under which purposive economic action and entrepreneurial calculation occur. Gary

Becker’s Accounting for Tastes supplies the mechanism: accumulated habits and experience

function like capital because they alter the shadow price of later actions. An action fitting an

established habit may require less adjustment; an action that violates it may carry additional costs

of attention, uncertainty, social sanction, or foregone satisfaction. (Becker, Accounting for Tastes,

1996.) Stigler and Becker make the population-level point explicit by treating stable preferences

as a condition for explaining observed behaviour through changes in prices, constraints, and

accumulated capital rather than by inventing a new taste function for every observation. Culture

therefore matters economically when a regularity survives aggregation and changes the parameters

faced by many participants. (Stigler and Becker, “De Gustibus Non Est Disputandum,” 1977.)

opportunities, rules, expectations, and discoveries generated partly by the actions of others. The

absence of a single designer does not imply the absence of order; it identifies the manner in which

the order is produced. Hayek’s point is epistemic before it is celebratory: an order formed through

many adjustments can embody more particular knowledge than any directing mind could possess

at once.

       [449] Read through the ontology already established, spontaneous order is an economic

pattern within creaturely secondary causation. It neither replaces Providence nor supplies the

metaphysics of the person. It describes how distinct finite agents, acting from several conditions

and partial knowledge, can generate a coordinated order whose concrete detail exceeds the

intention of each participant. The Austrian account therefore descends from the ontology without

redefining it: the economic process remains real, causal, corrigible, and irreducible to the

consciousness of any single actor.

changes the opportunity confronting incumbents; a new contract changes the feasible plans of

buyer and seller; an unexpected shortage changes the significance of inventories which yesterday

appeared ordinary. The relevant field is thus historically moving while participants act within it.

Coordination must occur before complete knowledge can be assembled, because the attempt to

assemble it takes place inside the same changing order it seeks to describe.

        [456] Producers expand, contract, substitute, imitate, or experiment; consumers revise

priorities; investors compare uncertain futures; entrepreneurs search for discrepancies between

prevailing arrangements and opportunities not yet generally recognized. Coordination therefore

proceeds through discovery, interpretation, and revision rather than through command or

omniscience. (Hayek, “The Use of Knowledge in Society.”)

transmitting their complete causes. Hayek's familiar scarcity example is exact on this point:

when a material becomes relatively scarcer, users do not need a common account of whether the

disturbance arose from lost supply, increased demand, transport interruption, or some conjunction

of all three. The altered price changes the terms upon which innumerable local plans can continue,

and each actor can economize, substitute, expand, contract, or search according to knowledge

which remains his own. The economy therefore coordinates through informational compression.

       [458] The signal becomes economically operative only because it is received by persons

already capable of valuation. Price does not inject desire into an inert subject; it enters a

account of subjective valuation meet without being collapsed into one another: the former

identifies the creaturely capacity to tend beyond present possession toward apprehended good,

while the latter explains how the acting person orders concrete alternatives, selects means, and

revises conduct when altered prices change the sacrifice required for attainment. As established

in paragraphs [88]-[89], real individual action does not require ontological self-sufficiency.

(Mises, Human Action, I.1 and IV.1-2.)

       [459] Money intensifies this communicative economy without becoming a measure of the

complex capitalist production, while Luhmann's account of money as a symbolically generalized

medium clarifies the communicative achievement involved: payment and nonpayment allow

selections to be connected across persons who need not share purposes, biographies, or complete

knowledge. Money communicates economically actionable distinctions; it does not disclose the

moral or ontological worth of what is priced.

require a static centre toward which history mechanically returns. It requires a process in which

       [464] The Austrian development of this subjectivist tradition removes the temptation to

treat competition as a static arrangement and restores it to the temporal order of discovery.

Entrepreneurial profit appears where an actor perceives a discrepancy between present factor

prices and the future value he expects consumers to place upon a possible output; loss appears

where that conjecture fails. Competition is therefore constituted by rival acts of perception,

commitment, imitation, correction, and entry rather than by the mere existence of a predetermined

number of firms.

       [465] Competition acquires its proper meaning only under this ignorance. If the relevant

facts, superior methods, profitable opportunities, and efficient allocations were already known to

all participants, rivalry would add little beyond the costly duplication of a result already possessed.

Hayek's discovery argument reverses the usual order: competition matters because the facts which

will determine successful conduct are not fully available before the process occurs. Rival plans are

therefore experiments conducted with owned resources under conditions in which their

comparative fitness is disclosed only through subsequent response. What is discovered includes

not only new products or technologies, but also that an existing expectation was false, that a cost

formerly tolerated can be avoided, that a consumer preference has shifted, that a complementary

resource is scarcer than supposed, or that another actor has perceived a use which prevailing

arrangements concealed. (Hayek, 'Competition as a Discovery Procedure,' 179-190.)

       [466] Profit and loss consequently possess an epistemic office without becoming infallible

verdicts upon moral worth. Profit records that, under the prevailing configuration of prices, rights,

expectations, and voluntary purchases, a conjecture has obtained revenues exceeding the

monetarily reckoned costs charged against it; loss records the contrary. Neither result tells the

whole truth about the action, yet both return information which the entrepreneur lacked when the

commitment was made. In recursive form, conjectured knowledge K_t issues in action a_t; market

response yields prices and profit or loss; and those outputs alter the knowledge from which the

next action proceeds: K_t -> a_t -> (P_t, pi_t) -> K_(t+1). The economic actor is therefore neither

blind nor omniscient. He acts from partial knowledge and becomes differently informed through

consequences generated by the independently acting persons upon whom his plan depended.

a general theory of price and economic action. Mises and Hayek then developed consequences

which the earlier Scholastics could not have stated in modern Austrian terms: monetary

system. The vocabulary changes and the analytical machinery becomes more exact, but the finite

       [468] Kirzner’s entrepreneur is indispensable at this juncture because alertness concerns

over a world already rendered transparent; he notices, interprets, conjectures, and acts within a

field whose relevant possibilities are disclosed through the process of competition. This is why

entrepreneurial discovery belongs naturally beside the account of opacity developed above rather

than beside a model of omniscient maximization. (Kirzner, Competition and Entrepreneurship,

       [469] Kirzner's account deserves to be carried beyond the bare proposition that

entrepreneurs discover opportunities. Alertness names a mode of attention to discrepancies

among plans, prices, resources, wants, and possible uses which have not yet been brought into

coordination. An opportunity for profit exists because some relevant relation has not yet been

noticed, believed, or acted upon by the persons whose conduct would otherwise have removed it.

Entrepreneurial action is therefore not merely the execution of a known production function. It is

the intervention of judgment into an incompletely disclosed field.

       [470] The discovery is relational in its economic object. A price discrepancy, unmet

want, unused resource, superior contractual form, or untried combination is never an isolated fact


possessing entrepreneurial significance by itself. It becomes an opportunity through a relation

among persons and things: one person values a good differently from another; a resource can

serve an end its present holder has not perceived; a consumer want can be answered by a

productive combination not yet assembled. Alertness apprehends a possible coordination among

elements which remain distinct.

       [472] The danger of specialization is not that division of labour is economically defective,

but that productive excellence in a narrow office does not by itself supply the whole formation

the point economically relevant: market participants depend upon forms of knowledge and

discipline which no individual participant invented and which price alone does not exhaust.

Household formation, education, religious instruction, civic association, and professional

apprenticeship therefore remain sources of human and institutional capacities upon which the

market continually draws.

       [473] Discovery is therefore not knowledge appearing ex nihilo within an economically

isolated consciousness. It is the particularization of received powers within a field the actor did

not create: memory, skill, language, prices, property, institutions, technologies, and the actions of

strangers furnish materials upon which singular judgment operates. The opportunity discovered

is genuinely the entrepreneur’s discovery, and the act which follows is genuinely his act,

although neither could exist in an ontologically self-sufficient agent. Austrian entrepreneurship is

thus an economic instantiation of the secondary causation established in paragraphs [87]-[89]:

novelty can be locally real without requiring the creature to become the fountain of its own

       [474] The sequence is at once Austrian and cybernetic, and neither vocabulary is employed

conjecture enters exchange and price formation; realised profit and loss return information

concerning the compatibility of plans; and that returned information becomes an input into

subsequent action. What appears at one moment as output re-enters the system at the next as

condition, signal, constraint, memory, or opportunity. The market process is therefore recursively

causal rather than merely sequential, and its coordination is achieved through feedback among

actors who remain distinct and partially opaque to one another. (Hayek, ‘The Use of Knowledge

in Society,’ 519-530; Wiener, Cybernetics; Ashby, An Introduction to Cybernetics, chs. 10-11.)

       [475] Market process is recursive because the persons who observe its signals are

themselves altered in knowledge, expectation, resource command, and feasible action by what

they observe.

       [476] This qualification is essential to the present thesis. The market coordinates capacities

which it did not create, and it also weakens capacities upon which its long-term order depends.

Education, household formation, religious instruction, civic participation, and moral habituation

are not inefficient remnants awaiting absorption into price. They are institutions through which the

rational creature acquires the variety necessary for judgment. A society which permits commercial

specialization to hollow them out becomes wealthier in goods while becoming poorer in the human

powers required to preserve justice, liberty, and intelligent self-government.

       [477] The Austrian defense of markets is not a doctrine of merchant infallibility. Hayek’s

knowledge problem applies to private actors as rigorously as it applies to magistrates: each

entrepreneur, banker, producer, and trader acts from a partial position and can mistake his local

interest for the structure of the whole. Competition matters because private plans are corrigible.

Rival entry, changing prices, refusal by consumers, withdrawal of credit, and realised loss expose

errors which no participant is entitled to treat as universal knowledge merely because he possesses

specialized expertise.

       [478] Market success can itself become a source of epistemic corruption when a locally

successful plan is mistaken for an indefinitely valid law of action. Profit records the success of a

plan under the circumstances in which the relevant exchanges occurred; it does not confer

omniscience upon the entrepreneur, convert wealth into moral authority, or guarantee that

yesterday’s combination remains suited to tomorrow’s conditions. The discipline of the market is

therefore recursive: successful actors remain exposed to later loss, new competitors, altered tastes,

technological change, and discoveries which overturn the informational advantage upon which

earlier success depended.

       [479] Austrian political economy consequently opposes the fantasy of automatic innocence

no less than the fantasy of administrative omniscience. Private property, voluntary exchange,

monetary calculation, entrepreneurship, and competition create a powerful order of coordination

because they preserve distributed judgment and make error corrigible through consequences. They

do not abolish fraud, coercion, breach, theft, monopoly privilege, or the bounded offices of civil

justice. The question remains jurisdictional: which office possesses the authority, knowledge,

feedback, and accountability proper to the wrong or coordination problem before it? The

presumption against centralised mastery does not abolish law; it denies law the pretense of

possessing the economic knowledge generated only through the market process.

       [480] The resulting political economy is polycontextural in operation. Household

prudence, commercial exchange, civil justice, education, defense, public infrastructure, ecclesial

formation, and entrepreneurial calculation obey different immediate codes and cannot be reduced

without distortion to a single monetary calculus. Money communicates economically actionable

distinctions, but it cannot determine the worth of worship, friendship, learning, courage, or mercy.

Human law can compel restitution for injury, but it cannot manufacture beneficence by command

without changing the form of the act. Market coordination is powerful precisely because its

jurisdiction is economic rather than total.

       [481] Hayek located this epistemic problem within a contest between two traditions of

liberty. Against the rationalistic Continental tendency to treat social order as the product of a

comprehended design, he defended the evolutionary individualism associated with the British

tradition of Ferguson, Burke, the Old Whigs, and the spontaneous-order strand of eighteenth-

century political economy: institutions can embody knowledge and adjustment which no directing

intelligence first possesses as a complete plan. Liberty is therefore joined to intellectual humility.

Human reason is real and powerful, but it operates within languages, rules, practices, expectations,

and inherited institutions which precede the particular reasoner and exceed his capacity for

simultaneous comprehension. (Hayek, ‘Individualism: True and False’; Hayek, The Constitution

of Liberty, ch. 4.)

        [482] Hayek's later distinction between an organisation and the market order sharpens the

same jurisdictional point. An organisation such as a firm may arrange offices under a

comparatively determinate hierarchy of purposes: managers direct resources toward a productive

plan, employees undertake bounded tasks, accounts test performance against the firm's continuing

operations, and authority may properly be concentrated because the office itself is constituted for

specified ends. The wider market order is different. It coordinates households, firms, lenders,

workers, consumers, associations, and other offices pursuing heterogeneous and sometimes

incompatible particular ends. To treat that order as though it were one vast firm is therefore to

mistake an order among many purposes for an organisation possessing one scale of purposes.

(Hayek, Law, Legislation and Liberty, vol. 2, ch. 10, 'The Market Order or Catallaxy.')

        [483] This distinction also explains why decentralised exchange and centralised direction

can coexist without conceptual embarrassment. The question is not whether authority exists, but

whether a particular office possesses the jurisdiction, knowledge, and feedback necessary for the

decision assigned to it. A production manager may know more than an external buyer about the

sequencing of operations inside a plant; the buyer may know more than that manager about the

urgency of his own use; a lender may know the borrower's payment history while remaining

ignorant of engineering conditions inside the borrower's factory. Market order joins these partial

jurisdictions without requiring their collapse into a universal command. Hayek's catallaxy is

therefore especially important here: coordination among many ends is achieved through rules,

exchange, prices, and revision, not by first reducing those ends to a single administrative objective.

       [484] Austrian economics begins from purposive action: persons employ means they

presently control in pursuit of ends they prefer, while their knowledge of means, opportunities,

other persons, and future consequences remains partial and revisable. Nothing in that economic

starting point requires the acting person to be ontologically self-sufficient.

       [485] On the contrary, the action-space within which choice becomes possible is already

populated by property titles recognized by others, prices generated through prior exchanges,

money accepted by strangers, contracts sustained by juridical institutions, productive knowledge

inherited through social transmission, and opportunities that exist only because other persons

possess different wants, resources, expectations, and information. Mises therefore supplies the

formal priority of action, Hayek the dispersion of knowledge, and Kirzner the entrepreneurial

discovery through which previously unperceived opportunities enter economic consciousness.

       [486] The affinity with the Hookerian account is now visible without making either thinker

say what belongs to the other. Hooker's rational creature receives being, law, powers, language,

history, and office within an order he did not manufacture, yet acts as a genuine secondary cause

through his several condition. Hayek's economic actor likewise judges from knowledge which is

particular to his position and coordinates through signals generated by innumerable other

judgments which he neither authored nor comprehends in total. Hooker establishes why finite

reason can be genuine without being sovereign; Hayek shows what follows economically when

many such finite reasoners must coordinate their plans without a sovereign earthly intelligence

containing the whole. The scholastic and Austrian arguments therefore meet at the same creaturely

fact from opposite directions: real human judgment is possible precisely as finite judgment within

an order greater than the judging mind.

       [487] The implication for the knowledge problem is severe. Central direction does not fail

merely because officials are unintelligent or insufficiently benevolent. The difficulty persists even

under intelligence and good intention because the directing office must act upon knowledge which

is generated, revised, and often possessed only within positions dispersed through the order. To

enlarge the office may enlarge its information; it does not thereby convert situated knowledge into

a single contemporaneous datum or eliminate the delay by which reported conditions become

obsolete. The economic case for distributed judgment is therefore not a cult of smallness. It is a

claim about the relation between decision and the knowledge necessary to correct that decision.

Where knowledge and consequences are distributed, the capacity for revision must likewise remain

distributed unless some office can actually absorb the relevant variety without destroying the local

judgments upon which its own information depends. (Hayek, 'The Use of Knowledge in Society';

Hayek, Law, Legislation and Liberty; Ashby, An Introduction to Cybernetics, ch. 11.)

       [488] Menger, Mises, Hayek, and Kirzner together supply the modern economic anatomy

of the commercial actor. Menger identifies subjective valuation; Mises begins from purposive

action and shows its expansion through exchange, division of labour, social cooperation, monetary

calculation, and entrepreneurship; Hayek discloses the dispersion of economically relevant

knowledge; Kirzner explains the discovery process through which unnoticed opportunities and

errors become actionable. Communal ontology states the deeper constitution of the same acting

person: he is genuinely distinct, genuinely responsible, and genuinely capable of choice, yet the

economic powers through which he acts are relationally constituted rather than possessed by an

antecedently complete atom. Capitalism consequently requires distinct agents, but it does not

function according to the assumptions of ontologically self-sufficient agents, because no such

agents exist.

order goods, command of complementary factors, and anticipated human wants. The machine

remains this machine, the acre this acre, the hour of labour this hour; nevertheless what each is

economically cannot be stated apart from the ordered relations through which it can serve an end.

Distinction is preserved, but isolation destroys the very economic office under examination.

       [491] The person becomes more, not less, economically particular as the network of

relations required for his ordinary life becomes denser. The machinist becomes more completely

a machinist precisely because he need not also be his own farmer, physician, carrier, banker,

builder, and tailor. Misesian social cooperation therefore supplies no picture of persons

dissolving into society. It displays differentiated offices whose increasing specialization enlarges

the productive consequence of each while making autarky progressively less adequate to the life

made possible by their cooperation.

       [492] Salamanca supplies the price-form of the same structure. Common estimation is

real, but it is not the valuation of a super-person standing above buyers and sellers. It arises from

several estimations which remain several. No single valuation is the market price, yet without the

distinct valuations there is no market price. The price is therefore a relational social fact

generated through the interaction of judgments which are neither absorbed into one

consciousness nor economically irrelevant to one another.

       [493] The return to communal ontology is consequently exact. Menger gives relational

economic identity; Mises gives differentiation through interdependence; Lachmann gives

complementarity among heterogeneous productive offices; Hayek gives coordination among

dispersed knowers; Kirzner gives recursively consequential discovery; Luhmann gives action

across interpersonal opacity. None of these propositions establishes the ontology from below.

They show, within economics, the recurrent form already established above: distinct beings act

through relations which neither abolish their distinction nor leave them self-sufficient.

       [494] Austrian market-process economics describes how these finite and mutually opaque

persons coordinate without any participant possessing the whole; communal ontology explains

why their distinction never entails metaphysical isolation. (Menger, Principles of Economics;

Mises, Human Action; Hayek, ‘The Use of Knowledge in Society’; Kirzner, Competition and

Entrepreneurship.)

       [495] The constructive economic consequences drawn in this work from Hooker’s

participatory anthropology are therefore not introductory ornaments to an otherwise independent

economics; they remain operative throughout the economic argument. Praxeological action

specifies real creaturely secondary causation within the economic field; subjective valuation is a

determinate exercise of the ecstatic movement toward apprehended good; property establishes a

bounded office of disposition; entrepreneurship particularizes received powers through

conjecture under uncertainty; exchange carries those powers outward in kenotic relinquishment

toward another; profit, loss, repayment, reputation, and trust constitute forms of redditus through

which consequences return to condition subsequent action; specialization increases productive

distinction even as it enlarges dependence upon complementary offices. The chain established in

paragraphs [88]-[113] therefore reappears, not by analogy but by institutional specification, in

the market process described in paragraphs [367]-[417].

       [496] None of these identifications replaces Austrian explanation with theology. They

state the ontological constitution of the actors and powers whose economic operations Austrian

theory describes.

       [497] Market order is morally conditioned coordination. Situated actions become an

economy through prices, justice, sympathy, self-command, communicative credibility, and the

surrounding institutions of human formation. These elements are not externally stacked. They

condition one another recursively: prices alter action, action alters reputation, reputation alters

trust, trust alters the range of possible exchange, and institutions form the persons who interpret

every signal.

       [498] Providential cooperation is now stated without theological ambiguity. It is not the

claim that God supplies favorable market outcomes to pious actors, nor that profit proves

conformity to divine will. It is the claim that real secondary causes, operating within the

government of the Second Eternal Law, produce goods whose total pattern exceeds the knowledge

and intention of every participant. Human freedom remains real, error remains possible, sin

remains culpable, and institutional correction remains necessary. Providence is neither displaced

by emergence nor confused with it.

       [499] The moral preconditions of commerce are therefore simultaneously epistemic

preconditions. Because persons cannot know one another exhaustively, truthfulness substitutes

reliable testimony for impossible inspection; because contracts cannot enumerate every

contingency, equity and practical judgment govern the lacunae which drafting cannot abolish;

because enforcement cannot observe every breach, character bears part of the regulatory burden;

because prices compress information and omit most moral predicates, institutions and persons

must still name goods which money does not measure. Virtue does not confer omniscience. It

makes action possible where omniscience is unavailable, supplying reliable orientation at points

where surveillance, were it expanded until nothing remained unobserved, would extinguish the

liberty whose coordination the system exists to preserve.

       [500] Hooker and the Austrian account now converge at different levels of the argument.

Hooker explains the uncreated order through which differentiated creaturely operations remain

within Divine government. Salamanca supplies the historical economic passage from scholastic

law into property, valuation, money, contract, and market price; Menger, Mises, Hayek, and

Kirzner explain the modern market process through which finite actors value, cooperate, calculate,

discover, err, and revise. Opacity, feedback, communication, reflexive observation, and requisite

variety disclose how their partial acts generate and correct an order no single observer can possess.

These claims do not compete for explanatory primacy because they answer different levels of the

same question.

while morally serious communities can remain materially poor; prosperity therefore bears no

single moral genealogy. The economic function previously assigned to a putative stock of 'moral

capital' is stated more precisely as trust. In Luhmannian terms, trust reduces complexity by

allowing an actor to proceed under uncertainty without reopening every possible contingency in

another actor's future conduct.

       [502] In economic terms, stronger trust can reduce verification, monitoring, bargaining,

enforcement, and financing costs, thereby enlarging the range of exchanges and delegations that

are feasible among mutually opaque persons. Trust is not virtue itself, and high internal trust does

not prove that the system in which it operates is good; it is a mechanism of expectation stabilization

whose moral quality depends upon the acts and ends it enables.

       [503] Trust is, in this precise sense, the operational presence of the past within the future,

without any attribution of agency to history itself. Fulfilled expectations do not determine what

another person will do tomorrow; they alter the range of tomorrow’s possibilities upon which one

is prepared to rely today. Past performance enters present expectation, present expectation

modifies present action, and present action becomes part of the informational environment within

which later expectations are formed. Trust is therefore recursive rather than static, and its economic

importance lies in permitting coordinated action where complete knowledge is structurally

unavailable. (Luhmann, Trust and Power, Part I, chs. 2-4 and 7.)

       [505] Consequence. Trust formed from past conduct conditions present expectations and

therefore present action: the past becomes operational in the future without supplying complete

knowledge of it. As trust rises, the cost of verification and enforcement can fall; as breach

accumulates, actors must substitute monitoring, collateral, contractual detail, hierarchy, or exit for

the confidence previously supplied by expectation.

       [506] The argument now stands in its proper order. Communal ontology is the ontological

thesis: human persons are distinct creatures whose identities, capacities, and judgments are

constituted within ordered relations. Capitalism requires distinct agents, but it neither operates nor

can operate according to the assumptions of ontologically self-sufficient agents, because no such

agents exist. Capitalist order is constituted by persons and offices whose economic powers are

continuously specified, enabled, constrained, communicated, and transformed through relations

with other persons, offices, and institutions. Individual agency is real; ontological self-sufficiency

is not. The former has repeatedly been mistaken for the latter only because distinction has been

confused with isolation.

       [507] Hooker’s Second Eternal Law identifies their participatory ground, for every creature

receives being and operation from God according to its several condition. Salamanca and Austrian

economics show those distinct persons inhabiting commercial society through property, subjective

estimation, exchange, specialization, monetary calculation, entrepreneurship, and price. Within

those relations, opacity, feedback, conversation, operational closure, reflexive observation, and

requisite variety explain how partial actions become consequential without becoming transparent.

Opacity here is broader than a particular information asymmetry.

       [508] Information asymmetry concerns a determinate difference in what parties know;

economic opacity names the more general condition in which no finite participant possesses the

complete causal, institutional, historical, and interpersonal field that constitutes the action-space

in which he chooses. The agent is therefore not merely ignorant of others. He is unable to render

exhaustively transparent even the relational conditions of his own economic agency. This is a real

condition of finite social being, not an analogy drawn from information theory. This order protects

the argument from two reductions. The market is not the source of communal being, because

persons and moral capacities precede its exchanges.

        [509] The community is not the source of personal being, because every person and relation

depends vertically upon God. Commercial society intensifies horizontal dependence while

concealing it. Hooker’s participatory metaphysic explains why that dependence is real without

being ultimate; Misesian social cooperation and Hayekian dispersed knowledge explain how it

becomes productively coordinated without requiring a comprehensive earthly mind. In economic

life, communal ontology begins from persons whose desires and judgments have already been

formed in households, churches, trades, local associations, law, and inherited practice. Economic

action is therefore never only allocation. It enacts relations, rewards habits, transmits standards,

and alters the institutions that will form later agents.

        [510] A market coordinates capacities it did not create; its recursive incentives can enlarge,

redirect, or corrode the households, habits, skills, expectations, and institutions through which later

agents acquire those capacities, even while material output continues to rise. Its moral evaluation

must include what its recursive operation makes of the persons and communities that sustain it.

        [511] Information asymmetry is thus a species of economic opacity rather than its whole

genus. The former identifies a determinate inequality of information between parties; the latter

names the permanent finitude of a social field composed of reflexive observers whose actions alter

the circumstances subsequently observed. I may fail to know what you know, fail to know what

you will do, fail to know which institutional changes will alter your action, and fail to know the

full consequences of my own intervention once it enters the feedback of the market. Capitalism

does not abolish this opacity. Its characteristic institutions make action possible within it.

        [512] Economic communication reproduces itself, learning occurs through response, and

financial feedback amplifies fragility. The remaining moral question is therefore unavoidable:

what kinds of persons and institutions will inhabit these recursive structures? A system may

preserve payment while consuming trust, increase output while deforming judgment, and adapt

successfully to appetites which ought instead to be disciplined. The relation between wealth and

virtue is not an external appendix to coordination. It concerns the human ends toward which

coordinated powers are directed. The relation between wealth and virtue must nevertheless be

stated with theological severity. Wealth is neither condemned in itself nor permitted to become a

rival deity.

        [513] It is an instrument, entrusted and therefore morally charged, whose significance lies

in its acquisition, use, disposition, and end. Saint Ambrose directs possession toward stewardship,

almsgiving, and the claims of the poor; Saint John Cassian directs judgment inward toward the

avarice which survives poverty, renunciation, or ostentatious generosity. Together they preserve

the two dimensions required by communal ontology: the outward obligation of provision and the

inward ordering of desire. (Ambrose, Exposition of the Holy Gospel According to Saint Luke;

Cassian, Conferences 5 and 24) Ambrose’s strength lies in his refusal to separate title from

obligation.

        [514] Possession remains real, but its moral legitimacy cannot be stated without the

neighbor whose need addresses the possessor and the God whose gift made possession possible.

Surplus becomes an occasion of condescensive provision; where office demands provision,

withholding is a failure even though no civil court compels beneficence. Cassian’s strength lies in

his refusal to identify avarice with the inventory of the hand. The poor man is possessed by what

he lacks, while the wealthy man holds abundance without worshiping it. The decisive field is

desire, memory, fear, and the imagined security by which finite goods are made to bear an infinite

burden.

       [515] These teachings do not replace Austrian political economy or invite an administrative

abolition of markets. They identify the formative question which prices cannot answer: what

manner of person and institution receives the signal, controls the asset, commands the worker,

extends the credit, and disposes of the surplus? Law restrains fraud and injury; accounting

discloses cost; competition disciplines error; profit and loss test entrepreneurial conjecture; but

formation teaches the possessor how powers received within one office are to be exercised under

goods which exceed the price relation itself. Capitalism must therefore be judged not only by the

goods it produces, but by the persons, offices, and appetites its recursive operation forms.

       [516] No figure better illustrates the principles here advanced than Manistee’s own Charles

Forsythe Ruggles: millionaire lumberman, salt magnate, dealer in real estate, and proprietor of

extensive timber holdings in both Manistee County and Amador County, California. The choice

strikes some readers as provincial, for the reflex of modern economic literature is to reach

immediately for names of continental scale, Andrew Carnegie, Henry Ford, men of unquestionable

historical magnitude and philanthropic reach. Yet I contend that Ruggles furnishes a more precise

exemplar, not because he looms larger, nor because of his connection to my own mother city of

Manistee, but because he is legible at the proper scale. What is abstract in Carnegie becomes

concrete in Ruggles; what is systematized in Ford stands here as vocation enacted without

spectacle.

       [517] Born in Maine on March 3, 1846, and reared in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, Charles

Forsythe Ruggles was the son of Daniel D. Ruggles, a timber salesman who would later become

the leading figure of the Temperance movement in Manistee. The elder Ruggles was a man of

public moral zeal; the son, though personally abstinent, was conspicuously uninterested in crusade.

He did not smash saloons, harangue drinkers, or pursue the iconoclasm of reform for its own sake.

Instead, he acted obliquely, prudently, and with an instinct for structure rather than coercion.

       [518] Behind his office on River Street, he installed a well and steam pump and provided

a continuously flowing public drinking fountain. This was prior to the construction of the

municipal waterworks. For a time, it was the only public fountain in Manistee, offering thirsty

loggers a ready alternative to the saloon, not by prohibition, but by provision. In this small but

decisive act, one sees the grammar of ordered participation: moral constraint effected not through

compulsion, but through the reordering of available choices. He did not legislate sobriety; he

supplied an alternative, and thereby allowed prudence to operate through provision rather than

command.

       [519] The roots of this disposition lay partly in memory. Ruggles’ family, by most

accounts, was poor during his youth. He is said to have passed many a Christmas without receiving

a gift of any kind. When wealth later accrued to him, not suddenly, but through long years of

enterprise in lumber, salt, rail, and land, he did not treat that memory as grievance, nor did he

transmute it into sentimentality. He translated it into obligation. Determining that no child in

Manistee County should experience the privation he had known, Ruggles for many years prepared

hundreds of pounds of candy in his own kitchen and, during the Christmas season, distributed

small bags to every child in the city.

       [520] When I think of capitalist titans operating within the ontological and providential

reality outlined throughout this work, it is actions of this kind that come to mind. The evidence

sustains the judgment. No government compelled these acts. No price signal demanded them. They

were neither reputational strategies nor investments in goodwill. They were, quite simply, the

exercise of surplus under a moral anthropology that understood wealth as responsibility before it

understood wealth as power, and that permitted beneficence to remain beneficence precisely

because it was not extracted by force.

       [521] By 1890, the enterprises of Ruggles and Buckley had expanded to a scale among the

most extensive in Manistee, encompassing investments across Michigan and Minnesota, in

addition to sawmills, salt works, and rail infrastructure. In 1920, catastrophe struck: the lumber

mills and salt plant of Buckley and Douglas were destroyed by fire. Ruggles responded not with

retrenchment but with recommitment. He purchased Edward Buckley’s remaining interests and

undertook the construction of a new salt plant. When completed, the Ruggles and Rademaker Salt

Plant reportedly cost three million dollars, a staggering sum for its time, and was regarded as the

largest and most modern facility of its kind in the world. By 1930, it would be sold to Morton Salt,

whose presence in Manistee endures to this day.

       [522] Here, again, the pattern is instructive. Ruggles did not absolutize accumulation, nor

did he despise it. He understood, whether explicitly or intuitively, that, as Saint Ambrose taught,

riches are not condemned in themselves, nor are rich men condemned per se, but are judged

according to their orientation toward the common good, that is, by the ends to which they are

ordered. Wealth, in this view, is morally indeterminate until it is directed; judgment falls not upon

possession, but upon use.

       [523] In the late summer of 1930, local reporter Ewald Pfeiffer conducted an interview

with Ruggles, portions of which were published in the Manistee News Advocate on August 22 of

that year and remain accessible. Pfeiffer's summative description captures the man with a precision

no hagiography could improve: "And so this strange old man, eccentric in his living, a benefactor

to all humanity and especially to the citizens of Manistee, incapable of being understood even by

his friends, in fact oft times misunderstood, died as he had lived, in solitude and away from the

world." This testimony establishes that the ends toward which Ruggles ordered himself were good.

       [524] What is visible in the life of Charles Forsythe Ruggles is not a theory of benevolence

but a grammar of ordered participation lived under conditions Hooker renders metaphysically

intelligible and Austrian economics renders economically legible: property exercised through

entrepreneurial judgment, heterogeneous capital assembled under uncertainty, labour and credit

coordinated through contract, profit realised through successful anticipation, and surplus

subsequently disposed through the owner’s continuing jurisdiction.

       [525] Ruggles does not act as a moral hero standing above the world, nor as an actor

supplied with a complete set of outcomes to optimize, nor as a reformer purifying society by will.

He acts through concrete powers, inherited memory, commercial judgment, and local

responsibility, all of them real secondary causes whose effects extend farther than his knowledge.

In Hooker's terms, his action belongs inescapably to the rational and human contextures of the

Second Eternal Law, as does every creaturely action. Its moral conformity is visible in the ordering

of wealth, liberty, prudence, and beneficence toward provision rather than domination.

       [526] In the economic terms developed above, the same action exceeds strict contractual

obligation without contradicting it: property remains secure, exchange remains voluntary, wages

and debts remain claims of justice, and surplus can therefore become free beneficence precisely

because it is not first owed under the bargain. Providence operates neither around these creaturely

acts nor in competition with them, but through the ordered field in which they possess their powers

and consequences. Ruggles’ industrial life also instantiates the Austrian structure of capital.

Timber, salt, rail, land, machinery, credit, and labour were not a homogeneous pile owned at once

in the same manner. They were heterogeneous capacities held through titles, partnerships,

contracts, corporate continuities, entrepreneurial plans, and future expectations.

       [527] Their productive unity depended upon managers who directed, workers who knew

the local operation, creditors who accepted temporal claims, customers who sustained demand,

and a civic order which recognized the enterprise through catastrophe and reconstruction. The

enterprise was communal in constitution before any benefaction issued from its surplus. The

destruction of the Buckley and Douglas lumber mills and salt plant in 1920 supplies a particularly

exact demonstration. Physical capital was lost; contractual and institutional relations remained

sufficiently coherent for recommitment; Ruggles purchased the remaining interests and undertook

construction of a new plant. The act joined accumulated property to future demand under

uncertainty.

       [528] It required a valuation of capacities not yet producing, credit or liquid reserves

capable of carrying the interval, confidence in labour and transport, and an expectation that the

rebuilt order would answer needs beyond the owner’s sight. Investment here is not an abstraction.

Economically, it is an intertemporal commitment of present capital under uncertainty: current

resources are surrendered or immobilized because expected future revenues are judged sufficient

to compensate for time, risk, financing cost, depreciation, and the possibility of failure.

       [529] Ontologically, that economic mechanism is possible only because present command

is already relationally constituted by claims upon future buyers, workers, creditors, suppliers,

transport, law, and institutions whose future actions the investor neither controls nor completely

knows. The communion is therefore real, but it is the constitution of the economic relation, not a

metaphor substituted for investment theory. The firm’s subsequent continuity likewise exceeded

the biological and managerial continuity of any one participant. The plant, accounts, contracts,

skilled practices, supplier relations, and civic consequences remained capable of operation as

ownership and personnel changed.

        [530] This is precisely the derivative identity named by the corporate equation. The

institution is real because its operations recur and its obligations persist; it is not ultimate because

every recurrence depends upon persons, property, law, material conditions, and the Divine gift of

created actuality. The public fountain and the Christmas distributions then stand in their proper

order. They do not retroactively sanctify every industrial act, nor do they purchase absolution for

wealth. They disclose a disposition toward surplus and office.

        [531] The fountain converted private command over capital into a durable public provision

without coercing the recipient, while the gifts answered remembered deprivation through a

repeated practice whose beneficiaries were not required to justify themselves before receiving it.

In both cases condescension called forth no servile repayment. The asymmetry remained, but the

asymmetry became gift rather than extraction. The distinction between justice and beneficence is

visible throughout the institutional structure already established. Ruggles’ workers, creditors,

partners, and customers possessed claims which benevolence could not replace; wages, contracts,

titles, and remedies belonged to justice. The fountain and gifts exceeded those claims and therefore

retained the freedom proper to beneficence.

        [532] A theory which collapses the two either excuses injustice by pointing to charity or

destroys charity by treating every good as debt. Ordered communion requires both: the floor which

is enforceable and the higher reciprocation which must be given. The case also exposes the limits

of heroic narration. Ruggles could not know the complete causal field of his enterprises or

benefactions. He could not enumerate every household sustained by wages, every business served

by infrastructure, every future user of the plant, or every effect of the wealth and authority

concentrated in his office. His responsibility did not disappear with that opacity.

       [533] It became a responsibility to act through trustworthy institutions, to receive

correction, to preserve adequate variety below him, and to order capital toward goods which did

not require providential knowledge of the whole. The final judgment is therefore neither

hagiographic nor neutral. Ruggles exemplifies capitalist action ordered toward communal

provision because property was held as office, surplus descended as beneficence, investment

sustained differentiated productive powers, and authority remained bounded by justice and

prudence. Where evidence is absent, no universal innocence is inferred; where good is visible, no

suspicion is manufactured merely because capital and hierarchy were present. The action is judged

within the several contextures of the Second Eternal Law, by its source, form, recipient,

reciprocation, and end.

       [534] Being is communion. The proposition with which this work began returns now

neither as ornament nor as a formula imposed upon commerce from without, but as the

intelligible condition disclosed wherever economic life has been followed to its actual

operations. Ruggles possessed because others were excluded from possession; commanded


because offices distinguished authority from obedience; invested because present goods could be

relinquished toward a future dependent upon judgments not his own; rebuilt because labour,

credit, transport, law, memory, and expectation survived the destruction of physical capital; and

gave because what was truly his could pass freely into the use of another. None of these acts

dissolved the person into a collective subject. Each required his distinction, and each became

efficacious only through relations which distinction made possible.

       [535] Capitalism therefore requires distinct agents, but it does not and cannot function

according to the assumptions of ontologically self-sufficient agents, because no such agents exist.

Property, contract, price, credit, specialization, enterprise, investment, profit, and voluntary

exchange presuppose persons whose jurisdictions are bounded, whose knowledge is finite, whose

powers are received, and whose purposes enter an order already inhabited by other purposes. The

market does not cure that finitude by assembling an earthly providence. It coordinates within it:

dispersed judgments meet in exchange; prices communicate without exhausting value; profit and

loss return consequences to conjecture; trust permits action where exhaustive verification is

impossible; law fixes boundaries which consent alone cannot continuously recreate; and

differentiated offices preserve varieties of knowledge and authority which no universal temporal

administrator can possess at once.

       [536] The order defended here is consequently neither the sovereignty of the isolated

individual nor the absorption of the individual into society. It is an order in which liberty is real

because office is bounded, property is private because persons are distinct, exchange is voluntary

because the other cannot be reduced to an extension of the self, and coordination is possible

because neither party must comprehend the whole before acting within it. Its historical garments

may alter; its corporations, instruments, statutes, technologies, and conventions may pass away

and be replaced. Yet so long as economic life belongs to finite creatures who must act through

received powers, partial knowledge, differentiated jurisdictions, and the free cooperation of

persons they cannot absorb or command absolutely, the creaturely body beneath those garments

remains. Distinction does not overcome communion. It is the form in which communion becomes

capable of action. Being is communion.
