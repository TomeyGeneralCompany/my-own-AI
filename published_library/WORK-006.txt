Pneumatic Radio Device
By Isaiz James Tomey
Manistee, MI




I suppose I ought give you context for this idea since, it is highly unusual by contemporary standards,
given how the modern world is so obsessed with cramming electronics and AI into every facet of every
gizmo this side of east texas, so under the guidance of my tutelary angel, I had founded a company in my
early adult years, Tomey General Company, and, while we have made substantial progress at a speed
hitherto unimaginable by Manistee standards, I had been going through a bit of a block. This is not
terribly uncommon, I have been assured, but I had gone to bed in rather a sour mood, having been unable
to think of any new ideas, or, for that matter, make any progress on previous ones, and, as is common
when men take to sleep, I had a dream. I closed my eyes, and when I opened them, I was standing, in a
flowing white robe, as white as fresh snow, and was standing in a garden, full of trees bearing all sorts of
exotic fruits, in colors, which, in spite of my command of the English language, I could not conceive of
the words to describe, and it was in this garden, that my tutelary angel, resplendent in his golden armor,
which was carved with the most intricate imagery of the cosmos, the urth, sky, sea, sun, moon, and
constellations like Orion and the one which I was born under, that being Scorpio, appeared before me at
the command of the Lord. He carried a scroll in his hand, placed it before me, and commanded me to eat
it. I took the scroll and ate it. When I had done so, the knowledge of this radio, this gadget, was placed
within me. So the purpose of this document, if nothing else, is to put to paper those things which, by the
consumption of the scroll, exist within my mind. The receiver uses a 24-inch tuned copper loop and
electrostatic leaf to control five pneumatic relay stages and a slotted air valve feeding a desk horn. For an
assumed 30 mV/m carrier field and 50% single-tone modulation, the nominal model predicts 50.68 dB
SPL at 1 kHz, 19.69 inches directly in front of the horn. Gold pressure diaphragms and copper flexures
supply the low mass and compliance targets; the waterwheel and compressor supply approximately 10.82
W of shaft input.

The complete project

Idea
Build a receiver that a skilled engineer in Hero of Alexandria’s first century AD workshop could fabricate
when supplied with the design knowledge. Receive WTCM 580 AM at a desk in Manistee. Use the
waterwheel and compressor to supply listening energy while the radio driven leaf controls its release.
Retain the original acoustic apparatus alongside the radio path.

Claims
    Period metals, minerals, metalworking and pneumatic methods supply the component materials and
        construction processes.
    The copper loop intercepts the broadcast field, and square law electrostatic attraction recovers its
       audio envelope.
    Compressed air supplies sound energy through a mechanically controlled pilot and relay.

    The connected five-stage model predicts quiet desk output at the stated field, with frequency
       coloration and explicitly calculated sensitivity to mass, damping and horn loss.

Proof
Construction boundary: materials and fabrication methods available to Hero of Alexandria in the first
century AD. Classical and Hellenistic evidence is relevant within that boundary. Newly supplied design
knowledge is allowed; modern precision manufacture, synthetic films, electronic amplification and
manufactured semiconductor devices are excluded. Dates use BC and AD. Workshop dimensions are
given in inches and feet alongside SI equations.
The wheel drives the compressor, the compressor charges the reservoir, and the receiver uses that air to
produce sound under radio control. The rotating slotted disc is unnecessary for AM reception. It can
remain in the original acoustic transmitter on a separate drive branch. Both systems can occupy the same
installation without treating the acoustic carrier as a radio carrier.

The radio leaf controls an opposed pilot nozzle pair. Five gold-diaphragm relay cartridges pass its

Mechanical gain comes from flowing compressed air. The radio pickup and square law force detector use
the received field; the wheel supplies listening energy.

Material and workshop evidence
Theophrastus describes attractive stones, rock crystal, metallic minerals, abrasives, lathes and
metallurgical processing in On Stones. The work is dated around 315 BC by the consulted edition, before
Hero’s first century AD workshop boundary. It supplies evidence for the material and fabrication
traditions available before Hero. [H2]

A Metropolitan Museum gold earring dated to the middle of the fourth century BC establishes classical
Greek gold working. The proposed coil adapts those metalworking skills: form wire by hammering and
rolling metal strips or rods, anneal it, join lengths mechanically, and separate turns on a dry former. A
modern precision drawplate is not assumed. [H3]

Pneumatic construction evidence
Hero’s Pneumatics describes mechanically compressed air in a metal vessel, polished hinged bronze
pump valves, water driven trumpet sound and lathe bored bronze piston cylinders. These are direct
precedents for the pressure vessel, pump and acoustic portions. The numbering used here follows
Greenwood’s translation edited by Woodcroft in 1851: propositions 9, 10, 16 and 27. [P1]
Parsons’s US816180A of 1906 describes a spring supported grating controlling compressed air into a
trumpet. The Science Museum preserves an auxetophone reproducer associated with a 1903 patent. These
supply component precedents for mechanical control of air powered sound. The final synthesis equations
evaluate actuation by the radio driven leaf. Aluminium, rubber seats and modern screw adjusters
mentioned in later apparatus are not imported into this design. [P2–P3]

Fabrication proceeds by hammering, annealing, lapping, boring and mechanical adjustment. No deposited
films, etched silicon channels, polymer diaphragms, enamel wire, ball bearings or precision micrometers
are assumed. Leather can serve in compressor seals, but an ordinary leather flap is not credited with the
detector’s 10 mg effective mass. The tiny valve is the difficult craft component; the wheel can remain
modest in size.

The received radio field

Idea
Treat the carrier field at the intended desk as the input to every receiving calculation.

Claims
    WTCM operates at 580 kHz.
    Received force scales as the square of carrier field when geometry and tuning remain fixed.
    The calculations cover several field strengths so a local measurement can select the applicable
       operating case.

Proof

Station field and propagation calculation
The FCC public inspection listing identifies WTCM, Traverse City, facility 70524, at 580 kHz. Its
broadcaster confirms the News Talk 580 service and regional coverage. Neither page supplies a calibrated
field at the intended receiver. [S1, S2]
A 50 kW daytime transmitter is used as a working scenario, matching commonly reported station data; the
current engineering authorization and directional pattern were not independently retrieved here. A 1.1 kW
nighttime scenario is likewise illustrative. The numerical receiver comparison is parameterized by
measured E and does not depend on accepting either power value as a verified license datum.
E_rms ≈ sqrt(30 P_rad G(θ)) / r
This follows by combining power flux S = P_rad G/(4πr²) with S = E_rms²/Z0 and Z0 ≈ 376.73 Ω. It is a
far field free space reference, not a broadcast groundwave prediction. For P_rad = 50,000 W, G = 1, and r
= 70,000 m, E ≈ 17.5 mV/m. Using G = 3 gives 30.3 mV/m before propagation loss. Here G is power
gain in the receiver direction, and P_rad is radiated power, not automatically transmitter input power.
E_ground ≈ E_ref × A_ground(f, σ, εr, r) × sqrt(η_ant)
A_ground represents the propagation correction, and η_ant represents antenna radiation efficiency if the
starting power is transmitter input. They cannot be assigned from distance alone. FCC 47 CFR 73.184
explicitly makes the groundwave field depend on conductivity, permittivity, antenna geometry and inverse
distance field. Its standard curves have a 100 mV/m at 1 km normalization that must be scaled. [S3]

The 70 km path is a regional scale example relevant to the Manistee area, not a surveyed address to
transmitter distance. Land conductivity, Lake Michigan paths, directional nulls, local noise and buildings
can change E substantially. Skywave cannot be treated as an improvement without checking cochannel
interference. At unchanged pattern and propagation, reducing power from 50 to 1.1 kW reduces E by
0.148 and electrostatic audio force by 0.022, about 33.2 dB.

Flux linkage and inductance
Use f = 580 kHz, ω = 2πf and a uniform magnetic field normal to the loop. The incoming electric field E
is used as a convenient equivalent through B = E/c. That relation is a propagation approximation; a local
field measurement should establish the magnetic component directly. The maximum pickup orientation is
assumed. [T3]

V_s,rms = ω B_rms Σ A_i ≈ (ω E_rms / c) Σ A_i
The ribbon is laid as a square spiral with outer side D = 0.6096 m, N = 40, ribbon width w = 0.001 m, gap
s = 0.001 m and thickness t = 0.0003 m. The centerline side of turn i is D − w − 2i(w+s), for i = 0 to 39.
Sum its square for the linked area and four times its side for conductor length.
Σ A_i = 11.3467 m²; ℓ = 84.896 m = 278.53 ft
V_s,rms = 1.3793 mV (E_rms = 10 mV/m)
An approximate square spiral current sheet formula gives the inductance. Define inner side Di = D −
2[Nw+(N−1)s], average side Dav = (D+Di)/2, and fill ratio u = (D−Di)/(D+Di). This geometrical estimate
sets the starting winding; measure self resonance after assembly. [T4]
L ≈ (μ0 N² Dav × 1.27 / 2)[ln(2.07/u) + 0.18u + 0.13u²]
L ≈ 1.8032 mH; C_total = 1/(ω²L) = 41.758 pF

Conductor and joint losses
Copper purity affects resistance but not the linked flux of the same loop. Conductor shape, joins,
proximity loss and support leakage must be accounted for together. The numerical material reference is
copper resistivity 1.724 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m. This is a favorable material value, not a measured ancient copper
specimen. Modern oxygen free copper is not assumed as a period manufacturing process. [T5]
δ = sqrt[2ρ/(ωμ0)] = 86.77 μm; R_surface = ρ/δ = 0.1987 mΩ
R_strip ≈ R_surface ℓ/(2w) = 8.43 Ω
The strip estimate assumes its two broad faces carry current and ignores edges. Apply a factor of two for
proximity and nonuniform current, then add 2 Ω for joins: Rcoil ≈ 18.87 Ω. This factor is a sensitivity
allowance, not a verified field solution. The approximate coil only Q would be 348; the chosen loaded Q
is lower at 200.
R_total,target = ωL/200 = 32.86 Ω
R_other,allowed ≈ 32.86 − 18.87 = 13.99 Ω
The remaining resistance budget must cover support loss, capacitor loss, contacts and other dissipation
expressed as an equivalent series resistance. Larger copper resistivity or proximity losses consume this
allowance. A specimen reaching Q = 100 rather than 200 is evaluated below, rather than silently assuming
ideal conductor performance.

Proof

Resonance voltage and speech bandwidth
At resonance, an ideal series loop source feeding its parallel tuning capacitance has capacitor voltage
approximately QVs, where Q = ωL/Rtotal. The detector capacitance and winding stray capacitance are
included in Ctotal. The winding and detector capacitances belong in this total.
Q = 200; V_RF,rms ≈ Q V_s,rms = 0.27586 V
Δf_RF ≈ f/Q = 2900 Hz; H(f_a) ≈ [1 + (2Q f_a/f)²]^(-1/2)
For a single audio tone at 1 kHz, H = 0.8232. At 300 Hz it is approximately 0.979, and at 1.5 kHz
approximately 0.695. These are first order sideband transfer magnitudes near resonance. The chosen
tuning accepts a restricted speech band rather than the original 3 kHz audio target.

Detector and stray capacitance budget
C_detector ≈ ε0 A_e/g = 17.708 pF

require fewer turns or a changed geometry.

The electrostatic leaf and vented electrode

Idea
Hold a conductive flexible leaf above narrow fixed electrode strips. Attraction follows voltage squared, so
its mean force contains the audio envelope. Rear slots let displaced air escape.

Claims
    The leaf follows audio rather than individual 580 kHz carrier cycles.
    With active area 0.5 cm² and a 25 μm gap, the 1 kHz peak force is 22.19 nN at 10 mV/m and 199.68
       nN at 30 mV/m.
    The vented strip estimate gives 0.01448 N s/m air damping; the model uses 0.01948 N s/m total.
    The detector moving mass includes the attached pilot flapper and participating supports.

Proof

Envelope force derivation
The strict non electronic detector uses a conductive flexible membrane held above a fixed electrode. The
voltage alternates at 580 kHz, but attraction acts in the same direction for both polarities. Mechanical
inertia suppresses the twice carrier motion while allowing the audio variation in mean attraction. The
membrane therefore need not track the radio cycles. The field energy force law is standard electrostatic
actuation; the component arrangement applies that law to period materials. [T1]
C_d(x) = ε0 A/(g − x); F(t) = ½ V(t)² dC_d/dx
V(t) = sqrt(2) V_C,rms [1 + m cos(Ωt)] cos(ωc t)
F_bar(t) ≈ ε0 A V_C,rms² [1 + m cos(Ωt)]²/(2g²)
Here x is membrane motion toward the plate, g the resting air gap, A the electrically active area, m the
modulation depth of a single tone, Ω the tone angular frequency and V_C,rms the unmodulated carrier
voltage. This expression assumes x much less than g and a stiff enough electrical drive over the small
motion.
F_DC = ε0 A V_C,rms²(1 + m²/2)/(2g²)
F_1,peak = ε0 A m V_C,rms²/g²; F_2,peak = ε0 A m² V_C,rms²/(4g²)
At m = 0.5, the second harmonic force is 12.5% of the fundamental, approximately −18.1 dB before
mechanical filtering. This square law detector recovers speech with intrinsic distortion. It is not an ideal
linear envelope follower. For speech u(t), replace m cos Ωt by u(t); the output includes 2u + u², with
intermodulation among speech components.
Increasing voltage by two multiplies force by four. Reducing the gap by two also multiplies force by four
at constant voltage, but doubles Cd and changes tuning. Increasing area increases capacitance and loading
as well as force. The complete circuit must be retuned after any such change.

Vented leaf force and damping calculation

mechanical response and attached flapper mass. A sealed ear cavity belongs only to the direct listening

The acoustic area is Ad = 1 cm², while the strips occupy Ae = 0.5 cm². These are equivalent piston areas
in a lumped model. An actual flexible membrane has a nonuniform mode shape, so its measured force
overlap, moving mass and acoustic area must replace these assumptions together.
V(t) = sqrt(2) V_RF,rms[1 + m cos(Ωt)] cos(ωt)
F = (1/2)V² dC/dx; F_1,peak = ε0 A_e m V_RF,rms² H(f_a)/g²
F_1,peak = 22.19 nN (m = 0.5; f_a = 1000 Hz; E = 10 mV/m)
A solid nearby plate would strongly damp a moving leaf. For long strips of width a, with both long edges
vented to approximately ambient rear pressure, integrating the small displacement Reynolds equation
gives bstrip ≈ μ Ae a²/g³. Use a = 0.5 mm, g = 25 μm and air viscosity 1.81 × 10⁻⁵ Pa s. This neglects slot
outlet restriction, border corrections and compressibility. [T6]
b_strip ≈ μ A_e a²/g³ = 0.01448 N s/m
b_total = b_strip + b_other = 0.01448 + 0.005 = 0.01948 N s/m
The additional damping 0.005 N s/m is assigned. It is not a property established for gold leaf or animal
membrane. Narrow vented strips make the air loss smaller in this model, but they halve the electrode area.
Both changes are included in the force and damping calculations; ventilation is not credited as a free gain.
No maintained static charge is needed. A stable conductive connection to each electrode is required. The
square law also produces distortion: at m = 0.5, the second harmonic force is one eighth the fundamental
before frequency response, or −18.1 dB. Restricted bandwidth and distortion are accepted design
compromises, not eliminated effects.

Period foil and gap setting
The proposed copper winding uses hammering, annealing and forming ribbon on spaced supports. The
fixed electrode uses separately formed metal strips, leaving open slots, rather than a modern etched grid.
The diaphragm uses beaten conductive foil with an edge support, or a conductive leaf applied to a thin
animal membrane. Greek gold foil is documented in an object dated about 350–300 BC. Use that foil
work in the proposed diaphragm fabrication; measure the completed membrane’s mass, tension and
surface accuracy. [H7]
A one square centimetre gold sheet 5 μm thick would weigh about 9.65 mg before support. This gives the
sheet mass before the support is included. For any membrane, total mass, modal mass and effective
acoustic area are distinct quantities. Support preparation and tension must be specified from an actual
specimen.
Set the electrode position with a shallow wedge and inspect for contact while approaching the nominal

precision spacer is excluded. Measure gap stability over the working leaf during vibration and humidity
changes. A 0.002 in trial gap has a calculated operating case and avoids making success depend
exclusively on the smallest clearance.

The reservoir and pressure regulation

Idea
Store compressed air upstream of the receiver. Feed the relay at the main pressure and reduce the pilot
supply with a diaphragm or weight loaded regulator.

Claims
    The nominal relay supply is 10 kPa and the pilot supply is 1 kPa.
    Large upstream storage smooths pump delivery; small downstream pilot volumes preserve audio
        response.
    A 5 litre reservoir has approximately 3.21 × 10⁻⁸ m³/Pa compliance at the stated pressure.

Proof
All pressures in the nozzle equations are gauge pressures. Compressibility uses absolute pressure,
approximated by ambient pressure in the small signal model. The pilot supply is 1,000 Pa or 0.145 psi; its
two chambers rest near 500 Pa. The nominal larger relay uses 10,000 Pa or 1.45 psi, with chambers near
5,000 Pa. Higher supply pressure is not credited as automatic gain; the pressure force, airflow and
stability all change.
A water head reference of 4.01 in corresponds to 1 kPa. A weight loaded bell or a diaphragm regulator
and water manometer set the pressure and indicate its value. Use a large reservoir and quiet connecting
passages upstream; keep pilot chambers and downstream passages tiny. These volumes serve opposite
purposes.

A 5 litre reservoir at about 111 kPa absolute has compliance 3.21 × 10⁻⁸ m³/Pa. A trial full-flow pump
ripple of 0.27053 litre/s at 2 Hz gives approximately 670 Pa ripple when outlet conductance is neglected.

The opposed nozzle pilot valve

Idea
Attach a light flapper to the detector leaf between two opposed nozzles. Leaf motion changes their escape
gaps, creating differential pressure while equal steady forces are mechanically balanced.

Claims
    Each pilot nozzle has a 0.020 in bore, 0.001 in gap and nominal 0.6802 cm³/s flow.
    Each pilot chamber, including its passages, uses a nominal volume of 1 mm³ or 0.001 cm³.
    Pilot pressure is evaluated with the first gold diaphragm attached in the complete cascade; the
        standalone calculation below isolates its unloaded response.
    Nozzle reaction and relay loading enter the final coupled solution.

Proof

Gap flow and pressure continuity
Aexit ≈ π d h ; An = π d² / 4
q = Cd π d h √(2P / ρair)
Kq = ∂q/∂x ≈ q/h ; G ≈ q/P
The simple flow calculation uses Cd = 0.6, air density 1.2 kg/m³ and chamber pressure P = half the supply
pressure. Each inlet restriction is selected to pass the same steady flow as its nozzle. With equal pressure
drops, the sum of inlet and outlet pressure conductances is G = q/P. These are independently derived low
pressure approximations, not measured coefficients of an ancient nozzle.
Cp = Vp / (γ Patm) ; Dp(s) = G + sCp
The 1 mm³ pressure budget includes the cavity, all passages and the pressure face of the next diaphragm.
A 0.020 in bore passage 0.197 in long already occupies approximately 1 mm³. Place the pilot and relays
immediately beside one another, with short drilled or formed ports.

Calculation reproducibility
The exploratory search used five relay stages, seed 128, differential evolution with 180 iterations,
population multiplier 9 and no polishing. Relay effective areas ranged from 10⁻⁵·⁵ to 10⁻³·⁵ m²; nozzle
bores from 10⁻³·⁶ to 10⁻²·⁵ m; free mechanical frequencies from 10²·³ to 10³·⁶ Hz; damping ratios from
10⁻⁰·⁵ to 10⁰·⁶. The sound-valve area, frequency and damping used the corresponding ranges. The
objective ranked the minimum at six tones with a response-spread penalty, required pressure-cascade
poles below −50 s⁻¹, bounded peak travel to 25% of the gap and shaft power to 30 W. The chosen case
was then checked on a denser sweep and its chamber volumes corrected for the gold diaphragm physical
area. The search establishes this operating case within its stated model and bounds.
